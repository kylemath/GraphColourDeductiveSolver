\begin{abstract}This paper, its proofs, its Lean formalisation and its computations were produced by AI agents (Claude models) under the direction of the author; Section~\ref{sec:disclosure} describes who did what and how it was checked. % Abstract after its first sentence: SquireTeamSevern; states only what the ledger supports.
We summarise one route to the Four Colour Theorem, the vacancy induction, and what is established about it. Delete a degree-5 vertex, add a legal fan of two chords, colour the smaller triangulation by induction, and complete the colouring by singleton slides and Kempe swaps. The theorem reduces to one hypothesis, VH$\exists$: some degree-5 vertex and fan work for every inductive colouring. We give this reduction as a hand proof. The vertex property it needs, that every colouring of the deletion of a degree-5 vertex reaches such a completion by Kempe swaps alone, is, as far as we can tell, Tilley's D-resolvability, and whether every degree-5 vertex is D-resolvable is his published open problem. Our contributions are: a hand reduction from Lemma R$^\ast$ (one D-resolvable degree-5 vertex in every 4-connected triangulation of a relative class) to VH$\exists$ and the Four Colour Theorem, with a Lean form of the reduction; a compiled and audited Lean reduction of the Four Colour Theorem for combinatorial spherical maps to R$^\ast$ on a smaller \emph{frame class}, from which separating triangles and occurrences of the Birkhoff diamond and of configuration 2.122 of Robertson, Sanders, Seymour and Thomas are excluded; bounds on the Kempe radius at holes whose neighbours have degree 5 (at most 3), or all but one of them (at most 6), and Euler counting lemmas, all compiled in Lean~4 and audited; exact counting identities for the Kempe classes at a degree-5 hole, built in Lean; and an analysis of the cases these do not cover. Every hole covered by our radius bounds contains a Birkhoff diamond, a reducible configuration, so these partial results do not bear on a minimal counterexample. Results that hold at every degree-5 hole of a triangulated spherical map, and so do bear on the frame class, are compiled in Lean: a lock-parity theorem, and a chain-parity law for the number of Kempe chains, proved through a mod-4 chain-count formula whose Lean proof derives Tutte's parity identity by linear algebra over $\mathbb Z/2$. Together these exclude Kempe classes made only of rigid states. We also compile the reduction of R$^\ast$ on the frame class to the absence of frozen Kempe orbits, and report an exhaustive census of the frame class to order 33. VH$\exists$ and R$^\ast$ are open, and nothing about the Four Colour Theorem is proved here. We state the remaining gap as a conjecture about Kempe classes on the sphere (LPC), whose near-rigid case reduces to a statement about single orbits (NRC). We also record the most instructive failures, and the repository that holds the full record.\end{abstract}

\section{The vacancy induction and the hypothesis VH$\exists$}\label{sec:vh}
% Sources: LT/swarm/hole-induction.md ; LT/swarm/vh-exists.md (Theorem A and Lemmas 3.1, 3.2 accepted by Math; START-HERE.md section 6).
A \emph{spherical triangulation} is a simple graph $T$ on $n\ge4$ vertices with a fixed embedding in the sphere in which every face is a triangle. A \emph{colouring} is a proper colouring with the colours $\{0,1,2,3\}$. The neighbours $x_0,\dots,x_{d-1}$ of a vertex $v$ of degree $d$, in rotation order, form a cycle, the \emph{link} of $v$.

\paragraph{Fans.} Let $v$ have degree 5. The \emph{fan} with apex $x_i$ is the pair of chords $\tau_i=\{x_ix_{i+2},x_ix_{i+3}\}$ (indices mod 5). It is \emph{legal} if neither chord is an edge of $T$; some fan is always legal. For a legal fan, $T^\ast_\tau=(T-v)+\tau$ is a spherical triangulation on $n-1$ vertices. \lab{hand}
% vh-exists.md Lemma 1.2.

\paragraph{States and moves.} A \emph{state} $(h,c)$ is a vertex $h$, the \emph{hole}, with a colouring $c$ of $T-h$. A \emph{Kempe swap} exchanges two colours on one connected component of their two-colour subgraph of $T-h$. A \emph{singleton slide} moves the hole to a neighbour $u$ whose colour occurs exactly once on the link of $h$, giving $h$ that colour. A state is \emph{filled} if the link of its hole uses at most three colours; then the hole can be coloured. Both moves are reversible. A path of swaps only is \emph{pure}; its hole never moves. \lab{hand}
% vh-exists.md section 1.2 and Lemma 1.3.

\paragraph{The hypothesis.} The \emph{starts} of a legal pair $(v,\tau)$ are the colourings of $T-v$ that are proper on both chords, that is, the colourings of $T^\ast_\tau$. The pair is \emph{good} if every start reaches a filled state by slides and swaps. \textbf{VH$\exists$} says: every spherical triangulation of minimum degree 5 has a good pair, chosen before the colouring. VH$\exists$ is \lab{open}.
% vh-exists.md section 1.3; quantifier order from REP/IndependentWP18AndBeltReview.md.

\begin{theorem}[VH$\exists$ implies 4-colourability; \lab{hand}]\label{thm:A}
If VH$\exists$ holds, every spherical triangulation has a 4-colouring.
\end{theorem}
% vh-exists.md Theorem A; accepted by Math.
\begin{proof}[Sketch]
Induction on $n$, from $K_4$. Since $\sum_v(6-\deg v)=12$, some vertex $v$ has degree at most 5. Degree 3: colour $T-v$ and give $v$ a free colour. Degree 4: add a missing diagonal of the link, colour, and restrict to $T-v$; if the link uses four colours, one Kempe swap in $T-v$ frees a colour, by the Jordan curve theorem. Minimum degree 5: take a good pair $(v_0,\tau)$, colour $T^\ast_\tau$ by induction; its colouring is a start, which reaches a filled state of $T$.
\end{proof}
The hypothesis is applied once, to $T$; the induction is on $T^\ast_\tau$. An induction confined to minimum degree 5 does not close, because $T^\ast_\tau$ can leave that class. Two lemmas make the hypothesis concrete. \emph{Containment:} two starts in the same Kempe class of $T^\ast_\tau$ are joined by Kempe swaps of $T-v$, so it suffices to check one start per class. \emph{Apex singleton:} at every start the apex colour occurs once on the link, so the slide to the apex is always legal. \lab{hand}
% hole-induction.md "The circular variant"; vh-exists.md Remark 2.1, Lemmas 3.1 and 3.2.

\paragraph{Context.} Kempe's argument at a degree-5 vertex makes two swaps at once, and Heawood showed that the second chain can be blocked~\cite{kempe1879,heawood1890}. The proofs of the Four Colour Theorem show instead that a minimal counterexample contains none of a large unavoidable set of reducible configurations~\cite{birkhoff1913,appel1977,robertson1997}; the proof of Robertson, Sanders, Seymour and Thomas was formally verified in Coq~\cite{gonthier}, and a recent paper finds linearly many reducible configurations~\cite{inoue2026}. \lab{cited} VH$\exists$ uses the single configuration ``one degree-5 vertex'', and gets past Heawood's objection only by allowing long, non-local paths: Kempe chains anywhere, and slides that move the hole.
% vh-exists.md section 4.5; literature-check.md.

\section{The reduction to Lemma R$^\ast$, and what is proved toward it}\label{sec:rstar}
% Sources: WK/MathReviewCleanToVHE.md (links L1-L6); audit re-derivation MSG/..._1325_audit_..._R-star-chain-adversarial-read.md;
% REP/MathTriangleCarryResearch.md, REP/MathVHCoreAdvance.md, REP/MathFixedHoleReview.md; Navigator revisions 119, 122, 130, 131.
\paragraph{Locks and radius.} Let $s$ be an unfilled state at a degree-5 hole $v$. Its link uses four colours with one repeat; rotate so that it reads $(\alpha,\beta,\alpha,\gamma,\delta)$ at $x_0,\dots,x_4$. The state is \emph{doubly locked} if $T-v$ has a $\{\beta,\gamma\}$-path from $x_1$ to $x_3$ and a $\{\beta,\delta\}$-path from $x_1$ to $x_4$; otherwise it fills in one swap. Its \emph{Kempe radius} is the least number of Kempe swaps of $T-v$ that fill it. The vertex $v$ is \emph{clean} if every state at $v$ has finite radius.
% WK/creative-intel-2026-10-05/l-attack.md section 1; WK/MathConjectureR.md section 1.

\paragraph{The relative class.} Let $\mathcal C$ be the class of spherical triangulations with a designated face $\varphi$ in which every vertex off $\varphi$ has degree at least 5. A pair is \emph{$\varphi$-good} if it is good with every hole along every path kept off $\varphi$; swaps may recolour $\varphi$. $\mathrm{VH}_{\mathcal C}$ asks for a $\varphi$-good pair in every member. A least failure of $\mathrm{VH}_{\mathcal C}$ is 4-connected and has order at least 12, and $\mathrm{VH}_{\mathcal C}$ implies VH$\exists$. \lab{hand}
% REP/MathTriangleCarryResearch.md (triangle reduction); REP/MathVHCoreAdvance.md items 5 (order >= 12; Navigator revision 120 ruling 3); ledger structural-face-reduction.

The property asked of $v$ in the next statement, that every colouring of $T-v$ reaches a filled state by Kempe swaps of $T-v$, is, as far as we can tell, what Tilley calls \emph{D-resolvability} of $v$. Tilley proved that a degree-5 vertex is not D-reducible and left open whether every degree-5 vertex of a planar graph is D-resolvable, a statement stronger than 4-colourability~\cite{tilley2017}. \lab{cited} Lemma R$^\ast$ asks for one D-resolvable degree-5 vertex in each core triangulation, so it is not our own conjecture: Tilley's open problem implies it.
% Tilley: MSG/..._1514_longtable_..._CORRECTION-R-star-is-Tilley-D-resolvability.md (abstract verified by Long Table; full-text definition match reported by a sub-agent, audit check pending). Coordinator: say 'as far as we can tell'.
\begin{conjecture}[Lemma R$^\ast$; \lab{open}]
In every 4-connected member of $\mathcal C$, some degree-5 vertex off $\varphi$ has every doubly locked state reach a filled state by finitely many Kempe swaps.
\end{conjecture}
\begin{theorem}[\lab{hand}]\label{thm:chain}
Lemma R$^\ast$ implies $\mathrm{VH}_{\mathcal C}$, hence VH$\exists$, hence (Theorem~\ref{thm:A}) 4-colourability.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}[Sketch]
Finite radius at every doubly locked state makes the vertex clean, since a state that is not doubly locked fills in one swap. A clean vertex off $\varphi$ has a $\varphi$-good fan, because its paths are pure and the hole never moves. Take a least failure of $\mathrm{VH}_{\mathcal C}$: it is 4-connected of order at least 12, so Lemma R$^\ast$ gives a clean degree-5 vertex off $\varphi$, hence a $\varphi$-good pair, a contradiction.
% Audit claim read C2 (MSG/..._1502_audit_...): the chain does not use the Euler lemma. Finally $\mathrm{VH}_{\mathcal C}$ implies VH$\exists$ by lifting good pairs across separating triangles.
\end{proof}
% Links L3-L6 of MathReviewCleanToVHE.md; L1 (Theorem A of MathConfinementAttack) and L2 (Corollary B) explain why clean-vertex existence is necessary.

The six links of this chain were reviewed by a Math sub-agent and re-derived independently by the audit. \textbf{Nothing about the Four Colour Theorem is proved by this.} Lemma R$^\ast$ is open, and it is at least as strong as VH$\exists$, not weaker. Our contribution here is the reduction, not the statement of R$^\ast$.

\paragraph{The frame class.} Reducible configurations shrink the class R$^\ast$ must cover. Let $\mathcal F$ be the class of connected spherical triangulations of minimum degree 5 with no separating triangle and with no \emph{occurrence} of the Birkhoff diamond (interior degrees $(5,5,5,5)$, ring size 6) or of configuration 2.122 of~\cite{robertson1997} (interior degrees $(6,5,5,5)$, ring size 7), in either orientation. An occurrence is ring-embedded: distinct ring vertices and the full rotation at each interior vertex.
\begin{theorem}[\lab{compiled}]\label{thm:frame}
If every member of $\mathcal F$ has a degree-5 vertex $v$ at which every colouring of $T-v$ fills by finitely many pure Kempe swaps, then every spherical map is 4-colourable.
\end{theorem}
This is \texttt{four\_color\_of\_RStarFrame}, with hypothesis \texttt{RStarFrame}; the audit passed its statement and its module check. The proof is a strong induction (\texttt{four\_color\_of\_smaller\_gate}): a separating triangle is reduced by colouring both sides and gluing (\texttt{colorable\_of\_separating\_triangle}); an occurrence of either configuration by a generated D-reducibility certificate (\texttt{colorable\_of\_occ}), which uses the compiled non-crossing of Kempe chains on a ring; otherwise the hypothesis gives $v$, which is coloured last (\texttt{extend\_of\_pureClean}). The core-class hypothesis of Section~\ref{sec:lean} implies \texttt{RStarFrame} (\texttt{rStarNoSepTri\_of\_core}, \texttt{rStarFrame\_of\_noSepTri}), so it is the weakest form of R$^\ast$ in the library, and still open. Here, as throughout, a spherical map is the library's combinatorial object (a graph with a rotation system whose cycles are sums of faces); the passage from a topological embedding is not formalised. Both exclusions bite: the icosahedron contains the diamond and an order-22 triangulation contains 2.122 (\texttt{not\_diamondFree}, \texttt{not\_conf2122Free}) \lab{compiled}. The class is not empty: a 22-vertex member is checked in Lean (\texttt{frameClass\_nonempty}) \lab{compiled}$^\dagger$.

\paragraph{From appearances to occurrences.} Robertson et al.\ say a configuration \emph{appears} when an induced subgraph has the prescribed degrees, a weaker notion than an occurrence. In a triangulation with no separating triangle, an appearance of either configuration whose two tips have no common neighbour other than the two centres (\texttt{TipsClean}) is an occurrence in one orientation (\texttt{occ\_of\_appears}). So Theorem~\ref{thm:frame} also holds with $\mathcal F$ defined by excluding appearances with clean tips (\texttt{four\_color\_of\_RStarFrameApp}, via \texttt{rStarFrame\_of\_app}). \lab{built, audit pending} If the tips are not clean, they and a third vertex lie on a separating 4-cycle through a centre. Removing separating 4-cycles is the classical reduction (F2), and it is not formalised, so the Lean class is not yet exactly the class with no appearance.

\paragraph{What is proved toward R$^\ast$.}
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Theorem H.} If $v$ and its five neighbours have degree 5 and no separating triangle passes through $v$, every doubly locked state at $v$ has radius at most 3.
% WK/MathRadiusGeometry.md (with erratum); ledger structural-theorem-h, structural-theorem-h-lean.
\item \emph{Theorem HP.} If $v$ has degree 5, four of its neighbours have degree 5, the fifth has any degree, and no separating triangle passes through $v$, every doubly locked state at $v$ has radius at most 6, so $v$ is clean.
% Ledger structural-theorem-hp, structural-theorem-hp-lean (revision 131). The order-22 example on its source page is not cited (no face list).
\item \emph{Weak F6.} If $v$ has degree 5, four of its neighbours have degree 5 and the fifth has degree $d\ge5$, and either no separating triangle passes through $v$ or $d\le6$, then $v$ is clean. With no separating triangle this is the case of Theorem HP, which is stronger (it bounds the radius by 6). It is new only for $d\in\{5,6\}$ at a vertex on a separating triangle, where the hand result on separating triangles below already applies. Its proof is independent of HP's, through the permutation $\pi$ of Section~\ref{sec:quarter}.
% pureClean_of_four_consecutive_fives (QuarterHole4Bridge.lean), pureClean_of_degrees (QuarterHole6Bridge.lean), pureClean_of_hole4 (QuarterHole6Gen.lean);
% compared with theorem_HP (VacancyIcosahedral.lean) and pureClean_of_theorem_HP (RStar.lean) by reading both statements; ledger qf-weak-f6 (revision 143).
\item \emph{Euler lemma.} Every triangulation of minimum degree 5 has at least 12 degree-5 vertices with at most one neighbour of degree $\ge12$; in the relative class, at least $9-n_4$ such vertices lie off $\varphi$, where $n_4\le2$ is the number of degree-4 vertices on $\varphi$.
% MSG/..._1240_audit_..._counting-lemma.md; MSG/..._1233_math_..._Euler-lemma-accepted-strengthened.md; WK/MathReviewCleanToVHE.md section 6 (relative repair).
\item \emph{Separating triangles.} A degree-5 vertex on a separating triangle fills within two Kempe swaps from every colouring, so it gives a good pair; in any failure of VH$\exists$ every vertex of a separating triangle has degree at least 6.
% REP/MathFixedHoleReview.md; ledger structural-fixed-hole-triangle.
\end{itemize}
In the 4-connected setting, Theorems H and HP give finite radius at every degree-5 vertex with at most one neighbour of degree other than 5; this includes every degree-5 vertex of Florek's two-pole graphs. A fill lemma for every hole of the Euler class would imply VH$\exists$, so the Euler lemma says only where to look. \lab{hand}
% MSG/..._1248_audit_..._fill-lemma-equals-VHE-strength.md item 1.

\paragraph{Scope: these holes contain a Birkhoff diamond.} If three cyclically consecutive neighbours $x_j,x_{j+1},x_{j+2}$ of a degree-5 vertex $v$ have degree 5 and no separating triangle passes through $v$, then $v,x_j,x_{j+1},x_{j+2}$ span a Birkhoff diamond, with centres $v$ and $x_{j+1}$ and tips $x_j$ and $x_{j+2}$; a run of degrees $(5,6,5)$ likewise spans an appearance of 2.122. \lab{hand} Four of five link vertices always include three consecutive ones, so every hole covered by Theorems H and HP, weak F6 and Theorem F5 (Section~\ref{sec:quarter}) contains a Birkhoff diamond. The diamond is reducible~\cite{birkhoff1913}, and its D-reducibility certificate is compiled in both orientations, so these holes do not occur in a minimal counterexample: \textbf{these results do not move the Four Colour Theorem}. They test the method where reducibility already decides. In Lean such a hole lies outside $\mathcal F$ when the tips are clean (\texttt{no\_555\_run}, \texttt{no\_565\_run}; minimum degree 5 is not needed) \lab{compiled}$^\dagger$; otherwise the tips lie on a separating 4-cycle, the unformalised case F2.

\section{The quarter floor}\label{sec:quarter}
% Sources: WK/MathQuarterFloorFinal.md; WK/MathQuarterFloorLemmaA.md; WK/MathQuarterFloorBijections.md; WK/QuarterFloorSection-draft.md;
% WK/interns-2026-10-06/intern-A-cycle8.md; MSG 1753, 1805, 1811, 1827, 1848, 1855, 1901, 1920, 1925; commits c24d895, 0c0e098, 8ab6f1d, 2163e02, 9c1e03c, 707840b, 534dfb2, 3a346c8, ba90dfd.
Fix a degree-5 vertex $v$ of a triangulation $T$, with link $x_0,\dots,x_4$ (indices mod 5), and a Kempe class $S$ of 4-colourings of $T-v$. A state is \emph{filled} if its link uses three colours; let $F_i\subseteq S$ be the filled states whose singleton colour sits at $x_i$, and $F=\sum_i|F_i|$. An unfilled state has one repeated colour, at $x_j,x_{j+2}$; let $U_j\subseteq S$ be these states, $D_j\subseteq U_j$ the doubly locked ones, and $U=\sum_j|U_j|$. Every state uses four colours, so all ratios below are the same for labelled colourings and up to renaming. Tilley's D-resolvability asks only $F>0$; the data suggest much more.

\paragraph{Observation.} At orders 12--24, at every degree-5 vertex of every triangulation of minimum degree 5, every Kempe class of $T-v$ has \emph{at least a quarter of its states filled}: $U\le 3F$. This covers 156{,}033 holes and 160{,}979 classes; 419 classes sit exactly at $1/4$ and none lies strictly between $1/4$ and $16/59$. At degree 6 and 7 there is no such floor (minima $1/8$ and $2/17$). An adversarial search over 95{,}884 further core graphs found no class below $1/4$. \lab{computed} In the exhaustive frame-class census of orders 22--33 the floor is met exactly and never broken (Section~\ref{sec:open}).
% 0c0e098 (MSG 1805); 9c1e03c (MSG 1848). The statement was found from data, so these checks are post hoc.
Since a filled state extends uniquely to $T$, summing over the classes of one hole gives $P(T,4)\ge\frac14P(T-v,4)$.

\paragraph{The fan identity.} Let $G=T-v$ and let $G_i$ be $G$ with $x_i$ and $x_{i+2}$ identified. In a proper colouring of the 5-cycle with $k$ colours exactly $5-k$ of the pairs $\{x_i,x_{i+2}\}$ are equal, so for every $q$
\[P(T,q)\;=\;(q-5)\,P(G,q)\;+\;\sum_{i=0}^{4}P(G_i,q).\]
\lab{hand} (Long Table at $q=4$; Intern A at general $q$; unreviewed.) For $q\ge5$ the coefficient $q-5$ is non-negative, so positivity of $P(T,q)$ follows from positivity for smaller planar graphs; this is consistent with Birkhoff and Lewis's theorem that $P(G,q)>0$ for planar $G$ and real $q\ge5$~\cite{birkhoff1946}, though we have not checked that it is their argument. At $q=4$ the coefficient is $-1$: $P(T,4)=\sum_iP(G_i,4)-P(G,4)$, and the induction would need $\sum_iP(G_i,4)>P(G,4)$, which is the statement $F>0$ again. The quarter floor is the quantitative form $\sum_iP(G_i,4)\ge\frac54P(G,4)$; for a free 5-cycle the filled fraction is $1/2$. \lab{hand}
% WK/QuarterFloorLiterature.md (no prior statement of such a floor found; Birkhoff-Lewis conjecture at q >= 4 open).

\paragraph{What is proved.}
\begin{lemma}[\lab{hand}, audited: correct per $j$]\label{lem:quarterA}
In every Kempe class and for every $j$, $|U_j\setminus D_j|\le|F_{j+3}|+|F_{j+4}|$.
\end{lemma}
The map is Kempe's own swap, made injective: if the lock to $x_{j+3}$ fails, swap the component of $x_{j+3}$ in its two colours; otherwise swap that of $x_{j+4}$. The map is at most 2-to-1 over all $j$ (both cases occur), so $\sum_j|U_j\setminus D_j|\le2F$: the states that are not doubly locked are paid for. Only the doubly locked states remain.
% MathQuarterFloorLemmaA.md; 81,571 filled states with exactly two preimages (2163e02).

Join each unfilled state with the lock to $x_{j+4}$ to its image under the rotation $R$ (swap the component of $x_{j+2}$ in colours $\{\alpha,c(x_{j+3})\}$). The resulting graph is a disjoint union of paths and cycles, and
\[3F-U\;=\;2N_0+\tfrac32L_F+\sum_{\text{paths }P}\bigl(1-d(P)\bigr)-D_{\mathrm{cyc}},\]
where $N_0$ counts unfilled states with neither lock, $L_F$ the ``long bits'' of filled states, $d(P)$ the doubly locked states inside a path, and $D_{\mathrm{cyc}}$ those on cycles. \lab{hand}, reviewed by two interns, not yet by the audit; it holds with no mismatch on all 46{,}488 degree-5 classes of orders 12--23 and the order-24 floor holes \lab{computed}. At the floor every term vanishes, which explains the observed structure: four equal blocks $F_i,U_{i+1},D_{i+4},U_{i+2}$.
% MathQuarterFloorBijections.md sections 2-3; interns B (7ba6411) and C (4fac326); 2163e02.

\begin{conjecture}[Quarter floor, per-$j$ form; \lab{open}]
For every degree-5 vertex, every Kempe class of $T-v$ and every $j$: $|U_j|\le|F_{j+1}|+|F_{j+3}|+|F_{j+4}|$. Summed over $j$, every class has at least a quarter of its states filled.
\end{conjecture}
Given Lemma~\ref{lem:quarterA} and the identity, this is equivalent to $|DD_j|\le\mathrm{room}_j$, where $DD_j$ is the set of doubly locked states whose rotation is again doubly locked and $\mathrm{room}_j$ is a sum of terms from the identity. It held in every class tested, with equality occurring; in the adversarial search the largest excess $|DD_j|-\mathrm{room}_j$ was 0, although all-doubly-locked rotation cycles of length up to 880 occur. \lab{computed}
% 9c1e03c; MathQuarterFloorFinal.md data table.

\paragraph{Lean form: a permutation and two identities.} Kempe's swap and the rotation combine into one Kempe step $\pi$ that permutes every Kempe class (\texttt{piMove\_bijOn\_class}). An unfilled state with the lock to $x_{j+4}$ goes to its rotation, one without it to a filled state, and a filled state to an unfilled or a filled state according to one further lock; give these four cases the weights $\lambda=+1,-1,-1,-3$. On every finite set $S$ of states with $\pi(S)=S$, such as a Kempe class:
\[3F-U\;=\;-\sum_{S}\lambda\;=\;-5w\quad(w\in\mathbb Z),\qquad \sum_{S}\lambda\;=\;|DD|-2N_0-E_2-3\tau,\]
where $|DD|$ counts the states $c$ with $c$ and $\pi c$ both doubly locked, $N_0$ the unfilled states with neither lock, $E_2$ the unfilled runs of length 2, and $\tau$ the states $c$ with $c$ and $\pi c$ both filled. The first is \emph{Theorem W}; with it the quarter floor at $v$ is equivalent to $\sum\lambda\le0$ on every class (\texttt{three\_F\_sub\_U\_winding\_class}, \texttt{quarterFloor\_iff\_lam}). The second is the exact class identity (\texttt{exact\_identity\_class}). \lab{built} The exact identity held with no failure on about 4.2 million classes \lab{computed} (exploratory, not declared in advance). Planarity enters only through Jordan separation at the hole, which makes the swaps defining $\pi$ well defined. If every $\pi$-orbit at $v$ contains a state that is not doubly locked, $v$ is clean (\texttt{pureClean\_of\_no\_allDL\_orbit}) \lab{built}; so a failure of R$^\ast$ at $v$ is a $\pi$-orbit of doubly locked states, a \emph{frozen} orbit.
% QuarterPi.lean, QuarterWinding.lean, QuarterLemmaP.lean, QuarterBitDynamics.lean; ledger qf-theorem-w (revision 143; 4.2M classes = Studio Job D). WK/NightEulerHole.md.

\begin{theorem}[F5; \lab{built}]\label{thm:F5}
If the five neighbours of $v$ all have degree 5, every Kempe class of $T-v$ is at least a quarter filled. No separating-triangle hypothesis is needed.
\end{theorem}
This is \texttt{quarterFloor\_of\_fiveLink}. In the hand proof the swap of the $\{\alpha,\beta\}$-component of $x_{j+1}$ maps the doubly locked states of one type injectively, within the class, to states with neither lock, and each such state accounts for at most two of the steps counted by $|DD|$, so $|DD|\le2N_0$ and $\sum\lambda\le0$; the proof was reviewed adversarially by another AI session. \lab{hand} The census minimum of exactly $1/4$ shows it is sharp on the data. As Section~\ref{sec:rstar} notes, every such hole contains a Birkhoff diamond.

\paragraph{Strength, and why it is global.} The conjecture implies $F>0$ in every class, which is Tilley's D-resolvability at every degree-5 vertex, so it is \emph{at least as strong as R$^\ast$, and hence as the Four Colour Theorem}. It is a quantitative strengthening of a published open problem, not a way around it. It cannot be proved locally: a class with no filled state would satisfy every local fact used above, and doubly locked rotation orbits can be infinite ($A_r$). The data agree: a charging injection that looks only within $k$ Kempe swaps needs $k_{\min}=7$ at order 24 (per $j$; 6 if pooled over $j$), growing slowly with order, although about 98\% of cases match within 3 swaps. \lab{computed} No fixed-radius charging proof of this form exists on the data.

\section{Planarity at every hole: lock parity and the chain-parity law}\label{sec:parity}
% Sources: WK/TrackF/LockParity.md; WK/TrackC/README.md sections 5-9; WK/TrackK/FProof.md and TrackK-review; WK/TrackI/RigidIsolation.md and TrackI-review;
% WK/TrackJ/README.md, WK/TrackL/SigmaType.md, WK/TrackJL-review/README.md; CoordinatorPlan.md status log 7-8 Oct.
% Commits b60a2896 (lock parity), a81e5098 (ChainCount), a245e43e (ChainMod4), 82ec9162 (F review), 1a3e5454 (ChainF), 8f8ecb40 (JL review).
Theorems H, HP, F5 and weak F6 hold only at holes that contain a Birkhoff diamond. The results of this section hold at every degree-5 hole of every triangulated spherical map, and they fail on other surfaces. Unlike those theorems, then, they bear on the holes of the frame class. None of them yet excludes a frozen orbit.

Fix a degree-5 hole $v$ and an unfilled state $c$, rotated as in Section~\ref{sec:rstar} so that the link reads $(\alpha,\beta,\alpha,\gamma,\delta)$ at $x_0,\dots,x_4$. Write $K_{pq}(x)$ for the vertex set of the component of $x$ in the $\{p,q\}$-subgraph of $T-v$. The two locks are $L_1$: $x_3\in K_{\beta\gamma}(x_1)$, and $L_2$: $x_4\in K_{\beta\delta}(x_1)$; we also write $L_i\in\{0,1\}$ for their indicators. At a doubly locked state, $\pi$ swaps $K_{\alpha\gamma}(x_2)$.

\begin{theorem}[Lock parity; \lab{compiled}$^\dagger$]\label{thm:LP}
On a triangulated spherical map, $L_2$ holds if and only if an odd number of edges of $T$ leave $K_{\alpha\gamma}(x_2)$ (edges to $v$ included), if and only if $K_{\alpha\gamma}(x_2)$ contains an odd number of vertices of odd degree in $T$. The same holds for $L_1$ with $K_{\alpha\delta}(x_2)$. An odd number of edges always leave $K_{\alpha\beta}(x_2)$.
\end{theorem}
The proof has two parts. The first is a parity identity on every oriented triangulated surface, with no planarity: an odd number of edges leave $K_{\alpha\gamma}(x_2)$ if and only if $x_0\notin K_{\alpha\gamma}(x_2)$, and likewise for $K_{\alpha\delta}(x_2)$. It is a dart sum checked against a finite table by \texttt{decide} (\texttt{parity\_alphaA}, \texttt{parity\_alphaB}, \texttt{parity\_alphaMu}). The second is Kempe duality at the hole, $L_2\Leftrightarrow x_0\notin K_{\alpha\gamma}(x_2)$ and $L_1\Leftrightarrow x_0\notin K_{\alpha\delta}(x_2)$, which was already in the library and is where the sphere enters. The theorem is \texttt{lock2\_iff\_odd\_boundary}, \texttt{lock1\_iff\_odd\_boundary}, their odd-degree forms and \texttt{alphaMu\_odd\_boundary}. Off the sphere duality can fail; in a class with no filled state, the states where it fails are exactly the ends of the non-cyclic $\pi$-orbits. \lab{hand}, not independently reviewed. On the sphere, then, a class with no filled state is a union of doubly locked $\pi$-cycles.

\paragraph{Chain counts.} Let $N(c)$ be the number of Kempe chains of $c$: summed over the six colour pairs, the number of components of the two-colour subgraph of $T-v$ (\texttt{nChains}). A doubly locked state is \emph{rigid} if its pairs $\alpha\beta,\gamma\delta,\alpha\gamma,\beta\delta,\alpha\delta,\beta\gamma$ have $1,1,2,1,2,1$ chains (\texttt{RigidAt}). On a triangulated sphere every doubly locked state has $N\ge8$, with equality exactly at rigid states (\texttt{eight\_le\_nChains}, \texttt{rigidAt\_iff}). \lab{compiled}$^\dagger$

\paragraph{The chain-count formula.} Identify the colours with $\mathbb Z_2^2$. Read each face $(u,w,z)$ in the orientation of the map and call it \emph{clockwise} if its triple $(c(u){+}c(w),\,c(w){+}c(z),\,c(z){+}c(u))$ is a cyclic shift of $(1,2,3)$. Let $\mathrm{cw}(c)$ count the clockwise faces not containing $v$, and let $\eta(c)\in\{0,1\}$ indicate whether $(\alpha{+}\beta,\alpha{+}\gamma,\alpha{+}\delta)$ is a cyclic shift of $(1,2,3)$, the link being read along the face orientation.
\begin{theorem}[Chain-count formula; \lab{compiled}$^\dagger$]\label{thm:F}
Let $T$ be a triangulated spherical map on $n$ vertices, none isolated. At every unfilled state at a degree-5 hole,
\[2N(c)\;\equiv\;\mathrm{cw}(c)+(n-1)-\eta(c)+2(L_1+L_2)\pmod 4.\]
\end{theorem}
This is \texttt{conjectureF}; it was found in data and named Conjecture~F before it was proved. The hand proof was accepted by an independent AI review. Without the hole, it is a mod-4 form of Tutte's parity theorem for 4-colourings of plane triangulations, $J_A=2|A|-\deg(A)+n-3$, where $J_A$ adds the numbers of chains of the three pairs containing the colour $A$ and subtracts those of the other three, combined with Fisk's degree of the colouring as a simplicial map to the boundary of the tetrahedron, both as presented by Mohar and Singer~\cite{mohar2019} (Theorems~1 and~3 there) \lab{cited}. At the hole, the pentagon is filled with the two lock diagonals $x_1x_3$ and $x_1x_4$, which merge two chains exactly when a lock fails. \lab{hand} The Lean proof uses no topology beyond the library's planarity condition (every even edge set is a sum of faces). For each of the three ways of splitting the colours into two pairs, it proves Tutte's identity for the two sides by rank computations over $\mathbb Z/2$ (\texttt{tutte\_sides}, with Euler's formula as \texttt{euler\_tri}). It puts $v$ on one side, where its two edges to that side play the role of the lock diagonals, and it computes $\mathrm{cw}$ mod 4 by a dart sum against a table checked by \texttt{decide}. A local lemma W says that every swap of a component meeting no link vertex leaves $\mathrm{cw}$ unchanged mod 4, on every oriented triangulated surface (\texttt{lemmaW}, \texttt{lemmaW\_linkFree}), and that on the sphere $\pi$ at a doubly locked state changes $\mathrm{cw}$ by $2$ mod 4 (\texttt{cw\_piMove}) \lab{compiled}$^\dagger$. Its first statement, without a boundary term at the link, was false.

\begin{theorem}[Chain-parity law; \lab{compiled}$^\dagger$]\label{thm:law}
Let $T$ be a triangulated spherical map without isolated vertices, and $c$ a doubly locked state at a degree-5 hole. Then $N(\pi c)+N(c)$ is odd if and only if $\pi c$ is doubly locked.
\end{theorem}
This is \texttt{chainParityLaw\_sphere}, which combines Theorem~\ref{thm:F} with lemma W (\texttt{chainParityLaw\_of\_F}). A second hand proof, by curve surgery in the dual, was accepted by an independent AI review. \lab{hand} Three consequences are compiled. \emph{Rigid isolation}: the $\pi$-image of a rigid state is not rigid (\texttt{rigid\_isolation}); since $\pi$ permutes each class, no Kempe class consists only of rigid states (a one-line corollary, not stated separately in Lean, \lab{hand}). \emph{Remark~7}: at an unfilled state, a swap of a component that meets no link vertex preserves $N+L_1+L_2$ mod 2 (\texttt{remark7}). \lab{compiled}$^\dagger$ And along a doubly locked $\pi$-cycle $N$ alternates in parity, so the cycle has even length. \lab{hand} All of this is specific to the sphere: on the torus, the Klein bottle and the projective plane a rigid state can map to a rigid state. \lab{computed}
% Rigid->rigid off the sphere: TrackH-review (torus 23, Klein 1, RP2 29). Chain-parity law fails on 282/544 torus pi-steps (TrackC section 6.4).

\paragraph{Near-rigid classes.} Call a doubly locked state \emph{near-rigid} if $N\le9$. An \emph{in-shape} state has $N=9$ and both $\pi$-neighbours rigid.
\begin{lemma}[\lab{hand}, independent AI review]\label{lem:J}
On a triangulated sphere every unfilled state at a degree-5 hole has exactly 8 chains that meet the link, so $N-8$ counts the chains that miss it. If a Kempe class consists only of states on near-rigid doubly locked $\pi$-cycles, then: along each cycle $N$ alternates $8,9,8,9,\dots$; every state with $N=9$ is in-shape; its single link-free chain $Z$ is an $\{\alpha,\beta\}$-chain (the \emph{$\sigma$-type lemma}, proved by Euler counting); swapping $Z$ is a fixed-point-free involution of these states; and every cycle has length divisible by 10 and at least 20.
\end{lemma}
These are Lemmas J1--J5 of one AI session and the $\sigma$-type lemma of another. A third session reviewed them adversarially with code written from scratch, found every step correct on the sphere (no failure on up to $4.5\times10^8$ states, \lab{computed}), and corrected two statements that do not affect the lemma: rigidity must be defined by the chain counts, and one inequality holds on the sphere only. The shape is realised in 43 non-planar 4-colourable graphs in which every edge lies in a triangle and the chain-parity law holds \lab{computed}, so excluding it must use planarity itself. The lemma reduces near-rigid classes to the single-orbit statement NRC of Section~\ref{sec:open}.

\section{What does not work}\label{sec:neg}
Most first attempts were false. These are the most instructive, each with its exact scope.

\paragraph{Uniform short fills.} One might hope a well-chosen pair fills every start within a fixed number of moves. On the order-17 triangulation 17:1, every legal pair has a start that needs 3 mixed moves \lab{computed}; on 24:7228 a start needs 3 mixed moves but 5 pure swaps \lab{computed}; and on general graphs pure distance is not bounded by any function of mixed distance \lab{hand}. Slides alone and one swap then slides fail at a frozen 21-vertex hole \lab{computed}; whether some uniform fixed swap budget suffices is \lab{open}. Lean confirms the positive side only up to length 2: a mixed fill of length at most 2 gives a pure fill of the same length \lab{compiled}.

\paragraph{Lock persistence (Conjecture L) and the family $A_r$.} Conjecture~L said that repeating one fixed swap $F$ keeps a state doubly locked only a bounded number of times, which would have made every vertex clean. It is false: a 20-vertex triangulation has a chain of length 6, and on the 5-fold symmetric triangulations $A_3,A_4,A_5$ (minimum degree 5) some states stay doubly locked under $F$ forever, with period 60. These certificates were confirmed by three independent programs: the L-Attack team's code, the Long Table lead's verifier and the audit's replay. \lab{computed} Yet every such state still fills in 2 or 3 swaps: locks persist, but other swaps fill. The number of infinite-orbit classes on $A_r$ for $r=3,\dots,9$ is 20 times a Jacobsthal number \lab{computed}, not replayed by the audit beyond $r=5$; that this continues is \lab{open}.

\paragraph{Radius is not bounded by 3.} The data on $A_r$ suggested radius at most 3. \section{The open problem, and the record}\label{sec:open}
\textbf{The open problem} is Lemma R$^\ast$ in the case that Theorems H and HP leave: a 4-connected member of $\mathcal C$ in which every degree-5 vertex off $\varphi$ has at least two neighbours of degree other than 5. As far as we can tell it is a case of Tilley's open D-resolvability problem~\cite{tilley2017}, which asks the same of every degree-5 vertex. There the radius can be 4 ($T_4$ is such a graph), and local data do not decide it. A pre-registered search found four doubly locked states of radius exactly 5 in triangulations of minimum degree 5 with no separating triangle, at vertices that are still clean, so R$^\ast$ is not refuted; these certificates were replayed by the audit. \lab{computed}

In its weakest Lean form the problem is R$^\ast$ on the frame class $\mathcal F$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:frame}): find, in each member, a degree-5 vertex with no frozen $\pi$-orbit, which suffices (\texttt{rStarFrame\_of\_no\_allDL\_orbit}). With clean tips, no degree-5 vertex of $\mathcal F$ has three consecutive neighbours of degree 5 or a consecutive $(5,6,5)$ run, so none of Theorems H, HP, F5 or weak F6 applies there. Section~\ref{sec:parity} applies everywhere, and organises the problem as follows. \lab{open}

\textbf{LPC}, in its form on the sphere (on other surfaces it concerns classes all of whose states obey lock parity). \emph{At a degree-5 hole of a triangulated sphere, no Kempe class of $T-v$ consists only of states on doubly locked $\pi$-cycles.} \lab{open} On the sphere $\pi$ permutes each class, and a class with no filled state has only doubly locked states (Section~\ref{sec:quarter}), so LPC says that every Kempe class at every degree-5 hole has a filled state. That is Tilley's D-resolvability at every degree-5 vertex, more than R$^\ast$ needs, and it implies \texttt{NoFrozenFrame} and so, by compiled steps, the Four Colour Theorem for spherical maps. Sort a hypothetical counterexample class by the chain counts along its cycles. A class of rigid states is excluded by rigid isolation, and a cycle on which $N$ is constant by the chain-parity law \lab{compiled}$^\dagger$. A class of near-rigid cycles has the shape of Lemma~\ref{lem:J} \lab{hand}, and is excluded by NRC below. Cycles with higher or mixed counts have no handle yet.

\textbf{NRC} (near-rigid cycle exclusion). \emph{On a triangulated sphere, no $\pi$-cycle at a degree-5 hole consists of doubly locked states with $N\le9$.} \lab{open} It concerns a single orbit, as rigid isolation does, and rigid isolation is its case $N=8$. It fails in non-planar graphs that satisfy the chain-parity law (Section~\ref{sec:parity}). On the sphere, such a cycle has length at least 10; the longest run of such states along a $\pi$-orbit in the frame census is 8, at order 33, and this maximum has grown by about one every one or two orders. \lab{computed}

\textbf{N1}, a counting form of NRC. Let the \emph{excess} of a graph with a degree-5 hole be $e=|E(T-v)|-(3n-11)$, which is $0$ on a triangulated sphere. N1 says: \emph{a $\pi$-cycle of doubly locked states with $N\le9$, along which the chain-parity law holds, has $e\ge1$}. With Theorem~\ref{thm:law}, N1 implies NRC. Its status is computational \lab{computed}. Cycles of this kind exist in graphs that are not sphere triangulations, in eight lineages seeded from triangulations of the sphere, the torus, the Klein bottle and the projective plane, always with $e\ge1$. For three minimal ones, two exact solvers prove that no graph carrying the same cycle has $e=0$, with matching linear-programming bounds; $e=1$ is attained. Attempts by Euler counting and by linear-programming duality have not proved N1. No dual certificate uniform along the orbit exists, and certificates fitted to particular cycles do not generalise, which suggests that N1 and NRC need an input at the level of components or Jordan curves, as rigid isolation did.

Four narrower targets remain. \lab{open}
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{G66.} At a degree-5 vertex whose five neighbours have degree 6, show that no $\pi$-orbit is frozen, or at least that every Kempe class has a filled state. Some members of $\mathcal F$ (duals of fullerenes with isolated pentagons) have only such holes \lab{hand}, so this pattern is unavoidable, and the neighbourhood of radius 2 forces nothing \lab{hand}. No frozen orbit was found at these holes of the duals from $C_{60}$ to $C_{100}$, exhaustively, or in adversarial searches, but the longest doubly locked run grows with size \lab{computed}, exploratory.
\item \emph{BudgetUnionPos.} The inequality B$'$ restricted to groups containing a positive orbit implies the quarter floor at the hole (\texttt{quarterFloor\_of\_budget\_pos}) \lab{built}, hence that the vertex is clean. It has no known failure: its least slack is 42 on the census of orders 25--27, and a targeted search came within 8 \lab{computed}, exploratory. It repackages the floor; it is not easier.
\item \emph{No all-interior $\pi$-cycle} (Section~\ref{sec:neg}): if no $\pi$-cycle consists of interior states, the greedy rule fills every state. No counterexample is known, on or off the frame class.
\item \emph{F2 in Lean:} the reduction of separating 4-cycles, which would make the Lean frame class exactly the class with no appearance of either configuration.
\end{itemize}
