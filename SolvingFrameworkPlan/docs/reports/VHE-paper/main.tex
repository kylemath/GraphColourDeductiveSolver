\begin{abstract}This paper, its proofs, its Lean formalisation and its computations were produced by AI agents (Claude models) under the direction of the author; Section~\ref{sec:disclosure} describes who did what and how it was checked. % Abstract after its first sentence: drafted by SquireTeamSevern; states only what the ledger supports.
We record what is solid in one route to the Four Colour Theorem, the vacancy induction. Delete a degree-5 vertex, add a legal fan of two chords, colour the smaller triangulation by induction, and try to complete the colouring by singleton slides and Kempe swaps. The theorem then reduces to one hypothesis, VH$\exists$: some degree-5 vertex and fan work for every inductive colouring. We give the reduction as a hand proof; VH$\exists$ itself is open. We list the results compiled in Lean~4 (short fills, a three-move obstruction, degree-five mobility, lifts across clique separators, and results on a two-pole belt family), hand results on the shape of a least failure (among them that a degree-5 vertex on a separating triangle gives a good pair, and a bound of three swaps at icosahedral holes), negative results (lock persistence is false, and on a 4-connected 17-vertex triangulation some states at one hole need four Kempe swaps), finite evidence on orders 12 to 24 stated for those graphs only, and open problems. Every claim carries a label saying how it was checked.\end{abstract}

% Sources: LT/swarm/hole-induction.md ; LT/swarm/vh-exists.md (Math-accepted: Theorem A, Lemmas 3.1, 3.2;

This section states the route. It fixes the definitions used in the rest of the paper.

\subsection{Triangulations, links and fans}
A \emph{spherical triangulation} is a simple graph $T$ on $n\ge 4$ vertices with a fixed embedding in the sphere in which every face is a triangle. Then $|E(T)|=3n-6$. Colours are $\{0,1,2,3\}$, and a \emph{colouring} always means a proper colouring with these four colours.

Let $v$ have degree $d$, with neighbours $x_0,\dots,x_{d-1}$ in rotation order. The faces at $v$ are the triangles $v\,x_j\,x_{j+1}$, so the neighbours form a cycle $C_v$, the \emph{link} of $v$ (indices mod $d$). An edge of $T$ joining two non-consecutive link vertices is an \emph{existing chord}. Existing chords lie on the far side of $C_v$ and so do not cross. \lab{hand}
% vh-exists.md Lemma 1.1.

Now let $d=5$. A \emph{chord} is a pair $x_ix_{i+2}$ (indices mod 5); there are five. The \emph{fan} with \emph{apex} $x_i$ is
\[\tau_i=\{x_ix_{i+2},\;x_ix_{i+3}\}.\]
The fan is \emph{legal} at $v$ if neither chord is an edge of $T$. For a legal fan, $T^\ast_\tau=(T-v)+\tau$ is $T-v$ with the two chords drawn inside the pentagonal face left by $v$.

\begin{lemma}[fans; \lab{hand}]\label{lem:fans}
Let $v$ have degree $5$.
(a) A set of pairwise non-crossing chords has at most two elements, and a two-element set is a fan.
(b) $\tau_i\cap\tau_{i+1}=\emptyset$.
(c) Some fan is legal at $v$.
(d) For a legal fan $\tau$, $T^\ast_\tau$ is a spherical triangulation on $n-1$ vertices, with $V(T^\ast_\tau)=V(T-v)$ and $E(T^\ast_\tau)=E(T-v)\sqcup\tau$.
(e) In $T^\ast_{\tau_i}$ the link vertices $x_{i+1}$ and $x_{i+4}$ receive no chord, so their degrees drop by one.
\end{lemma}
% vh-exists.md Lemma 1.2 (a)-(e), with its correction to hole-induction.md line 63 (a fan covers three link vertices).
\begin{proof}[Sketch]
Chords cross exactly when they share no endpoint, so the compatible pairs are the fans. The existing chords lie in some $\tau_j$, and $\tau_{j+1}$ avoids them. The edge count is $3n-6-5+2=3(n-1)-6$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{States, moves and fills}
Fix a spherical triangulation $T$.
\begin{itemize}
\item A \emph{state} is a pair $(h,c)$: a vertex $h$, the \emph{hole}, and a colouring $c$ of $T-h$.
\item A \emph{Kempe swap} at $(h,c)$ chooses colours $a\ne b$ and one connected component $K$ of the subgraph of $T-h$ induced by the vertices coloured $a$ or $b$, and exchanges $a$ and $b$ on $K$. The hole does not change. Components of one vertex are allowed.
\item A \emph{singleton slide} at $(h,c)$ chooses a neighbour $u$ of $h$ whose colour $\alpha=c(u)$ occurs exactly once on the link of $h$. It colours $h$ with $\alpha$ and erases the colour of $u$. The new state is $(u,c')$.
\item A state is \emph{filled} if the link of its hole uses at most three colours. Write $F(T)$ for the set of filled states.
\item The \emph{move graph} $M(T)$ has the states as vertices and one edge for each move. A path in $M(T)$ is \emph{mixed}; a path of Kempe swaps only is \emph{pure} (its hole never moves).
\end{itemize}
Both moves send states to states, and each move is undone by a move of the same kind. A filled state $(h,c)$ gives a colouring of $T$: colour $h$ with a colour missing from its link. \lab{hand}
% vh-exists.md Lemma 1.3.
We write $\ell(s)$ for the least length of a mixed path from a state $s$ to $F(T)$, and $\kappa(s)$ for the least length of a pure path. Both may be infinite.
% Notation l and kappa: START-HERE.md section 6 (WP19), LT/wp19/beyond-short-fill.md.

\subsection{The hypothesis}
For a degree-5 vertex $v$ and a legal fan $\tau$, the \emph{starts} are the colourings $c$ of $T-v$ with $c(x)\ne c(y)$ for each chord $xy\in\tau$. By Lemma~\ref{lem:fans}(d) the starts are exactly the colourings of $T^\ast_\tau$.
\begin{itemize}
\item $\mathrm{VH}(v,\tau)$: every start $c$ has $(v,c)$ joined to $F(T)$ in $M(T)$. Such a pair $(v,\tau)$ is called \emph{good}.
\item $\mathrm{VH}^\exists(T)$: some degree-5 vertex $v$ and some legal fan $\tau$ at $v$ form a good pair.
\item \textbf{VH$\exists$}: $\mathrm{VH}^\exists(T)$ holds for every spherical triangulation $T$ of minimum degree 5.
\end{itemize}
% vh-exists.md section 1.3. Quantifier order from REP/IndependentWP18AndBeltReview.md as quoted in vh-exists.md.
The vertex and the fan are chosen \emph{before} the colouring. The path may depend on the start, it may move the hole, and the filled hole may have any degree. A \emph{failure} of VH$\exists$ is a triangulation $T$ of minimum degree 5 with no good pair. VH$\exists$ is \lab{open}.
% START-HERE.md section 1: "VH-exists itself is open". Ledger: structural-vacancy exploring; structural-math-t1 unstarted.

\begin{theorem}[VH$\exists$ implies 4-colourability; \lab{hand}]\label{thm:A}
If VH$\exists$ holds, every spherical triangulation has a colouring with four colours.
\end{theorem}
% vh-exists.md Theorem A; accepted by Math (START-HERE.md section 6, "VH-exists => 4-colourability, with the containment and apex-singleton lemmas").
\begin{proof}
Induction on $n$. For $n=4$, $T=K_4$. For $n\ge5$, since $\sum_v(6-\deg v)=12$, some vertex has degree at most $5$, and every vertex has degree at least $3$. Choose $v$ of minimum degree $d$.

If $d=3$, $T-v$ is a spherical triangulation on $n-1$ vertices. Colour it by induction; the link of $v$ is a triangle, so a colour is free for $v$.

If $d=4$, add an absent diagonal of the link, colour by induction and restrict to $T-v$. If the link uses four colours, one Kempe swap in $T-v$ frees a colour. Either the two vertices of one opposite pair lie in different components of their two-colour subgraph, and we swap one of them; or a two-colour path joins them, and closed through $v$ it is a Jordan curve that separates the other pair, which then lie in different components. This is the classical degree-4 step.

If $d=5$, $T$ has minimum degree 5. By $\mathrm{VH}^\exists(T)$ fix a good pair $(v_0,\tau)$. Colour $T^\ast_\tau$ by induction. Its colouring is a start at $(v_0,\tau)$, so it is joined in $M(T)$ to a filled state, which gives a colouring of $T$.
\end{proof}
The hypothesis is applied once, to $T$; it is not proved by the induction. The inductive call is on $T^\ast_\tau$, which has $n-1$ vertices. Applying the hypothesis, or the inductive claim, to a later state of the same $T$ would be circular, because that state has the same order. An induction restricted to minimum degree 5 does not close: $T^\ast_\tau$ has two vertices whose degree dropped (Lemma~\ref{lem:fans}(e)), and on the icosahedron this leaves the class at every pair. \lab{hand}
% hole-induction.md "The circular variant"; vh-exists.md Remark 2.1.
Every spherical triangulation on at most $11$ vertices has a vertex of degree at most $4$, so orders up to $11$ are coloured without the hypothesis; the icosahedron is the first place it is used. \lab{hand}
% hole-induction.md "Counting": n_5 >= 12, so minimum degree 5 forces n >= 12.

The induction uses the colouring of $T^\ast_\tau$ only to know that a start exists, so the content of VH$\exists$ is that \emph{every} start reaches a fill; letting the pair depend on all the inductive colourings at once gives an equivalent statement. \lab{hand}
% vh-exists.md Remarks 2.2 and 2.3.

\subsection{Two lemmas}
\begin{lemma}[containment; \lab{hand}]\label{lem:containment}
Let $\tau$ be legal at $v$. If two starts are joined by Kempe swaps of $T^\ast_\tau$, then they are joined by Kempe swaps of $T-v$. Hence whether $(v,c)$ reaches $F(T)$ is constant on each Kempe class of $T^\ast_\tau$.
\end{lemma}
% vh-exists.md Lemma 3.1.
\begin{proof}
$T-v$ is a spanning subgraph of $T^\ast_\tau$, so a two-colour component $K$ of $T^\ast_\tau$ is a disjoint union of two-colour components $K_1,\dots,K_r$ of $T-v$. Swapping $K_1$, then $K_2$, and so on, never changes the set of vertices coloured with the two colours, so each $K_j$ is still a component when its turn comes.
\end{proof}
The converse fails: a swap of $T-v$ can make a chord monochromatic.

\begin{lemma}[apex singleton; \lab{hand}]\label{lem:apex}
If $c$ is a start for $\tau_i$, then $c(x_i)$ occurs exactly once on the link of $v$. So the slide from $v$ to the apex is legal at every start. Moreover $(v,c)$ is filled if and only if $c(x_{i+1})=c(x_{i+3})$ and $c(x_{i+2})=c(x_{i+4})$.
\end{lemma}
% vh-exists.md Lemma 3.2 and Corollary 3.3.
\begin{proof}
$x_i$ differs in colour from its two link neighbours (edges of $T$) and from the two chord ends (the start is proper on $\tau_i$). For the second claim, the other four link vertices form a properly coloured path that must use at most two colours.
\end{proof}
By Lemma~\ref{lem:containment}, $\mathrm{VH}(v,\tau)$ holds if and only if every Kempe class of $T^\ast_\tau$ contains one colouring that reaches $F(T)$. \lab{hand}
% vh-exists.md R1.

\paragraph{Contraction.} For the apex fan $\tau_y$ at a degree-5 vertex $x$ with apex neighbour $y$, $T^\ast_{\tau_y}$ is the contraction $T/xy$, and the fan is legal exactly when no separating triangle contains $xy$. If, for a given start, some sequence of Kempe changes in $T-xy$ (with $x$ coloured like $y$) separates the colours of $x$ and $y$, that start has a pure fill at $x$. This links VH$\exists$ to Tilley's Kempe-locking~\cite{tilley2018}. \lab{hand}
% WK/creative-intel-2026-10-05/literature-check.md "A bridge we had not noticed"; tilley-separability.md section 7.
% Ledger: structural-tilley-bridge = proved (Math 20:42, REP/MathCreativeCatchUpReview.md).

\paragraph{Why slides are allowed.} The proofs of the Four Colour Theorem that close show that a minimal counterexample contains no reducible configuration from a large unavoidable set, in the line of Birkhoff~\cite{birkhoff1913,appel1977,robertson1997}. The proof of Robertson, Sanders, Seymour and Thomas was formally verified in Coq~\cite{gonthier}, and a recent paper finds linearly many reducible configurations~\cite{inoue2026}. \lab{cited}
% vh-exists.md section 4.5; literature-check.md sections 1 and 'Sources'; Gonthier (2005) and RSST from repo background notes (grep). VERIFY all.
 Kempe's argument at a degree-5 vertex performs two swaps at once, and Heawood showed that the second chain can be blocked~\cite{kempe1879,heawood1890}. \lab{cited} VH$\exists$ uses the single configuration ``one degree-5 vertex'' and gets past that objection only by allowing unboundedly long, non-local paths: Kempe chains anywhere, and slides that leave the link.
% vh-exists.md section 4.5 "Remark on the classical route"; repo summary "Kempe (1879) performed two chain swaps at once, and Heawood (1890) showed that the second chain..." (grep of SolvingFrameworkPlan, background notes).
Reading the hole as a fifth colour used once, slides are exactly the two-colour swaps whose component is one edge, so VH is Meyniel's theorem (all 5-colourings of a planar graph are Kempe-equivalent) restricted to the colourings that use the fifth colour at most once, and Meyniel's paths leave that set~\cite{meyniel1978}. \lab{hand}

\subsection{The move framework}
The vacancy modules work on an arbitrary simple graph \texttt{G : SimpleGraph V} and an arbitrary colour type, not only on planar graphs or four colours. \texttt{ProperOff G h c} says that $c$ is proper on $G-h$, and \texttt{Target G h c} that a colour is missing from the neighbours of $h$. \texttt{KempeStep} exchanges two colours on exactly one whole component of their two-colour subgraph of $G-h$; \texttt{slide} is the singleton slide; \texttt{MixedPath G n s t} is a path of $n$ moves; \texttt{PureFill G h c n} is a pure path of length at most $n$ at the fixed hole to a \texttt{Target} state.
% LEAN/VacancySlide.lean (ProperOff, UniqueAt), LEAN/VacancyShortFill.lean (Target, Whole, KempeStep, MixedPath, PureFill).

\subsection{Short fills and the three-move obstruction}
\begin{theorem}[short fills, M3; \lab{compiled}]\label{thm:M3}
On any simple graph and any colour type, a mixed path of length $n\le2$ from a state proper off its hole to a filled state gives a pure fill of length at most $n$ at the original hole. If the least mixed length is at most $2$, the least pure length equals it.
\end{theorem}
% Lean: SimpleGraph.VacancyShortFill.short_fill and optimal_short (LEAN/VacancyShortFill.lean lines 419, 461). REP/ShortFillLeanReport.md.
Lean names: \texttt{SimpleGraph.VacancyShortFill.short\_fill} and \texttt{optimal\_short}. So $\ell\le2$ implies $\kappa=\ell$.

\begin{theorem}[three-move obstruction, L3; \lab{compiled}]\label{thm:L3}
Suppose a state has no pure fill within $3$ swaps but has a mixed fill of length $3$. Then (a) that path starts with a singleton slide $h\to u$; and (b) after the slide, the first swap of any two-swap fill at $u$ uses a colour pair containing the slid colour $c(u)$.
\end{theorem}
% Lean: SimpleGraph.VacancyThreeMoveObstruction.first_is_slide, first_pair_contains_slide_colour. REP/ThreeMoveLeanReport.md.
Lean names, in namespace \texttt{SimpleGraph.VacancyThreeMoveObstruction}: \texttt{first\_is\_slide} and \texttt{first\_pair\_contains\_slide\_colour}. The premise is \texttt{$\lnot$ PureFill G h c 3}.

\begin{theorem}[Lemma L4; \lab{compiled}]\label{thm:L4}
In the setting of Theorem~\ref{thm:L3}, write $\sigma=c(u)$ and let the first swap after the slide be a whole $\{\sigma,\rho\}$-component $S$.
(a) If $h\notin S$, then $S$ contains no neighbour of $h$, $S$ contains a $\rho$-neighbour of $u$, and $u$ has a $\rho$-neighbour $y\notin S$ joined in the slid state by a $\{\sigma,\rho\}$-path to a $\rho$-neighbour of $h$.
(b) If $h\in S$ and $u$ has no $\rho$-neighbour in $S$, there is a pure fill of length $r$, where $r$ is the number of $\{\sigma,\rho\}$-components of $G-h$ that contain a $\rho$-neighbour of $h$, and $r\le m_\rho$, the number of $\rho$-neighbours of $h$.
(c) If there is no pure fill of length $\max(3,m_\rho)$, then $u$ has a $\rho$-neighbour in the $\{\sigma,\rho\}$-component of $h$ in the slid state.
\end{theorem}

\subsection{Separating triangles}
\begin{lemma}[connectivity core; \lab{hand}]
A spherical triangulation of order at least $5$ has no separating triangle if and only if it is 4-connected.
\end{lemma}
% REP/MathInteriorWitnessReview.md "Accepted connectivity core". The spanning-tree paragraph (section C of the source) is not part of the accepted core (START-HERE.md section 6).

\begin{theorem}[interior-witness lift; \lab{hand}]
Let $T$ have a separating triangle with sides $A$ and $B$, and let a degree-5 vertex strictly inside $A$, with a legal fan, be chosen before the colouring. If every start admitted by the fan has a fill path in $A$ (completed by its facial triangle) whose every hole stays strictly off the triangle, then the pair is good in $T$. So a least failure has no separating triangle with such an interior witness.
\end{theorem}
% REP/MathInteriorWitnessReview.md "Accepted interior lift". Compiled graph-level core: Theorem \ref{thm:lift}.
This does not prove that a least failure is 4-connected. Kempe components restrict correctly even when the hole is on the triangle; what fails there is that the hole's neighbourhood in $T$ is larger than in the side, both for slide legality and for the fill.

\begin{theorem}[fixed hole on a separating triangle; \lab{hand}]\label{thm:fixedhole}
Let $f$ be a degree-5 vertex on a separating triangle of $T$. Every colouring of $T-f$ fills the fixed hole $f$ within two whole-component Kempe swaps.
\end{theorem}
% REP/MathFixedHoleReview.md "The accepted theorem". Ledger structural-fixed-hole-triangle = proved.
\begin{theorem}[Math's corollary; \lab{hand}]
If a triangulation of minimum degree 5 has a degree-5 vertex on a separating triangle, it satisfies $\mathrm{VH}^\exists(T)$. Hence in any failure of VH$\exists$, every vertex of every separating triangle has degree at least $6$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Sketch]
The link of $f$ is $(p,a,q,b_2,b_1)$, with $pq$ the interface edge and $a$ on the other side from $b_1,b_2$. The fan with apex $a$ has chords $ab_2,ab_1$, which are absent because their ends lie on opposite sides of the separator. Theorem~\ref{thm:fixedhole} covers every colouring, in particular every start of this fan.
\end{proof}

Let $f$ have degree $6$ and lie on a separating triangle $\{f,p,q\}$, with side degrees $4$ and $4$. Every colouring of $T-f$ fills the fixed hole within three Kempe swaps, and the colours of $p,q$ are preserved.
\end{theorem}

\paragraph{Locks and radius.} Let $s$ be an unfilled state at a degree-5 hole $v$. Its link uses four colours with one repeat, at $x_j$ and $x_{j+2}$; rotate so that $j=0$ and the link reads $(\alpha,\beta,\alpha,\gamma,\delta)$. \subsection{The edge-colouring view of a lock}\label{sec:tait}
Identify the colours with $\mathbb Z_2\times\mathbb Z_2$, and give each edge $uw$ of $T-v$ the colour $c(u)+c(w)\neq0$. Its dual is then a proper 3-edge-colouring (a Tait colouring) of the dual graph $H$ of $T-v$, in which every node except the pentagon node $P$ meets one edge of each colour; a vertex colouring is recovered from it up to translation. \lab{cited}~\cite{tait1880,saatykainen}
% LT/explore-vhphi/pathways/pd2_lock_proof.md "Notation (Tait)"; WK/creative-intel-2026-10-05/pathway-D.md section 1 and line 31 (dictionary [cited] per the audit's 12:08 notes; ledger structural-pathway-d note rev 116).
Let the link of the unfilled state $s$ read $(\alpha,\mu,\alpha,c(a),c(b))$ at $x_j,\dots,x_{j+4}$, with $m=x_{j+1}$, $a=x_{j+3}$, $b=x_{j+4}$. Put $\beta=\alpha+\mu$, $\gamma=\alpha+c(a)$, $\delta=\alpha+c(b)$, and let $e_t$ be the edge of $P$ dual to $x_tx_{t+1}$; then $e_j,e_{j+1},e_{j+3}$ have colour $\beta$, $e_{j+2}$ has $\gamma$ and $e_{j+4}$ has $\delta$.
\begin{lemma}[Tait lock criterion; \lab{hand}, not yet reviewed]\label{lem:tait}
Let $Z_1$ be the $(\beta,\gamma)$-path of $H$ that leaves $P$ by $e_{j+2}$, and $Z_2$ the $(\beta,\delta)$-path that leaves $P$ by $e_{j+4}$. Then the lock from $m$ to $a$ holds if and only if $Z_1$ returns to $P$ by $e_{j+1}$, and the lock from $m$ to $b$ holds if and only if $Z_2$ returns by $e_j$; otherwise each returns by $e_{j+3}$. Consequently, if $s$ has the lock from $m$ to $b$, then $F(s)$ has its first lock (from its middle vertex to its $a$-vertex), because $F$ leaves the $(\beta,\delta)$ 2-factor unchanged.
\end{lemma}
% LT/explore-vhphi/pathways/pd2_lock_proof.md (Claim and proof); MSG/..._1239_longtable_..._pathway-D-lock-criterion-hand-proof-variant-X-killed.md items 1 and Corollary (commit f2e3ae7).
% Ledger rev 119: "Tait lock criterion and its corollary: [hand], by a Long Table sub-agent, read by the lead, not yet reviewed by Math".
% Code re-check (not part of the proof): 0 mismatches on 3,682 states (T4, A_3, A_4).
The proof is a Jordan-curve argument in the dual: $Z_1$ closed through $P$ crosses no $\delta$-edge, so it separates $m$ from $a$ exactly when the lock fails. It was written by a Long Table sub-agent and read step by step by Long Table; Math has not yet reviewed it. So whether $F$ keeps both locks reduces to a single chain condition.

\subsection{Lock persistence (Conjecture L)}
For a doubly locked state $s$ (Section~\ref{sec:failure}), let $F(s)$ swap the $\{\alpha,\gamma\}$-component of $x_2$, and let the \emph{chain length} of $s$ be the largest $k$ with $s,F(s),\dots,F^{k-1}(s)$ all doubly locked. Conjecture~L asserted an absolute bound on chain length, which would have made every degree-5 vertex clean.
% l-attack.md section 1 (definitions of F and chain length, with x_j repeat index); MathConjectureL.md via ledger structural-math-t1 note rev 99.
\begin{theorem}[Conjecture L is false; \lab{computed}]
(a) A plane triangulation on 20 vertices (degrees 3 to 8) has a doubly locked state at a degree-5 hole with chain length exactly $6$ (certificate W6).
(b) On the 5-fold symmetric triangulation $A_3$ (17 vertices, twelve of degree 5 and five of degree 6) some states have an $F$-orbit of period exactly $60$, all doubly locked, so the chain is infinite. The same holds on $A_4$ and $A_5$.
\end{theorem}
% l-attack.md section 0 items 1-2; audit replay MSG/..._0926_audit_..._conjecture-L-refutation-replayed.md items 1-3 and
% LT/audit/conjecture-L/REPORT.md; ledger structural-conj-l-refuted = killed ("confirmed by three independent checks").
W6 and $A_3$ were checked by Long Table's two programs, by Math's checker, and by the audit's own code, which also confirmed $A_4$ and $A_5$ by exhaustive enumeration. Since $A_3$--$A_5$ have minimum degree 5, L is false in the class the induction uses. Math's pre-registered search separately found 22 certified chains of length 6 to 10 on general triangulations, all with vertices of degree 3. \lab{computed}
% MSG/..._0919_math_..._conjecture-L-falsified-as-stated.md (22 certificates, longest 10, all with degree-3 vertices).

None of these refutes VH$\exists$. Every state on an infinite chain still fills: its Kempe radius is 2 or 3 on $A_3$, and 2 on the infinite-chain states of $A_4$ and $A_5$; W6's state has radius 2. \lab{computed}
% Audit 0926 item 4.
\emph{Lesson.} Locks persist, but a fill does not need the lock to break along the $F$-orbit: the Kempe class is larger than the orbit.

\paragraph{The family $A_r$.} Two lemmas of Long Table are accepted by hand after Math's independent review: $F$ and the lock test commute with the 5-fold rotation and with colour renaming (Lemma~A), and an orbit is infinite once four lock checks hold and $F^4s=\pi\circ s\circ\sigma$ for a rotation $\sigma$ and a colour permutation $\pi$ (Lemma~B). \lab{hand} The number of infinite-chain classes for $r=3,\dots,9$ is $20,20,60,100,220,420,860$, which is $20$ times a Jacobsthal number. \lab{computed} That this continues for every $r$ (Conjecture~J) is \lab{open}.

\subsection{Radius at most 3 (T4)}
The data on $A_3$--$A_5$ suggested that every doubly locked state has Kempe radius at most $3$. This is false.
\begin{theorem}[\lab{computed}]
Let $T_4$ be the 17-vertex triangulation of \texttt{MathConjectureR.md} (twelve vertices of degree 5, five of degree 6, 30 triangles, all of them faces). At hole 4, the 68 states up to colour renaming have Kempe radii $\{0{:}22,\ 1{:}25,\ 2{:}15,\ 3{:}4,\ 4{:}2\}$. So two doubly locked states have radius $4$.
\end{theorem}

% audit reproduction: MSG/..._1200_audit_..._P-E-on-P-B-and-link-class-kill.md item 1 ("exactly Math's numbers"). Ledger structural-conj-r-radius3-killed = killed.
$T_4$ has no separating triangle, so it is 4-connected and lies in the core class. It is one edge flip from $A_3$. \emph{Lesson.} The hypothesis of Theorem~\ref{thm:H} cannot simply be dropped: at hole 4 of $T_4$ two adjacent link vertices have degree 6, and the radius is 4.

\section{Open problems}\label{sec:open}
Everything in this section is \lab{open}.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{VH$\exists$.} Every spherical triangulation of minimum degree 5 has a degree-5 vertex and a legal fan such that every start reaches a filled state by slides and Kempe swaps. With Theorem~\ref{thm:A}, this would prove the Four Colour Theorem.
% START-HERE.md sections 1 and 6 "Open".
\item \textbf{U$\exists$.} Every triangulation of minimum degree 5 has a pair at which every Kempe class of $T^\ast_\tau$ contains a state that is filled or is filled by one swap of $T-v$. It implies VH$\exists$ with pure paths and is the degree-5 analogue of the degree-4 step. It passed on every graph of orders 21--24 (WP19).
% vh-exists.md section 5 item 12 and "Most promising"; REP/MathWP19Results.md.
\item \textbf{Conjecture R.} There is an absolute $R$ such that every doubly locked state at a degree-5 hole of a triangulation has Kempe radius at most $R$. By $T_4$, $R\ge4$. Its finite form, that no Kempe class at a degree-5 hole is targetless, is equivalent to every degree-5 hole being clean. Theorem~\ref{thm:H} proves $R\le3$ at icosahedral holes. Kempe classes of planar 4-colourings need not be unique, so R is a statement beyond the Four Colour Theorem.
% l-attack.md section 3 "Conjecture R"; WK/MathConjectureR.md sections 1 and 5 (the non-uniqueness of Kempe classes is "cited from memory, not rechecked" there).
% VERIFY: non-uniqueness of Kempe classes (Fisk-type examples; Mohar and Salas) -- literature-check.md section 3 summarises Mohar-Salas only for three-colourable triangulations.
\item \textbf{Four-connectivity of a plain failure.} Is a least failure of VH$\exists$ 4-connected? It is for the face-avoiding form (Section~\ref{sec:failure}). The trace-game hypotheses, for separating 4-cycles and for separating 5-cycles, are the missing step for a stronger reduction.
% Ledger structural-math-t2 (unstarted), structural-math-t2-attack (exploring); START-HERE.md section 6 "Open".
\item \textbf{D1 and statement (N).} D1 (every unfilled degree-5 state is separable for some admitting fan, or one swap from such a state) and statement (N) in its rigid case.
% Ledger structural-sep-d1, structural-math-t3 (unstarted).
\item \textbf{Bounded fill length.} Whether $m(T)$ is bounded (no graph with $m\ge4$ is known), and whether every start has $\ell\le4$ (M1). On general graphs $\kappa$ is unbounded in $\ell$, so a bound must use planarity with four colours.
% START-HERE.md section 6 "Open"; ledger structural-long-fill note.
\item \textbf{Conjecture J} on the family $A_r$, and the claim that its doubly locked states have radius 2 for every $r\ge4$.
% MSG/..._1102_math_... items 2 and 5.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Directions.} On 6 October the work turned from search to proof ideas~(``pathways''), each tested first against small adversarial examples.

\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Good neighbourhoods plus discharging (P-A).} Prove a fill bound for every degree-5 hole whose link has four vertices of degree at most 11 and one of arbitrary degree; Section~\ref{sec:failure} shows such holes always exist. Any such lemma must allow radius at least 4 ($T_4$), cannot be local to a ball of fixed radius around the hole, must handle a neighbour of unbounded degree as in the belt, and must survive the $A_r$ family and its one-flip variants.

\item \emph{Edge-colouring view (P-D).} In Tait language the two locks are two two-colour paths through the pentagon node (Lemma~\ref{lem:tait}, \lab{hand}, not yet reviewed). Parity invariants and local rules fail (Section~\ref{sec:neg}), so whether a lock persists depends on colours at distance 3 or more from the hole; what is needed is a global, Jordan-type argument about the two lock paths.
