\section{How this work was produced}\label{sec:disclosure}
\textbf{The mathematics, the Lean proofs, the computations and the text of this paper were produced by AI agents, not by a human.} The author directed the agents; he did not write the proofs, the code or this text, and he has not checked them line by line. The agents were Claude models made by Anthropic, mostly run in Claude Code: long-running sessions that read and wrote files, ran programs on the author's computers, and exchanged dated messages through a shared repository. The repository's commit trailers name the models used: Claude Sonnet~5.5 and Claude Opus~5.5, and in a few commits the agent of the Cursor editor. The models behind the night swarm and behind the pre-October agent rounds were not recorded. Three images committed in May 2026 were generated by Google's Gemini. Every commit carries Kyle Mathewson as its git author because the agents committed under his identity; the trailers, not the author field, show who wrote each change.

Each session had a fixed role.
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Long Table} proposed ideas, wrote the hand proofs and counterexamples, and designed and ran the declared experiments.
\item \emph{Math} reviewed hand proofs, accepted or refused results, wrote the Lean~4 formalisation and ran its module audit.
\item \emph{Audit} reran results with its own independently written code and read every claim adversarially.
\item \emph{Navigator} kept the status ledger and was the only session allowed to call a result proved, compiled, exploring or killed.
\item A \emph{coordinator} session sequenced work between the teams.
\item A \emph{night swarm} of overnight exploratory agents produced further notes and computations. These were checked less carefully than anything else here and are used only as leads.
\end{itemize}
Sessions also delegated sub-tasks to short-lived sub-agents, for example to write a checker for an experiment without seeing the program it checks.

\textbf{What the author did.} Kyle Mathewson is responsible for the claims. Everything he did is in the public record, either as his own commits and messages or as statements of his quoted by the agents, and \texttt{AUTHOR-RECORD.md} beside this paper lists each item with its source. In summary:
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Set up the project.} He created the repository in May 2026. Before October he ran earlier rounds of numbered AI agents, which wrote strategy reports, the first Proof Navigator and a graph-colouring solver. In October he started the team sessions and gave each one its role.
\item \emph{Set the direction.} He set the goal of a new, deductive route to the Four Colour Theorem. He pushed the teams to explore first and check afterwards, and asked for parallel sub-agent teams. He pointed the agents to the literature on large reducible-configuration sets. On 6 October he redirected the work from brute-force search back to ``intuition and trial and error on novel strategies''.
\item \emph{Set the rules and delegated authority.} He appointed a coordinating session to manage the teams (5 October). He let declared experiments start without his release or the Math team's go-ahead, with the Navigator gating success. He made the coordinator the source of go-aheads for the second machine.
\item \emph{Provided and ran the machines.} He provided a MacBook Pro (Apple M4 Pro) and a Mac Studio (Apple M4 Max), on which every computation ran. He installed and configured the agents on them, approved the permission prompts they raised, approved installing outside software (\texttt{plantri}), and restarted the teams after an overnight reboot. He ran the night swarm and switched the models behind sessions as the work went on.
\item \emph{Decided how the work is published.} He commissioned this paper and the preparation of the Lean library for Mathlib, and decided the authorship, this disclosure and the public link to the record.
\end{itemize}
He did not write the proofs, the code or this text. He did not review the hand proofs, the Lean statements or the computed outputs.

\textbf{How the results were checked, and by whom.} All checking was done by AI sessions or by the Lean kernel; none was done by a human. Every claim carries one label.
\begin{itemize}
\item \lab{compiled}: proved in Lean~4 with no \texttt{sorry} and only Lean's standard axioms, and included in the module audit. The Lean kernel certifies that the formal statement is proved. It does not certify that the formal statement means what the prose in this paper says; that match was checked only by AI sessions, and it is the main remaining risk in trusting a \lab{compiled} result.
\item \lab{hand}: a written proof reviewed by a different AI session. No human referee has checked any of these. AI reviewers can share blind spots, so treat these as unrefereed.
\item \lab{computed}: an experiment whose statement, programs and limits were fixed and hashed before it ran, whose output was checked by a second implementation written separately by another AI session (and in some cases a third, by Audit), and which claims only what happened on the graphs it ran.
\item \lab{cited}: taken from the published literature as summarised by the agents; consult the original.
\item \lab{open}: not claimed.
\end{itemize}
Each label comes from the Navigator's ledger, which is itself maintained by an AI session, and no status in this paper is stronger than that ledger. Mistakes were made along the way, among them wrong timestamps, a mislabelled process and conjectures later refuted. Each was corrected by a dated message rather than erased.

\textbf{The record.} The full record is public at \url{https://github.com/kylemath/GraphColourDeductiveSolver}, and the Lean development at \url{https://github.com/kylemath/mathlib4-planemap}. A tagged release, [\textsc{tag name to be set at posting}], fixes the version this paper describes.
