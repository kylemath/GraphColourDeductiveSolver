\title{The vacancy induction for the Four Colour Theorem:\\ a dated record of what is solid}
\author{Kyle Mathewson}
\date{6 October 2026 (draft outline)}
\begin{document}\maketitle
\begin{abstract}This paper, its proofs, its Lean formalisation and its computations were produced by AI agents (Claude models) under the direction of the author; Section~\ref{sec:disclosure} describes who did what and how it was checked. % Abstract after its first sentence: SquireTeamSevern; states only what the ledger supports.
We summarise one route to the Four Colour Theorem, the vacancy induction, and what is established about it. Delete a degree-5 vertex, add a legal fan of two chords, colour the smaller triangulation by induction, and complete the colouring by singleton slides and Kempe swaps. The theorem reduces to one hypothesis, VH$\exists$: some degree-5 vertex and fan work for every inductive colouring. We give this reduction as a hand proof. The vertex property it needs, that every colouring of the deletion of a degree-5 vertex reaches such a completion by Kempe swaps alone, is, as far as we can tell, Tilley's D-resolvability, and whether every degree-5 vertex is D-resolvable is his published open problem. Our contributions are: a hand reduction from Lemma R$^\ast$ (one D-resolvable degree-5 vertex in every 4-connected triangulation of a relative class) to VH$\exists$ and the Four Colour Theorem, with a Lean form of the reduction; bounds on the Kempe radius at holes whose neighbours have degree 5 (at most 3), or all but one of them (at most 6), and Euler counting lemmas, all compiled in Lean~4 and audited; and an analysis of the cases these do not cover. VH$\exists$ and R$^\ast$ are open, and nothing about the Four Colour Theorem is proved here. We also record the most instructive failures, and the repository that holds the full record.\end{abstract}

\section{The vacancy induction and the hypothesis VH$\exists$}\label{sec:vh}
% Sources: LT/swarm/hole-induction.md ; LT/swarm/vh-exists.md (Theorem A and Lemmas 3.1, 3.2 accepted by Math; START-HERE.md section 6).
A \emph{spherical triangulation} is a simple graph $T$ on $n\ge4$ vertices with a fixed embedding in the sphere in which every face is a triangle. A \emph{colouring} is a proper colouring with the colours $\{0,1,2,3\}$. The neighbours $x_0,\dots,x_{d-1}$ of a vertex $v$ of degree $d$, in rotation order, form a cycle, the \emph{link} of $v$.

\paragraph{Fans.} Let $v$ have degree 5. The \emph{fan} with apex $x_i$ is the pair of chords $\tau_i=\{x_ix_{i+2},x_ix_{i+3}\}$ (indices mod 5). It is \emph{legal} if neither chord is an edge of $T$; some fan is always legal. For a legal fan, $T^\ast_\tau=(T-v)+\tau$ is a spherical triangulation on $n-1$ vertices. \lab{hand}
% vh-exists.md Lemma 1.2.

\paragraph{States and moves.} A \emph{state} $(h,c)$ is a vertex $h$, the \emph{hole}, with a colouring $c$ of $T-h$. A \emph{Kempe swap} exchanges two colours on one connected component of their two-colour subgraph of $T-h$. A \emph{singleton slide} moves the hole to a neighbour $u$ whose colour occurs exactly once on the link of $h$, giving $h$ that colour. A state is \emph{filled} if the link of its hole uses at most three colours; then the hole can be coloured. Both moves are reversible. A path of swaps only is \emph{pure}; its hole never moves. \lab{hand}
% vh-exists.md section 1.2 and Lemma 1.3.

\paragraph{The hypothesis.} The \emph{starts} of a legal pair $(v,\tau)$ are the colourings of $T-v$ that are proper on both chords, that is, the colourings of $T^\ast_\tau$. The pair is \emph{good} if every start reaches a filled state by slides and swaps. \textbf{VH$\exists$} says: every spherical triangulation of minimum degree 5 has a good pair, chosen before the colouring. VH$\exists$ is \lab{open}.
% vh-exists.md section 1.3; quantifier order from REP/IndependentWP18AndBeltReview.md.

\begin{theorem}[VH$\exists$ implies 4-colourability; \lab{hand}]\label{thm:A}
If VH$\exists$ holds, every spherical triangulation has a 4-colouring.
\end{theorem}
% vh-exists.md Theorem A; accepted by Math.
\begin{proof}[Sketch]
Induction on $n$, from $K_4$. Since $\sum_v(6-\deg v)=12$, some vertex $v$ has degree at most 5. Degree 3: colour $T-v$ and give $v$ a free colour. Degree 4: add a missing diagonal of the link, colour, and restrict to $T-v$; if the link uses four colours, one Kempe swap in $T-v$ frees a colour, by the Jordan curve theorem. Minimum degree 5: take a good pair $(v_0,\tau)$, colour $T^\ast_\tau$ by induction; its colouring is a start, which reaches a filled state of $T$.
\end{proof}
The hypothesis is applied once, to $T$; the induction is on $T^\ast_\tau$. An induction confined to minimum degree 5 does not close, because $T^\ast_\tau$ can leave that class. Two lemmas make the hypothesis concrete. \emph{Containment:} two starts in the same Kempe class of $T^\ast_\tau$ are joined by Kempe swaps of $T-v$, so it suffices to check one start per class. \emph{Apex singleton:} at every start the apex colour occurs once on the link, so the slide to the apex is always legal. \lab{hand}
% hole-induction.md "The circular variant"; vh-exists.md Remark 2.1, Lemmas 3.1 and 3.2.

\paragraph{Context.} Kempe's argument at a degree-5 vertex makes two swaps at once, and Heawood showed that the second chain can be blocked~\cite{kempe1879,heawood1890}. The proofs of the Four Colour Theorem show instead that a minimal counterexample contains none of a large unavoidable set of reducible configurations~\cite{birkhoff1913,appel1977,robertson1997}; the proof of Robertson, Sanders, Seymour and Thomas was formally verified in Coq~\cite{gonthier}, and a recent paper finds linearly many reducible configurations~\cite{inoue2026}. \lab{cited} VH$\exists$ uses the single configuration ``one degree-5 vertex'', and gets past Heawood's objection only by allowing long, non-local paths: Kempe chains anywhere, and slides that move the hole.
% vh-exists.md section 4.5; literature-check.md.

\section{The reduction to Lemma R$^\ast$, and what is proved toward it}\label{sec:rstar}
% Sources: WK/MathReviewCleanToVHE.md (links L1-L6); audit re-derivation MSG/..._1325_audit_..._R-star-chain-adversarial-read.md;
% REP/MathTriangleCarryResearch.md, REP/MathVHCoreAdvance.md, REP/MathFixedHoleReview.md; Navigator revisions 119, 122, 130, 131.
\paragraph{Locks and radius.} Let $s$ be an unfilled state at a degree-5 hole $v$. Its link uses four colours with one repeat; rotate so that it reads $(\alpha,\beta,\alpha,\gamma,\delta)$ at $x_0,\dots,x_4$. The state is \emph{doubly locked} if $T-v$ has a $\{\beta,\gamma\}$-path from $x_1$ to $x_3$ and a $\{\beta,\delta\}$-path from $x_1$ to $x_4$; otherwise it fills in one swap. Its \emph{Kempe radius} is the least number of Kempe swaps of $T-v$ that fill it. The vertex $v$ is \emph{clean} if every state at $v$ has finite radius.
% WK/creative-intel-2026-10-05/l-attack.md section 1; WK/MathConjectureR.md section 1.

\paragraph{The relative class.} Let $\mathcal C$ be the class of spherical triangulations with a designated face $\varphi$ in which every vertex off $\varphi$ has degree at least 5. A pair is \emph{$\varphi$-good} if it is good with every hole along every path kept off $\varphi$; swaps may recolour $\varphi$. $\mathrm{VH}_{\mathcal C}$ asks for a $\varphi$-good pair in every member. A least failure of $\mathrm{VH}_{\mathcal C}$ is 4-connected and has order at least 12, and $\mathrm{VH}_{\mathcal C}$ implies VH$\exists$. \lab{hand}
% REP/MathTriangleCarryResearch.md (triangle reduction); REP/MathVHCoreAdvance.md items 5 (order >= 12; Navigator revision 120 ruling 3); ledger structural-face-reduction.

The property asked of $v$ in the next statement, that every colouring of $T-v$ reaches a filled state by Kempe swaps of $T-v$, is, as far as we can tell, what Tilley calls \emph{D-resolvability} of $v$. Tilley proved that a degree-5 vertex is not D-reducible and left open whether every degree-5 vertex of a planar graph is D-resolvable, a statement stronger than 4-colourability~\cite{tilley2017}. \lab{cited} Lemma R$^\ast$ asks for one D-resolvable degree-5 vertex in each core triangulation, so it is not our own conjecture: Tilley's open problem implies it.
% Tilley: MSG/..._1514_longtable_..._CORRECTION-R-star-is-Tilley-D-resolvability.md (abstract verified by Long Table; full-text definition match reported by a sub-agent, audit check pending). Coordinator: say 'as far as we can tell'.
\begin{conjecture}[Lemma R$^\ast$; \lab{open}]
In every 4-connected member of $\mathcal C$, some degree-5 vertex off $\varphi$ has every doubly locked state reach a filled state by finitely many Kempe swaps.
\end{conjecture}
\begin{theorem}[\lab{hand}]\label{thm:chain}
Lemma R$^\ast$ implies $\mathrm{VH}_{\mathcal C}$, hence VH$\exists$, hence (Theorem~\ref{thm:A}) 4-colourability.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}[Sketch]
Finite radius at every doubly locked state makes the vertex clean, since a state that is not doubly locked fills in one swap. A clean vertex off $\varphi$ has a $\varphi$-good fan, because its paths are pure and the hole never moves. Take a least failure of $\mathrm{VH}_{\mathcal C}$: it is 4-connected of order at least 12, so Lemma R$^\ast$ gives a clean degree-5 vertex off $\varphi$, hence a $\varphi$-good pair, a contradiction.
% Audit claim read C2 (MSG/..._1502_audit_...): the chain does not use the Euler lemma. Finally $\mathrm{VH}_{\mathcal C}$ implies VH$\exists$ by lifting good pairs across separating triangles.
\end{proof}
% Links L3-L6 of MathReviewCleanToVHE.md; L1 (Theorem A of MathConfinementAttack) and L2 (Corollary B) explain why clean-vertex existence is necessary.
% Navigator revision 122: structural-chain-rstar = proved as a conditional theorem.
The six links of this chain were reviewed by a Math sub-agent and re-derived independently by the audit. \textbf{Nothing about the Four Colour Theorem is proved by this.} Lemma R$^\ast$ is open, and it is at least as strong as VH$\exists$, not weaker. Our contribution here is the reduction, not the statement of R$^\ast$.

\paragraph{What is proved toward R$^\ast$.}
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Theorem H.} If $v$ and its five neighbours have degree 5 and no separating triangle passes through $v$, every doubly locked state at $v$ has radius at most 3.
% WK/MathRadiusGeometry.md (with erratum); ledger structural-theorem-h, structural-theorem-h-lean.
\item \emph{Theorem HP.} If $v$ has degree 5, four of its neighbours have degree 5, the fifth has any degree, and no separating triangle passes through $v$, every doubly locked state at $v$ has radius at most 6, so $v$ is clean.
% Ledger structural-theorem-hp, structural-theorem-hp-lean (revision 131). The order-22 example on its source page is not cited (no face list).
\item \emph{Euler lemma.} Every triangulation of minimum degree 5 has at least 12 degree-5 vertices with at most one neighbour of degree $\ge12$; in the relative class, at least $9-n_4$ such vertices lie off $\varphi$, where $n_4\le2$ is the number of degree-4 vertices on $\varphi$.
% MSG/..._1240_audit_..._counting-lemma.md; MSG/..._1233_math_..._Euler-lemma-accepted-strengthened.md; WK/MathReviewCleanToVHE.md section 6 (relative repair).
\item \emph{Separating triangles.} A degree-5 vertex on a separating triangle fills within two Kempe swaps from every colouring, so it gives a good pair; in any failure of VH$\exists$ every vertex of a separating triangle has degree at least 6.
% REP/MathFixedHoleReview.md; ledger structural-fixed-hole-triangle.
\end{itemize}
In the 4-connected setting, Theorems H and HP give finite radius at every degree-5 vertex with at most one neighbour of degree other than 5; this includes every degree-5 vertex of Florek's two-pole graphs. A fill lemma for every hole of the Euler class would imply VH$\exists$, so the Euler lemma says only where to look. \lab{hand}
% MSG/..._1248_audit_..._fill-lemma-equals-VHE-strength.md item 1.

\section{The quarter floor}\label{sec:quarter}
% Sources: WK/MathQuarterFloorFinal.md; WK/MathQuarterFloorLemmaA.md; WK/MathQuarterFloorBijections.md; WK/QuarterFloorSection-draft.md;
% WK/interns-2026-10-06/intern-A-cycle8.md; MSG 1753, 1805, 1811, 1827, 1848, 1855, 1901, 1920, 1925; commits c24d895, 0c0e098, 8ab6f1d, 2163e02, 9c1e03c, 707840b, 534dfb2, 3a346c8, ba90dfd.
Fix a degree-5 vertex $v$ of a triangulation $T$, with link $x_0,\dots,x_4$ (indices mod 5), and a Kempe class $S$ of 4-colourings of $T-v$. A state is \emph{filled} if its link uses three colours; let $F_i\subseteq S$ be the filled states whose singleton colour sits at $x_i$, and $F=\sum_i|F_i|$. An unfilled state has one repeated colour, at $x_j,x_{j+2}$; let $U_j\subseteq S$ be these states, $D_j\subseteq U_j$ the doubly locked ones, and $U=\sum_j|U_j|$. Every state uses four colours, so all ratios below are the same for labelled colourings and up to renaming. Tilley's D-resolvability asks only $F>0$; the data suggest much more.

\paragraph{Observation.} At orders 12--24, at every degree-5 vertex of every triangulation of minimum degree 5, every Kempe class of $T-v$ has \emph{at least a quarter of its states filled}: $U\le 3F$. This covers 156{,}033 holes and 160{,}979 classes; 419 classes sit exactly at $1/4$ and none lies strictly between $1/4$ and $16/59$. At degree 6 and 7 there is no such floor (minima $1/8$ and $2/17$). An adversarial search over 95{,}884 further core graphs found no class below $1/4$. \lab{computed}
% 0c0e098 (MSG 1805); 9c1e03c (MSG 1848). The statement was found from data, so these checks are post hoc.
Since a filled state extends uniquely to $T$, summing over the classes of one hole gives $P(T,4)\ge\frac14P(T-v,4)$.

\paragraph{The fan identity.} Let $G=T-v$ and let $G_i$ be $G$ with $x_i$ and $x_{i+2}$ identified. In a proper colouring of the 5-cycle with $k$ colours exactly $5-k$ of the pairs $\{x_i,x_{i+2}\}$ are equal, so for every $q$
\[P(T,q)\;=\;(q-5)\,P(G,q)\;+\;\sum_{i=0}^{4}P(G_i,q).\]
\lab{hand} (Long Table at $q=4$; Intern A at general $q$; unreviewed.) For $q\ge5$ the coefficient $q-5$ is non-negative, so positivity of $P(T,q)$ follows from positivity for smaller planar graphs; this is consistent with Birkhoff and Lewis's theorem that $P(G,q)>0$ for planar $G$ and real $q\ge5$~\cite{birkhoff1946}, though we have not checked that it is their argument. At $q=4$ the coefficient is $-1$: $P(T,4)=\sum_iP(G_i,4)-P(G,4)$, and the induction would need $\sum_iP(G_i,4)>P(G,4)$, which is the statement $F>0$ again. The quarter floor is the quantitative form $\sum_iP(G_i,4)\ge\frac54P(G,4)$; for a free 5-cycle the filled fraction is $1/2$. \lab{hand}
% WK/QuarterFloorLiterature.md (no prior statement of such a floor found; Birkhoff-Lewis conjecture at q >= 4 open).

\paragraph{What is proved.}
\begin{lemma}[\lab{hand}, audited: correct per $j$]\label{lem:quarterA}
In every Kempe class and for every $j$, $|U_j\setminus D_j|\le|F_{j+3}|+|F_{j+4}|$.
\end{lemma}
The map is Kempe's own swap, made injective: if the lock to $x_{j+3}$ fails, swap the component of $x_{j+3}$ in its two colours; otherwise swap that of $x_{j+4}$. The map is at most 2-to-1 over all $j$ (both cases occur), so $\sum_j|U_j\setminus D_j|\le2F$: the states that are not doubly locked are paid for. Only the doubly locked states remain.
% MathQuarterFloorLemmaA.md; 81,571 filled states with exactly two preimages (2163e02).

Join each unfilled state with the lock to $x_{j+4}$ to its image under the rotation $R$ (swap the component of $x_{j+2}$ in colours $\{\alpha,c(x_{j+3})\}$). The resulting graph is a disjoint union of paths and cycles, and
\[3F-U\;=\;2N_0+\tfrac32L_F+\sum_{\text{paths }P}\bigl(1-d(P)\bigr)-D_{\mathrm{cyc}},\]
where $N_0$ counts unfilled states with neither lock, $L_F$ the ``long bits'' of filled states, $d(P)$ the doubly locked states inside a path, and $D_{\mathrm{cyc}}$ those on cycles. \lab{hand}, reviewed by two interns, not yet by the audit; it holds with no mismatch on all 46{,}488 degree-5 classes of orders 12--23 and the order-24 floor holes \lab{computed}. At the floor every term vanishes, which explains the observed structure: four equal blocks $F_i,U_{i+1},D_{i+4},U_{i+2}$.

\begin{conjecture}[Quarter floor, per-$j$ form; \lab{open}]
For every degree-5 vertex, every Kempe class of $T-v$ and every $j$: $|U_j|\le|F_{j+1}|+|F_{j+3}|+|F_{j+4}|$. Summed over $j$, every class has at least a quarter of its states filled.
\end{conjecture}
Given Lemma~\ref{lem:quarterA} and the identity, this is equivalent to $|DD_j|\le\mathrm{room}_j$, where $DD_j$ is the set of doubly locked states whose rotation is again doubly locked and $\mathrm{room}_j$ is a sum of terms from the identity. It held in every class tested, with equality occurring; in the adversarial search the largest excess $|DD_j|-\mathrm{room}_j$ was 0, although all-doubly-locked rotation cycles of length up to 880 occur. \lab{computed}
% 9c1e03c; MathQuarterFloorFinal.md data table.

\paragraph{Strength, and why it is global.} The conjecture implies $F>0$ in every class, which is Tilley's D-resolvability at every degree-5 vertex, so it is \emph{at least as strong as R$^\ast$, and hence as the Four Colour Theorem}. It is a quantitative strengthening of a published open problem, not a way around it. It cannot be proved locally: a class with no filled state would satisfy every local fact used above, and doubly locked rotation orbits can be infinite ($A_r$). The data agree: a charging injection that looks only within $k$ Kempe swaps needs $k_{\min}=7$ at order 24 (per $j$; 6 if pooled over $j$), growing slowly with order, although about 98\% of cases match within 3 swaps. \lab{computed} No fixed-radius charging proof of this form exists on the data.

\section{What does not work}\label{sec:neg}
Most first attempts were false. These are the most instructive, each with its exact scope.

\paragraph{Uniform short fills.} One might hope a well-chosen pair fills every start within a fixed number of moves. On the order-17 triangulation 17:1, every legal pair has a start that needs 3 mixed moves \lab{computed}; on 24:7228 a start needs 3 mixed moves but 5 pure swaps \lab{computed}; and on general graphs pure distance is not bounded by any function of mixed distance \lab{hand}. Slides alone and one swap then slides fail at a frozen 21-vertex hole \lab{computed}; whether some uniform fixed swap budget suffices is \lab{open}. Lean confirms the positive side only up to length 2: a mixed fill of length at most 2 gives a pure fill of the same length \lab{compiled}.

\paragraph{Lock persistence (Conjecture L) and the family $A_r$.} Conjecture~L said that repeating one fixed swap $F$ keeps a state doubly locked only a bounded number of times, which would have made every vertex clean. It is false: a 20-vertex triangulation has a chain of length 6, and on the 5-fold symmetric triangulations $A_3,A_4,A_5$ (minimum degree 5) some states stay doubly locked under $F$ forever, with period 60. These certificates were confirmed by three independent programs: the L-Attack team's code, the Long Table lead's verifier and the audit's replay. \lab{computed} Yet every such state still fills in 2 or 3 swaps: locks persist, but other swaps fill. The number of infinite-orbit classes on $A_r$ for $r=3,\dots,9$ is 20 times a Jacobsthal number \lab{computed}, not replayed by the audit beyond $r=5$; that this continues is \lab{open}.

\paragraph{Radius is not bounded by 3.} The data on $A_r$ suggested radius at most 3. A 17-vertex 4-connected triangulation $T_4$, one edge flip from $A_3$, has two doubly locked states of radius 4 at one hole \lab{computed}, replayed exactly by the audit. A (6,6,6,6,6) hole of an order-28 triangulation of minimum degree 5 with no separating triangle also has a state of radius 4 \lab{computed}, not replayed by the audit. So Theorem~H's hypothesis cannot simply be dropped, and Conjecture~R (an absolute bound on radius) needs a bound of at least 4.

\paragraph{Local data do not decide.} The degrees of the link do not determine the radius: every hole of an order-14 triangulation has link degrees (5,5,5,5,6) and radius at most 2, while holes of $T_4$ with the same degrees have radius 4 \lab{computed}. No rule reading the colours within distance 2 of the hole decides whether $F$ keeps the locks on $T_4$ \lab{computed}, and the parity of the topological degree of a colouring is a Kempe invariant that does not see filling \lab{hand}, while on the graphs tested no higher modulus is invariant \lab{computed}.

\section{The open problem, and the record}\label{sec:open}
\textbf{The open problem} is Lemma R$^\ast$ in the case that Theorems H and HP leave: a 4-connected member of $\mathcal C$ in which every degree-5 vertex off $\varphi$ has at least two neighbours of degree other than 5. As far as we can tell it is a case of Tilley's open D-resolvability problem~\cite{tilley2017}, which asks the same of every degree-5 vertex. There the radius can be 4 ($T_4$ is such a graph), and local data do not decide it. A pre-registered search found four doubly locked states of radius exactly 5 in triangulations of minimum degree 5 with no separating triangle, at vertices that are still clean, so R$^\ast$ is not refuted; these certificates were replayed by the audit. \lab{computed}
