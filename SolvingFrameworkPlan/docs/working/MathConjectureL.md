## 1. The lock table [hand]

State s_k with repeat at x_j, c_j = c_{j+2} = alpha. Write m = c_{j+1} (middle single), a = c_{j+3}, b = c_{j+4}. Doubly locked means: an {m,a} path Lam_a from x_{j+1} to x_{j+3}, and an {m,b} path Lam_b from x_{j+1} to x_{j+4}. F swaps K = {alpha,a}-component of x_{j+2}.

**Lemma 1 (lock inheritance is exact).** In F(s): middle is b, the new indices give locks {b,m} from x_{j+4} to x_{j+1} (this is Lam_b, unchanged, since K has colours alpha,a, disjoint from {m,b}), and {b,a} from x_{j+4} to x_{j+2}, a path Q in the NEW colouring. So s_k has locks (Q_{k-1}, Q_k) with Q_{-1}, Q_0 := P1, P2 in the labelling of MathVHLine (P1 = beta-gamma x1~x3, P2 = beta-delta x1~x4). Only Q_{k+1} must be created at step k. Chords: P1 x1x3, P2 x1x4, Q1 x4x2 (delta,gamma), Q2 x2x0 (gamma,beta), Q3 x0x3 (beta,delta), Q4 x3x1, Q5 x1x4: the lock paths run along the pentagram chords in the order forced by Prop. 1. [hand; the Q-pairs agree with the diagnostic of MathVHLine §4]

**Lemma 2 (crossing lemma).** If s_k and F(s_k) are both doubly locked, then Lam_a(s_k) (the older lock, colours {m,a}) contains an a-coloured vertex u that is not in K. Proof: the curve C = v, x_{j+1}, Lam_a, x_{j+3}, v is simple and separates x_{j+2} from {x_{j+4}, x_j} (link order). The new path Q joins x_{j+2} to x_{j+4} avoiding v, so Q meets Lam_a in some vertex; Q has colours {a,b} in F(s_k), Lam_a has {m,a} in s_k, and the swap changes Lam_a only on K, where a becomes alpha; so the meeting vertex has colour a in both and lies outside K. Q can meet x_{j+1} (colour m) and x_{j+3} (now alpha) never. [hand]

Consequences: (i) Lam_a has at least two a-vertices (x_{j+3}, which is in K, and u), so at least 3 edges; (ii) u lies in an {alpha,a}-component K' different from K, so the {alpha,a} graph is disconnected between x_{j+2} and u. Checked [computed]: on random planar triangulations (orders 12 to 25, about 50000 locked states, 4 of them with chains of length 4, 1254 of length 3, 2725 of length 2) the weaker form "the {m,a}-component of x_{j+1} has an a-vertex outside K whenever F(s) is doubly locked" had 0 violations. The pairs, per step, are: s0: gamma-vertex of P1 outside K0; s1: beta-vertex of P2 outside K1; s2: delta-vertex of Q1 outside K2; s3: gamma?-type pattern cycles with the swap colour a.

## 2. KILLED LINES

**K4. "Jordan crossing alone contradicts a chain of length 6."** Dead [hand]. Lemma 2 is the only topological constraint the crossing gives, and it is a single-vertex existence condition (an a-vertex of Lam_a outside K). The five chords of the pentagram are each realised by a lock path, every pair of crossing chords x_{i-1}x_{i+1}, x_i x_{i+2} must share a vertex, but the shared vertices live in different colourings (a vertex u is on P1 in c0 and on Q1 in c1), so the planar constraints at different steps never meet in one colouring; the same abstract-model meta-obstruction as MathCleanVertexAttack §4 applies to a bound by boundary connectivity alone. I could not make two consecutive crossing vertices u_k, u_{k+1} conflict.

**K5. Z5-symmetric counterexample ansatz (infinite chain from a rotation).** Suppose T has an order-5 rotation rho about v, shifting link indices by 3, and F(s0) = pi o c0 o rho^{-1} with pi the colour 3-cycle forced by Prop. 1 (alpha fixed, gamma->beta, delta->gamma, beta->delta). Then F(s0) is the image of s0, so the chain is infinite by symmetry. This is dead [hand]: for y outside K0, c0(y) = pi c0(rho^{-1}y); for y in K0, c0(rho^{-1}y) = pi^{-1} sigma c0(y), sigma = (alpha gamma). Along a rho-orbit (length 5, the only fixed point other than v is the antipode w, which must be alpha outside K0) the colour transitions are beta->gamma, gamma->delta (outside K0) or gamma->alpha (in K0), delta->beta, alpha->alpha (outside K0) or alpha->delta (in K0). Closed walks of length 5 are only alpha^5 or (beta,gamma,alpha,alpha,delta) with the gamma in K0. Hence EVERY gamma vertex lies in K0, which contradicts Lemma 2 at s0 (P1 needs a gamma outside K0). So no rotationally symmetric infinite chain of this shape exists. (Orbit counts also give n_beta = n_gamma = n_delta.)

**K6. "Bound the chain by counting colour classes / lengths of lock paths."** Dead: Lemma 2 only gives |Lam_a| >= 3 and a vertex outside K, nothing that grows with k; no step forces the previous crossing vertex to be reused or destroyed. [open, no argument found]

## 3. The meta-obstruction (important) [hand]

**Proposition 3.** Conjecture L for degree-5 vertices of planar triangulations implies the four colour theorem by a half-page argument: in a minimal counterexample T take a vertex v of degree <= 5; degrees 3, 4 are Kempe-reducible; for degree 5 colour T - v by minimality, take s the induced state; if s is not doubly locked one swap fills the link (Theorem A Step 1); else by L some F^k(s), k <= N, is not doubly locked, one more swap fills the link, and v extends. So any hand proof of L, using only planarity, Jordan curves and Kempe swaps at one vertex, would be a short Kempe-style proof of 4CT, i.e. would also have to evade Heawood's counterexample type obstructions. This does not show L is false (4CT does not imply L), but it says L cannot be a "cheap local lemma"; it is at least as hard as an elementary proof of 4CT, and the bounded data (chains die at 4 or 5) are exactly what a true theorem and a hard-to-find counterexample would both produce. Likewise a targetless component (infinite chain) is not excluded by 4CT: it only says some Kempe-inequivalent colouring of T - v extends.
