# Intern A, cycle 8: a Birkhoff-Lewis-style route at degree 5, and where it breaks at q = 4

Hand only. Marks: [hand] derived here; [memory] unchecked recollection. I did not open Birkhoff-Lewis 1946; what follows is my own derivation of what such a proof must be.

## 1. The identity at general q [hand]
T plane triangulation, v of degree 5, link x0..x4 chordless, G = T - v, G_i = G with x_i, x_{i+2} identified (x_i, x_{i+2} are non-adjacent and lie on the pentagonal face of G, so G_i is planar). For a proper q-colouring c of G let k(c) = number of colours on the link. In a proper colouring of C5 every colour class has size <= 2, so the number t(c) of the 5 pairs {i,i+2} with equal colours is exactly 5 - k(c). Then
- P(T,q) = sum_c (q - k(c)) = (q-5) P(G,q) + sum_i P(G_i,q)
(P(G_i,q) counts colourings of G with c(x_i) = c(x_{i+2}); a chord would make that count 0, still correct). Both sides are polynomials, equal at all integers q >= 5, hence identical. At q = 4: P(T,4) = sum_i P(G_i,4) - P(G,4), Long Table's identity.
In G_i the vertex x_{i+1} has degree 3 with its three neighbours y = x_i = x_{i+2}, w_i, w_{i+1} pairwise adjacent, so P(G_i,q) = (q-3) P(T_i,q), where T_i = G_i - x_{i+1} is a plane triangulation on n-3 vertices (possibly with a separating 2-cycle, which factorises). So
- P(T,q) + (5-q) P(G,q) = (q-3) sum_i P(T_i,q).

## 2. How a q >= 5 bound goes [hand]
**Positivity.** For real q >= 5 the term (q-5) P(G,q) is >= 0 by induction (G is planar with fewer vertices), so P(T,q) >= sum_i P(G_i,q) > 0 by induction. Degree 4 and 3 are the same with the factors (q-4) and (q-3). A non-triangulated planar graph reduces by P(H) = P(H+e) + P(H/e), both planar and positive by induction. So the induction carries **no strengthened hypothesis** beyond "all planar graphs with fewer vertices are positive", and the key is that the coefficient (q-5) is non-negative, so the term can be dropped. This looks like the easy half of Birkhoff-Lewis; whether their 1946 text does exactly this I cannot say [memory, unchecked]. In my recollection their bound is a lower bound of the shape q(q-1)(q-2)(q-3)^{n-3}; but that expression is the chromatic polynomial of the stacked (degree-3 insertion) triangulation, which is the **maximum** over n-vertex triangulations (each inserted vertex has exactly q-3 choices), so I doubt it is a lower bound; this recollection should be checked, not used.
**A multiplicative bound P(T,q) >= c(q) P(T-v,q)** needs more than the induction gives: P(T,q)/P(G,q) = (q-5) + E[t], the expectation over uniform colourings of G of the number of equal distance-2 pairs. Dropping (q-5) and induction give only E[t] > 0 as a polynomial positivity, not a constant. A constant needs a lower bound on Pr[x_i = x_{i+2}] in G, i.e. a comparison of P(T_i,q) with P(G,q) = P(T_i)-type quantities, which is a relation between a triangulation on n-3 vertices and the pentagon-holed graph on n-1 vertices, not available by induction. So I cannot reconstruct a per-vertex constant at q = 5 by this route. Free-ring baseline [hand]: the 5-cycle with 5 colours has 1020 proper colourings, split 300 (k = 3), 600 (k = 4), 120 (k = 5), so the ratio E[q-k] = 1200/1020 = 20/17; at q = 4 it is 120/240 = 1/2 (Long Table's 1/2).

## 3. Where it breaks at q = 4 [hand]
The coefficient (q-5) becomes -1: P(T,4) = sum_i P(G_i,4) - P(G,4). The induction step needs sum_i P(G_i,4) > P(G,4), equivalently E[t] > 1 over colourings of G, and 4CT-type positivity of each P(G_i,4) = P(T_i,4) (note q-3 = 1, so P(G_i,4) = P(T_i,4) when T_i is simple) does not give it: a sum of five positive numbers need not exceed the subtracted P(G,4). The negative term is the **unfilled-plus-filled count** of G: P(G,4) = F + U, so the needed inequality is 2F + U > F + U, i.e. F > 0, which is the same statement again. So the q = 4 induction is circular unless U is bounded above independently by the smaller triangulations. There is an independent expression: with two chords from x0 (T' = G + x0x2 + x0x3, a triangulation on n-1 vertices) one has P(G,4) = P(T') + P(G_0) + P(G_3), whence F = P(T,4) = P(T_1,4) + P(T_4,4) - P(T',4) [hand, from the five fan identities]; again a subtraction of a larger term P(T') (n-1 vertices) from two smaller ones.

## 4. Is the quarter floor the natural q = 4 analogue? [hand]
Yes in this sense. Define f(q) = P(T,q)/P(T-v,q) = (q-5) + E[t]. At q >= 5 the induction only needs f(q) > 0 and discards (q-5). The quarter floor f(4) = E[t] - 1 >= 1/4 is the statement that the discarded negative term costs at most 3/4 of E[t], i.e. sum_i P(G_i,4) >= (5/4) P(G,4): the **smallest quantitative strengthening of E[t] > 1** that is still plausible (free ring has E[t] = 3/2, floor 5/4 sits between 1 and 3/2). To induct on it one would need a hypothesis H(T) (about every smaller triangulation T_i, T') such that H(T_i) for the five T_i, together with a cap on P(G,4), implies sum P(T_i,4) >= (5/4) P(T-v,4). The observed class-level floor (each Kempe class >= 1/4 filled) is a refinement that would give the inequality colouring by colouring, so it is a natural carrier, but the data say it is global (needs a growing radius), so no local-type hypothesis (a fixed vertex neighbourhood) seems to suffice.
