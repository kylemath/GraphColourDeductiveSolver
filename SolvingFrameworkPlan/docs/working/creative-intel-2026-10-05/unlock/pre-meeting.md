# Pre-meeting

Unlock team, Creative Intel, 2026-10-05. Rewriter, Spin, and Obstruction. The three positions stand as written. This discussion is fixed before the joint page and is not revised afterwards.

## The disagreement

**Spin.** Every proper colouring of a $5$-cycle has a singleton. The count that a frozen link has length at least $8$ is the special case in which all four colours appear at least twice. It is not the whole fact. The cycle is not $2$-colourable, and no colour class has size $3$, so the only multiplicity types on at most four colours are $(2,2,1)$ and $(2,1,1,1)$. Both have a colour of multiplicity one. Type $(2,2,1)$ is a three-colour fill. Type $(2,1,1,1)$ leaves the hole unfilled and still has three legal slides. A degree-$5$ hole is never frozen. The published slide on order $24$, graph $7228$, from vertex $17$ to vertex $8$, lands on the link colours $(1,3,2,1,0,3)$. Four colours remain. A singleton slide need not fill. Adding the apex-slide rule, which only repeats that the apex colour occurs once, does not yield $\mathrm{VH}\exists$. The colour count on the apex link is a fact about that neighbourhood. Feasibility of obtaining $\mathrm{VH}\exists$ from the defect theorem plus an apex slide: **Low**.

The two-chord sentence is the wrong object. The $7228$ slide fails by staying on four colours. It does not fail because a crossing is missing.

**Rewriter.** The defect theorem is true, and it is the hypothesis of a slide, not a filling derivation. A rewrite state is the circular word on the current link, together with the chain predicates in the deletion. The normal form a filling system would have to reach is the $(2,2,1)$ word. On the locked word $(\alpha,\beta,\alpha,\gamma,\delta)$ both blocking paths are present, so the Jordan rule, which deletes one blocking path by one swap, leaves four colours. That is a rewrite of the old word, and it stops.

The apex slide is the rule that leaves the $5$-cycle. The old link is $x_0,\ldots,x_4$, the apex is $x_i$, and the slide is $v\to x_i$. The new link carries $x_{i-1}$, $v$, $x_{i+1}$ consecutively, in that order or its reverse, coloured $c(x_{i-1})$, $c(x_i)$, $c(x_{i+1})$ in the first reading. Every further neighbour of $x_i$ lies off the old word. The old predicates are components in $T-v$; the predicates after the slide are components in $T-x_i$. A rewrite on circular $5$-words has no term whose right-hand side is that new link. Spin's $7228$ slide follows the defect at vertex $8$, while the apex of that fan is vertex $7$. The unnamed-link obstruction is not the same sentence as the colour count on one non-apex slide. Feasibility that a system on the old word fills: **Low**.

The two-chord sentence is not this obstruction. The chords are absent from $T-v$. They are not a rewrite of the word, and they are not the letter the apex rule still has to read.

**Obstruction.** Both of those stops are the same circular branch, described twice. A legal apex slide is an edge of $M(T)$, so the image lies in the same component. If that component misses $F$, the image misses $F$. Legality of the slide does not meet the fill set. A statement that the image's Kempe class contains a fill quotes a fill in a deletion of the same triangulation, at the same order. The induction colours $T^\ast_\tau$, of order $n-1$, and applies the hypothesis once to $T$. It does not colour the slid hole.

The sentence that would stay at the original hole, with the order unchanged, is the two-chord sentence. It is the missing sentence. The two chords of $\tau$ split the face $C_v$ into the three triangles $x_ix_{i+1}x_{i+2}$, $x_ix_{i+2}x_{i+3}$ and $x_ix_{i+3}x_{i+4}$, and therefore a bichromatic path in $T-v$ misses at least one of the two crossings those chords mark. The sentence is unavailable. The chords lie in the vacant face, on the side of $C_v$ left by deleting $v$, and the paths lie in $T-v$. A path there does not enter the vacant face. The pair $(v,\tau)$ is chosen before the colourings. Remark 2.3 equates $\mathrm{VH}\exists$ with the family sentence, one start chosen independently at each pair, and the equivalence stops there. A rule that reads one colouring's chains and then replaces the fan is a different sentence. Spin's preference for vertex $8$ over the apex, read off one published colouring, is that different sentence. Feasibility that the selection lemma can be written: **Low**.

**Spin, in reply.** An implication that does not follow is a false route. Calling it the missing sentence puts a non-sequitur in the place where a reason would have to stand. The joint page should retire it. The defect theorem remains the part of this discussion that can be checked by counting vertices and edges of $C_5$, and the apex slide still does not yield $\mathrm{VH}\exists$.

**Rewriter, in reply.** Agreed that the two-chord implication is not a rewrite rule. The right-hand side that is actually missing is the link of $x_i$, and the chain predicates do not supply it: they do not name the extra vertices. Disagreed that the defect theorem is the output. The locked component still lacks a reason it meets $F$, and the defect theorem does not state that reason. On the order of the triple, "that order or its reverse" must be pinned before the fragment is signed. A circular word up to reversal is one object. A directed rotation is one of the two readings, and the reading is forced by the rotation at $v$.

**Obstruction, in reply.** If the two-chord sentence is refused, the refusal has to be the geometric one: the chords are not edges of $T-v$, and a curve that is not in the graph does not separate a path in $T-v$. Rebuilding the degree-$4$ curve with $v$ present is a different sentence, and on the locked word that curve separates a pair whose swap leaves four colours. The page must not replace the retired sentence by a per-colouring retarget, and it must not replace it by a call at the slid hole. The family equivalence is not a permit to choose the fan after the chains are read.

The three claims do not coincide. Spin's engine is the singleton, and it stops because the slide need not drop the colour count. Rewriter's engine is an equational rule on the old word, and it stops because the new link is not a word on that cycle. Obstruction's engine is a Jordan sentence about the two chords, and it stops because the chords lie in the vacant face and the pair is fixed before the colourings. Each speaker rates the other's engine as the wrong sentence.

## What the joint page will assert

1. **Defect theorem.** A proper colouring of $C_5$ uses no colour more than twice, because every three vertices span an edge; that sentence is proved on the page. $C_5$ is not $2$-colourable. On at most four colours the only multiplicity types are $(2,2,1)$ and $(2,1,1,1)$. Both have a colour of multiplicity one. Type $(2,2,1)$ is a three-colour fill. Type $(2,1,1,1)$ has three legal slides and is unfilled. A degree-$5$ hole is never frozen. The published slide on order $24$, graph $7228$, vertex $17$ to vertex $8$, lands on link colours $(1,3,2,1,0,3)$, still four colours.

2. **Apex-slide fragment, signed only with the orientation pinned.** The positive rotation at $v$ is $(x_0,\ldots,x_4)$. The positive rotation at the apex $x_i$ then reads the consecutive triple $(x_{i+1},\, v,\, x_{i-1})$, and the new state colours it $c(x_{i+1})$, $c(x_i)$, $c(x_{i-1})$. The order $(x_{i-1},\, v,\, x_{i+1})$, coloured $c(x_{i-1})$, $c(x_i)$, $c(x_{i+1})$, is the opposite orientation of the same triple. In both readings $v$ lies between the two link neighbours. The other neighbours of $x_i$ are not determined by the old circular word. There is no rewrite system on circular $5$-words alone whose right-hand side is the new link. Feasibility of filling by such a system: **Low**.

3. **One Kempe rewrite at the fixed hole.** A swap can delete one blocking path; that is the degree-$4$ step. A locked word $(\alpha,\beta,\alpha,\gamma,\delta)$ carries both the $\beta\gamma$-path and the $\beta\delta$-path, so one swap does not fill. The two paths are written on the page. The claim is not expanded into a length bound.

4. **The two-chord selection sentence is retired.** The chords of the fan are drawn in the face left by $v$. They are not edges of $T-v$. A path in $T-v$ is not separated by a curve that is not in the graph. The sentence "therefore a bichromatic path misses one of the two crossings" does not follow from the splitting of that face. Rebuilding the degree-$4$ Jordan argument with $v$ present produces the curve through one blocking path and the hole, and that curve separates a pair whose swap leaves four colours. Remark 2.3 is taken as written: the family sentence is equivalent to $\mathrm{VH}\exists$, by one start at each of finitely many pairs, chosen independently. Choosing the fan after reading one colouring's chains is not that equivalence. A per-colouring retarget is a different sentence.

5. **Circularity.** The sentence "the apex slide lands in a Kempe class that contains a fill" quotes a fill at the same order. The induction colours $T^\ast_\tau$, of order $n-1$, and applies the hypothesis once to $T$. The page does not propose an induction on the slid hole.

6. **The output sentence.** A component of $M(T)$ that meets $S(v,\tau)$ and misses $F$ contains a locked start of type $(2,1,1,1)$, both blocking paths, and the legal apex slide, with the image still in the component. It does not contain a reason that the component meets $F$. That is the shape of the missing lemma, not the lemma. Feasibility that this shape can be filled without a length cap and without a same-order induction: **Low**.
