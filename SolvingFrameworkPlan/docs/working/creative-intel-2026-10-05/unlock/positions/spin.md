**[hand]** On four colours, a colouring of a cycle of length $d\le 7$ that uses all four colours has a colour of multiplicity $1$. Let $m_0,m_1,m_2,m_3$ be the multiplicities, each at least $1$, and let $s$ be the number equal to $1$. The other $4-s$ multiplicities are at least $2$, so $d\ge s+2(4-s)=8-s$, and therefore $s\ge 8-d\ge 1$. The inequality counts vertices only. A frozen link, proper and with every colour at least twice, is the case $s=0$, which forces $d\ge 8$. A $5$-cycle cannot carry four colours each at least twice.

The same count leaves proper colourings that use fewer than four colours free of defects. On the $7$-cycle with vertices $0,\ldots,6$ in order, colour $0$ on $\{0,2,4\}$, colour $1$ on $\{1,5\}$, and colour $2$ on $\{3,6\}$ is proper of type $(3,2,2)$.

On a $5$-cycle a proper colouring has a defect even when only three colours appear. The cycle is odd, so at least three colours appear. The independence number of $C_5$ is $2$, so each multiplicity is $1$ or $2$. Three parts in $\{1,2\}$ summing to $5$ are only $2+2+1$. Four parts in $\{1,2\}$ summing to $5$ are only $2+1+1+1$. The integer type $(3,1,1)$ has a part $3$, so two vertices of that colour are adjacent, and it is not a proper colouring. Two colours are impossible on an odd cycle.

**Defect theorem.** There is no proper $4$-colouring of a $5$-cycle without a singleton. The only types are $(2,2,1)$ and $(2,1,1,1)$. Call each multiplicity-$1$ colour a defect, and call a vertex of that colour a defect vertex.

Type $(2,2,1)$ is a $3$-colour fill: three colours appear, the missing colour colours the hole, and the unique colour is still a defect. Type $(2,1,1,1)$ has three defect vertices and uses four colours, so the hole stays unfilled. In both types a singleton slide is legal. The colour $\alpha$ of a defect vertex $u$ occurs once on the link, every other neighbour of the hole differs from $\alpha$, and the slide that puts $\alpha$ on the hole is proper.

**[hand]** Frozen, meaning no legal slide and no Kempe swap that reduces the link's colour count, cannot happen at degree $5$. A slide is available whenever there is a singleton, and the defect theorem supplies one. The slide clause fails, so the conjunction fails. The Kempe clause is idle at this degree.

A singleton slide need not reduce the colour count. The defect can move without the link becoming $3$-coloured. From a state $(h,c)$, let $u$ be a defect vertex of colour $\alpha$. The slide moves the hole to $u$ and colours $h$ with $\alpha$. Every neighbour of $u$ other than $h$ keeps its colour, which differs from $\alpha$ because $c$ is proper at $u$. So $\alpha$ occurs once on the new link, at the old hole. One defect has moved onto $h$. The new link is the neighbourhood of $u$, and the number of colours there is a fact about that neighbourhood.
