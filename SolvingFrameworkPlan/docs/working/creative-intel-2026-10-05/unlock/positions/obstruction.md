## The lemma

The proposed lemma has the quantifiers of $\mathrm{VH}^\exists$, and no cap on length.

For every spherical triangulation $T$ of minimum degree $5$, there exist a degree-$5$ vertex $v$ and a legal fan $\tau$ such that for every colouring of $T^\ast_\tau$, either the start has a mixed fill of length at most $2$, or the apex slide lands in a state whose Kempe class at the new hole contains a fill.

**[compiled]** Short fill. From a proper deletion colouring, a mixed filling path of length $n\le 2$ yields a pure Kempe filling path at the original hole of length at most $n$. The first branch is this theorem on those starts that already have such a path.

**[compiled]** Three-move obstruction. If a proper deletion start has no pure fill within three swaps, and some mixed path of length three reaches a fill, then the path starts with a legal singleton slide, and every two-swap finish after that slide has the slid colour in its first pair. The hypothesis includes a path. The conclusion is the first move and the first pair of that path.

**[hand]** On $\mathcal K$ the first branch is empty, and the second branch would put $\mathcal K$ into $F$. A mixed fill of length at most $2$ inside $\mathcal K$ becomes, by the short-fill theorem, a pure fill at the same hole, so $\mathcal K$ would meet $F$. The apex image lies in $\mathcal K$. A fill in its Kempe class at the new hole is reached by swaps, which are edges of $M(T)$ at that hole, so that fill would lie in $\mathcal K$. The three-move obstruction stays silent on $\mathcal K$: the component supplies no path of length three for the hypothesis to consume. A bound $m\le 2$, a bound $\kappa\le \ell+1$, a fixed Kempe budget, or a fitted rank would be a cap on every start. The sentence above has no such cap. Its second branch is where it breaks.

**[hand]** The second branch is the vacancy target one slide later, on a triangulation of the same order. The image of $v\to x_i$ is a colouring of $T-x_i$. The vertex set still has $n$ elements. The degree of $x_i$ is at least $5$, and the apex is free to have degree $6$ or more. The link of $x_i$ is a cycle of that length. A link on which each of the four colours occurs at least twice has at least eight vertices; that pattern sits at a hole of degree at least $8$. The degree-$4$ argument swaps one pair at a hole of degree $4$, using one diagonal and one Jordan curve. The link of $x_i$ has length at least $5$. A Kempe class of $T-x_i$ that contains a fill is a fill statement about a deletion of the same $T$. The circular form of this call is the one already written: from a colouring of $T-h$, the slide produces a colouring of $T-u$, and a call on $T-u$ names the same order. The smaller triangulation the induction colours is $T^\ast_\tau$, of order $n-1$, built from the original fan before any slide. The apex slide produces no second graph of order $n-1$.

## Two chords

The step that would replace the second branch is a reason one of the two paths in $T-v$ is absent, with the order left unchanged and with no call at the new hole. The degree-$4$ proof gets that reason from one diagonal: the path through the opposite colours, together with the hole, separates the two ends of the diagonal, and the swap runs in $T-v$, where the diagonal is absent. The candidate here is that the two chords of one fan play the role of that diagonal, and that joint presence of both crossings means the fan was the wrong fan.

**[lead]** For every spherical triangulation $T$ of minimum degree $5$, there exist a degree-$5$ vertex $v$ and a legal fan $\tau$ such that every colouring of $T^\ast_\tau$ reaches $F$, by the short-fill theorem when a mixed path of length at most $2$ is present, and otherwise because a locked start on $\tau$ has one of the two useful paths absent.

The first missing sentence is the following.

**Missing.** The two chords of $\tau$ split the face $C_v$ into the triangles $x_ix_{i+1}x_{i+2}$, $x_ix_{i+2}x_{i+3}$ and $x_ix_{i+3}x_{i+4}$, and therefore a bichromatic path in $T-v$ misses at least one of the two crossings those chords mark.

**[hand]** The chords lie in the vacant face, and the path lies in $T-v$. Existing chords of $C_v$ lie in the disk of $T-v$ opposite the star of $v$. A legal fan draws its two chords in the face left by deleting $v$, and those chords are absent from $T-v$. A bichromatic path of $T-v$ runs in the opposite disk. The cycle $C_v$ separates that path from both chords. A point common to the path and a chord would lie on $C_v$ only as a shared endpoint, and the path never enters the vacant face. Joint presence of the two useful paths is the locked placement itself, and legality of the fan is the absence of those same chords from $T$. The Jordan curves that the paths do form are the curves made by $v$ together with one path. The $\{\beta,\delta\}$-path from vertex $1$ to vertex $4$ separates vertex $0$ from vertex $2$, and the edge $2\,3$ keeps vertex $0$ outside the $\{\alpha,\gamma\}$-component of vertices $2$ and $3$. The $\{\beta,\gamma\}$-path from vertex $1$ to vertex $3$ separates vertex $2$ from vertex $4$, and the edge $4\,0$ keeps vertex $4$ in the $\{\alpha,\delta\}$-component of vertex $0$, with vertex $2$ outside it. The swaps of those separated pairs are among the eight listed above, and each leaves four colours. In the degree-$4$ step the curve separates the two ends of the added diagonal, and the filling swap is the swap of that separated pair, run in $T-v$ where the diagonal is absent. In the locked placement the pair joined by the path is the pair whose separation would have filled, and the pair the curve separates is a pair whose swap leaves four colours.

**[hand]** Replacing the fan leaves the paths where they are. Both paths are paths of $T-v$. The fan contributes no edge of $T-v$. A colouring $(\alpha,\beta,\alpha,\gamma,\delta)$ is proper on the three fans based at vertices $1$, $3$ and $4$, and the same two paths lock the link on each of those fans. Joint presence is a property of the colouring of $T-v$. It persists under a change of which legal fan admits that colouring to $S$.

**[lead]** The third branch written in `fan-link.md` is an interior of this kind inside the minimum-degree-$5$ class: the gyroelongated hexagonal dipyramid with one degree-$5$ vertex deleted, link $(0,1,0,2,3)$, both useful paths present through interior vertices, and neither chord of the fan at the $\beta$-vertex an edge. On that interior both useful paths are present in $T-v$, and both chords of the fan at the $\beta$-vertex are absent from $T$. The missing sentence requires one of those paths to be absent.

**[hand]** The pair is fixed before the chains can be read. $\mathrm{VH}^\exists$ chooses $(v,\tau)$, and the colourings of that one $T^\ast_\tau$ come afterwards. Remark 2.3 equates this with the family sentence: for every family $(c_{v,\tau})$ with $c_{v,\tau}\in S(v,\tau)$, some pair has $(v,c_{v,\tau})$ reaching $F$. The pairs are finite, and a start may be chosen independently at each pair, so a choice, at every pair, of one start that does not reach $F$ is one family at which no pair reaches $F$. A rule that reads the paths of a single colouring and then replaces $\tau$ chooses the pair after that colouring. That rule is a different sentence from the family sentence. Remark 2.3 equates the family sentence with $\mathrm{VH}^\exists$, and the equivalence stops there. On a triangulation whose every legal pair carries a locked component, the family assembled from those locked starts has both paths at every pair. The family sentence asks for a pair inside that same family.

**[open]** Whether any sentence whose data are only the embedding of $T$, the vertex $v$ and the fan $\tau$, fixed before the colourings, forces one of the two useful paths in $T-v$ to be absent.

## Feasibility

Feasibility that this selection lemma can be written: **Low**. The two chords lie in the vacant face, the chains lie in $T-v$, and $(v,\tau)$ is fixed before a colouring exists to inspect. The equivalent family reading sees a whole family at once and, where every pair is locked, finds both paths still present at every pair.

The single missing sentence: the two chords of $\tau$ split the face $C_v$ into three triangles, and therefore a bichromatic path in $T-v$ misses at least one of the two crossings those chords mark.
