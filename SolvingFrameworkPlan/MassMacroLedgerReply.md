## Corrections adopted

The universal claim is `for every T, there exists r in D(T) such that Good(T,r)`. `D(T)` is every degree-5 vertex. Exterior roots of one anchor are a regression set. A failure on all of them does not kill the existential claim.

`Good(T,r)` means every proper colouring of `T−r` with `p=1` has a sequence of at most two single-component swaps, interior components allowed, after which `R` is strictly smaller. Intermediate ranks may rise. Stop when `p=0`. Terminal states need not have `q=0`. The rank is the existing one: `p = max(0, |colours on B|−3)`, `q = Σ |K\B|²` over the six pairs and components meeting `B`, and `R = (6n²+1)p + q`.

Polynomial `Select`, or a nonempty equivariant set `S(T)` whose every member is good, followed by tie-break, is a separate unstarted node. A single vertex cannot be equivariant on the icosahedron. Root 8 fails `(σ, β)` and passes the tested two-swap macro, so the seven other graph-36 roots are not the selection target for this formula. `(σ, β)` is label-sensitive.

Failure classes, as corrected:

- one bad colouring at one root kills `Good(T,r)` only
- a set fails if it contains one bad root
- `S(T)=D(T)` fails as soon as any root fails, which is stronger than the existential claim
- a selector fails only if the root it returns is bad
- the existential claim fails only when every degree-5 root fails

A charge rule must conserve total charge and prove that positive receivers have degree 5. Simplicity of an added edge follows from distinct nonadjacent ends. The open completion obligations are eligible corners, rotation updates, and filling. A chord splits one face dart-orbit. A bridge merges two. The recursive-certificate idea stays one possible restriction, not an invariant and not the active attack.
