- **Nothing found.** I found no bound of the form P(T,4) ≥ c·P(T − v,4) for a degree-5 vertex, and no Kempe-class version, in Birkhoff–Lewis (as reported), Jackson's survey, the Tutte and golden-ratio literature, or the Kempe-class papers. Not exhaustive: Birkhoff–Lewis 1946 and Woodall were not read in full.
- **Nearest known:** Birkhoff–Lewis proved P(G,q) > 0 for planar G at q ≥ 5, and conjectured it at q ≥ 4, which is still open. From memory (unchecked), their q ≥ 5 proof uses a per-vertex multiplicative lower bound, the same kind of statement as the quarter floor, but at q ≥ 5.
- **[hand] Exact identity:** P(T,4) = Σ_{i=0}^{4} P(T − v with x_i = x_{i+2}, 4) − P(T − v, 4). So the quarter floor is "the five fan contractions together have ≥ 5/4 as many colourings as T − v". For a free 5-cycle the filled fraction is 1/2, so the floor is half the independent-ring value.
- **Strength:** a positive floor at some degree-5 vertex of every core triangulation implies the 4CT, so the bound is at least as hard as 4CT. The class version implies Tilley's R\* and is stronger still. Worth stating in the paper as an observed, quantitative strengthening of Tilley's conjecture, [computed] at orders 12–24.
