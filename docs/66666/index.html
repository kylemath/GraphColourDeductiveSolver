The Six-Ring Trap

Delete a degree-five vertex (the hole) and four-colour what is left. The hole can be filled once its five neighbours (its link) use at most three colours. A colouring that uses all four is doubly locked when two two-colour chains block the obvious Kempe swaps. Its radius is the number of whole-chain Kempe swaps needed to reach a filled state. In The Tidy Trap and The Dead-End Region the hard holes sat among degree-five neighbours. This page is about a hole whose five neighbours all have degree six.

For a hole whose neighbours all have degree five, Theorem H [hand, reviewed by Math, not audited] works because the locks leave only 3 possible colourings of the surrounding ring. Around a (6,6,6,6,6) hole the ring has ten vertices, and the locks allow 74 ring colourings [hand + computed]. No swap that stays inside the link breaks a lock for any of them [computed]. Every small swap that could break one touches a ring vertex, and ring vertices always have neighbours further out, so the chain can leak.

Math built a finite automaton that tracks the 74 patterns and the possible chain connections outside the ring. It has a closed trap of 5,650 abstract states: the outside can keep answering so that the locks never break, forever [computed, exploratory]. So no argument of this local kind (Math's ring automaton, which sees the ball and the outside chain joins) can bound the radius here. This does not rule out every local argument. A real graph shows the cost: below is an order-28 triangulation with a doubly locked state of radius 4 [computed]. That is why the team is now looking for global invariants. Whether any bound on the radius exists (Conjecture R) is still [open]. The proof route needs less than a bound: only that in each graph one degree-five vertex has every doubly locked state reach a filled state in finitely many swaps (Lemma R*). A uniform bound is not required.

Why Theorem H stops · Lemma 1

74 ring patterns instead of 3

After rotating so the link reads a b a g d, the locks force only four conditions on the ten ring colours (read w0 m1 w1 … w4 m0). Together with properness they leave these 74 patterns [hand + computed]. Bright patterns occur in a doubly locked state of this graph. The current state's pattern is outlined.

Math's automaton · K-A1, killed

A trap the outside can hold forever

The automaton's state is a link colouring, a ring colouring and, for each of the three ways to split the colours into two pairs, a non-crossing pattern of chain joins outside the ring. We choose swaps and an adversary chooses the unknown outside joins. The greatest set the adversary never has to leave has 5,650 states on 370 ball colourings, and it contains a lock-consistent start for every one of the 74 patterns. In every trap state that state's lock is still present [computed, exploratory].

The leak is the reset: a swap that reaches the ring recolours part of the outside, so the joins of the other two splits are unknown again. With at most two outside joins per split there is no trap, and every pattern is settled within 7 steps. Three joins trap 66 patterns, and four trap all 74 [computed].

This card restates Math's result. The trap was not recomputed for this page, and the audit has not replayed it.

Where this leaves us

This local model is not enough

The order-28 example kills the guess "radius ≤ 3 at (6,6,6,6,6) holes" and nothing more. The highest radius known at any hole is still 4. In Math's data, real graphs mostly avoid the trap's resets: 63 of the 69 radius-3 states at (6,6,6,6,6) holes of orders 22 and 23 have a shortest route whose first swap stays inside the ball. No colouring that can never be filled was found at any (6,6,6,6,6) hole of order 23 or less, or in about 2,200 grown holes of orders 24 to 32 [computed, exploratory]. In the order-28 graph on this page every colouring reaches a filled one (a single Kempe class) [computed]. A bound would need information that survives a swap, which is a global question. Conjecture R (some bound on the radius) stays [open]. The proof route needs only Lemma R*: a finite radius at one degree-five vertex per graph, not a uniform bound [open].
