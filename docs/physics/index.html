Potts antiferromagnet · cluster moves · sphere versus torus

The Spin-Glass View

Our problem has a second name in physics. Give every vertex of a planar triangulation one of four colours, charge one unit of energy for every edge whose two ends match, and you have the four-state Potts antiferromagnet. A proper four-colouring is a state of zero energy: a ground state. A Kempe swap, which exchanges two colours on one connected two-colour chain, is exactly the cluster move that physicists use to stir such a system without raising its energy. This page sets the two vocabularies side by side, says what the literature has proved on a torus and on a sphere, lets you flip clusters yourself, and reports what we measured about the energy landscape.

Section 1

The dictionary

Delete a vertex of degree five from a triangulation and four-colour what is left. The deleted vertex is the hole, and its five neighbours are the link. The hole can be filled when the link uses at most three colours: the fourth colour is free for the hole. In physics language the hole is a defect: a spot where the lattice has the wrong coordination number (five neighbours where the flat triangular lattice has six). On a sphere there must be at least twelve such defects, because the charges 6 − degree add up to 12.

A stuck class is what a spin-glass physicist would call a metastable basin: the system sits in a zero-energy state, every allowed move leads to another zero-energy state, and none of them is a state in which the defect disappears. [computed here: none of the graphs below has one] Whether one can exist on a sphere triangulation of minimum degree five is the open question the project is circling (Tilley's D-resolvability conjecture, Section 2).

Section 2

Torus versus sphere: what is proved

The cleanest known obstruction to ergodicity lives on the torus, and it lives there because of the shape of the surface. On a torus some chains can wind around a loop that cannot be shrunk, and a winding number survives every swap. On a sphere every loop shrinks to a point, and no such number is known.

Torus: loops that cannot shrink

A chain can close up around the hole or around the tube, and neither loop can be pulled to a point. On a three-colourable (Eulerian) torus triangulation, the degree of a colouring modulo 12 does not change under Kempe swaps [proved in Mohar–Salas 2009].

Sphere: every loop shrinks

A chain loop can always be shrunk through the surface, so the torus mechanism has nothing to grip. Whether another quantity plays that role is [open].

Summary of the literature read [Long Table reading job; sources marked as above]

  • For 4 colours, the only proved non-ergodicity results are on closed surfaces with an invariant tied to the surface (torus, degree mod 12) and on special plane triangulations (Mohar's akempic family, with degrees divisible by 3 and so no degree-5 vertex; Florek's belts).
  • On the sphere, every single-class result needs extra structure: Eulerian, χ < k, or 4-critical (Feghali 2022, as listed in the source note). General plane triangulations remain open, for example the two-odd-vertex case.
  • Our position: general sphere triangulations with degree-5 vertices are [open]. Numerics found in the literature are on tori (the T(6,6) counts), on named graphs and greedy runs (Tilley 2017), and on belts (Florek 2025). No spherical WSK or Kempe-class numerics were found, so our census (orders 12 to 26) may be the first systematic sphere data. unchecked: a targeted search could still find something.
  • Why the torus mechanism does not transfer [hand judgement, unchecked]: the degree is defined on a closed surface and is invariant only for Eulerian triangulations, and the value 6 mod 12 is realised by colourings that wind round non-contractible cycles. A minimum-degree-5 sphere triangulation has 12 or more odd vertices, so the invariant is not available. Long Table also found that no disc version of the degree is a Kempe invariant (disc-degree-parity.md, path5-tait-sign-invariant.md, as named in the source note). This does not show that flat regions on a sphere are safe: a sphere obstruction would need a different mechanism.

Section 4

Glassy landscapes: what we measured

Picture the colourings that cannot yet be filled as a landscape, and the radius as altitude: filled states are the valleys, and a swap is a step. A proof by descent would need a simple score that some single swap always lowers, from every unfilled state. A state where no swap lowers the score is a local minimum, or in the physicists' word, stuck. The plain finding of Studio compute run 5 [exploratory] is that the energy landscape has local minima for every simple energy we tried.

Run 5: eleven candidate scores on the hard holes [exploratory, Mac Studio; not audited]

  • Test: for each score, count the unfilled states of T − v from which no single Kempe swap lowers it. A swap into a filled state always counts as progress. A score works only if that count is 0.
  • The scores: connected pairs, link components, lock size (total lock-chain size), lock distance, intern D's K vector, Φ by role, and lexicographic combinations of these.
  • No score works on any of the eight holes, except that lock distance has no stuck state at Errera's two holes (it does at the other six). Link components are constant on unfilled states, so every unfilled state is stuck for them.
  • Closest overall: total lock-chain size, with 2 to 10 stuck states per hole out of 60 to 2,713 unfilled states (table below). So path 4 (find a potential that some swap always lowers) stopped by its rule.

Three more landscape facts [exploratory, Studio sage-QA runs; same README]

  • Link-touching moves alone never created a stuck class (Run 2). Restricting the moves to chains that meet the link, at every degree-5 hole of orders 12 to 20 (882 holes) and on the four radius-5 certificates, leaves no class without a filled state, and the depth is unchanged except 2 to 3 at 16 holes.
  • No vertex deletion ever created a new Kempe class (Run 4 and its follow-up): over every multi-class T at orders 12 to 23, deleting a vertex never produced a class that was not already there. A single deleted vertex that merges all of T's classes always exists (some vertex of degree 5, 6 or more), though a degree-5 merging vertex does not always exist (2, 3, 8 and 18 multi-class graphs without one at orders 20 to 23).
  • Edge deletion does create new classes, and they are not frozen (Run 1). There are 17 new classes in 16 edge orbits, each with exactly 6 states, and every state has 3, 4 or 5 connected colour pairs out of 6. The sage's guess that they are rigid colourings was not borne out.

Section 5

Ideas under test (the project lead's)

Two ideas from the project lead, written down as questions. They are ideas, not results: nothing here has been tested as a proof route, and the one test that exists is marked.

(i) Derivatives of charge

The idea. Discharging uses a charge 6 − degree at every vertex, and on a sphere the charges sum to 12. Read this as a discrete curvature, and discharging as a discrete curvature flow (a combinatorial Ricci flow). Its first derivative is a flux (charge moving along an edge) and its second is a Laplacian (the difference between a vertex and the average of its neighbours).

The Kempe-dynamics version. So far we ask for a score that some single swap lowers (first order). The second-order version asks that some sequence of two or three swaps lowers it: a lookahead. Studio compute tested it [exploratory; sage-qa-runs/, main ec92cd5] on the four radius-5 certificates, the F-cycle hole, the order-22 radius-5 hole and Errera's two holes. For the score (lock size, then lock distance), some sequence of at most 3 swaps lowers it or fills the hole at every unfilled state. Two swaps are not always enough: 5 states need 3. The number needed does not grow with the radius. Most states four or five swaps from a fill can lower the score in one.

Conjecture K3 [open; scale test queued on the Studio]: from every unfilled state at a degree-5 hole, at most 3 swaps lower (lock size, lock distance) or fill. Because the score is a non-negative whole number, K3 at every hole would imply that every degree-5 vertex can always be refilled (Tilley's every-vertex form). So it is almost certainly too strong to prove easily, and it may fail on larger graphs. But it is crisp and testable: the Studio is checking every state at least four swaps from a fill in the exhaustive census, orders 20–26.

The catch. The distance to a fill is trivially such a potential, since it drops by one at every swap along a shortest fill. So a useful score has to be computable locally, from a bounded neighbourhood of the hole.

(ii) Charge teleporting by cutting and gluing

The idea. In Perelman's Ricci flow with surgery, trouble spots are cut out and capped, and the flow continues on what remains. Can charge, or the hole, be moved by cutting and regluing the sphere?

  • (a) Short separating cycles. The 4CT proof already cuts along short separating cycles (necks), and our core class is "no necks".
  • (b) Cutting along a lock chain. A lock chain plus the hole forms a closed curve. Cutting along it splits the sphere into two discs whose boundary uses only two colours, apart from the hole. Swaps strictly inside one disc do not touch the other. Could one induct on the smaller disc? (Careful point, added by this page: a swap in a colour pair that includes one of the two boundary colours can cross the boundary at a single vertex.)
  • (c) Moving the vacancy to another degree-5 vertex (path 6). By Meyniel, this only measures cost.
  • (d) The monotone quantity. Perelman needed a quantity that never goes the wrong way and survives surgery. That is exactly what run 5 failed to find.

Advisor's assessment [advice relayed by the coordinator; not a ledger result]

Idea 1(a), discharging as a flow: no content. Discharging is a static redistribution that proves some configuration exists; nothing flows in time. Combinatorial Ricci flow acts on circle-packing radii, not on colourings. Dropped.

Idea 1(b), k-step descent: circular as posed, because distance-to-fill already descends in one step. It is only meaningful for a locally computable energy (a bounded neighbourhood of the hole plus which lock chains exist and where they end). An experiment is queued on the Studio: for the best energy (total lock-chain size), find the least k such that some k-swap sequence lowers it. If k ≤ 2 everywhere, "two swaps always shrink the locks" becomes a lemma to try. If k tracks the radius, the idea is circular and closed.

Idea 2(a), cutting along short cycles: already done classically (the core class has no necks). 2(b), cutting along a lock chain: this is Kempe's own picture, and it gives the opposite of leverage. The disc inside the lock curve is inert: swaps inside it cannot touch either lock, and swapping the curve itself only rotates the locks (Errera's cycle). It does give a pruning lemma: while a lock stands, its disc is inert, so any filling sequence must swap a component outside both lock curves. That lemma is worth half a day by hand plus a check on the radius-5 certificates. 2(c), moving the vacancy: measures cost, proves nothing. 2(d), Perelman: the honest end of the analogy. His key was a global monotone quantity; the known global quantities on colourings are constant on Kempe classes, so this is the open problem restated.

Verdict: two cheap experiments are worth doing this week (k-step descent, inert-disc lemma). Neither is a route to a proof; either sharpens the paper or closes a line cleanly. The rest is metaphor.

What the experiments found [exploratory]. k-step descent: at most 3 swaps everywhere tested, not tracking the radius; this became Conjecture K3 above. Inert disc: the advisor's pruning claim is false. Intern A showed by hand that a shortest fill can begin with a swap strictly inside a lock disc (docs/working/InternA-inert-disc.md). Studio compute then scanned all 81,564 shortest-fill moves at orders 17–20 and found exactly one real instance: order 20, plantri index 51, hole 1, a state three swaps from a fill whose first swap flips a single vertex inside the disc. The audit replay is pending. Such moves are rare: none occur at the eight hardest holes, and none at states four or more swaps from a fill.

Section 6

What this means

The physics language fits exactly: colourings are ground states, Kempe swaps are cluster flips, and a stuck class is broken ergodicity. The torus mechanism does not transfer to the sphere, because it needs a winding number that a sphere does not have. Nobody, including us, has a quantity that rules out traps on the sphere.

The cleanest empirical conjecture to come out of all this [exploratory]

Deleting a vertex of any degree never creates a new Kempe class. It held for every multi-class triangulation T from order 12 to order 23 (Studio run 4 and its follow-up). It is the statement most likely to interest experts, and it is a statement about data, not a proof.
