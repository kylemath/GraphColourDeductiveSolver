The Dead-End Region

In The Tidy Trap a single colouring blocked descent. Here, in a very symmetric triangulation (20 symmetries), root 3 is a degree-five hub: all five of its neighbours have degree five, ringed by degree-six vertices. At this root the dead end is a whole region: ten colourings from which every strictly downhill sequence of two-swap steps runs into a trap before it can reach a finished state. These are also exactly the ten colourings that breadcrumb runs leave warnings on.

Only five of them are strict traps (R = 1842): no two-swap step goes downhill at all. The other five are twins (R = 1850): each has a downhill two-swap step, but only into its trap. A trap and its twin differ by one two-vertex chain at the opposite hub (vertex 13 when the root is 3), and they have exactly the same boundary chain pattern. That is why the warning-charging idea failed here. Breadcrumb descent still gets out every time. Replay its recorded runs and watch the warnings drop.

The dead-end region as a network of swaps

trap twin (also a dead end) neighbour that descends neighbour that is a target thick line = trap and twin with the same boundary pattern · labels show R

A ring of five pits. At root 3, and at its mirror image root 13, the ten dead ends are five separate pits. Each pit is a trap (R 1842) and its twin (R 1850), joined by one small swap at the opposite hub. Pits never swap directly into one another. Neighbouring pits are linked only through two crossed connector states at R 1873: one joins this pit's trap to the next pit's twin, the other joins this pit's twin to the next pit's trap. The other ten are spurs at R 1866, each touching a single pit; they are not dead ends. All twenty states around the pits score 1866–1873, and none is finished. So the region is a circular moat, and a five-fold rotation about the axis through the two hubs carries each pit onto the next. Nothing downhill can cycle, because R falls at every step. A single downhill swap never leaves a pit: from a twin it goes only to its own trap. A two-swap step, though, can pass over a connector (R 1873 in the middle) and land in a neighbouring pit's trap, still ending lower (1850 to 1842). A typical four-warning run starts at a twin, drops into its own trap and warns, steps back, hops over connectors into two neighbouring traps, warning at each, then warns the twin itself and leaves with one three-swap escape.

The shape of the graph

A symmetric hub

Roots 3 and 13 are the only roots of this graph whose descent can fail, and they are images of each other under its symmetries. Each is a degree-five vertex whose neighbours all have degree five, surrounded by five degree-six vertices.

Data: every colouring (up to renaming colours) at every degree-five root of Plantri triangulation 17/3, from deadend_demo_data.py.
