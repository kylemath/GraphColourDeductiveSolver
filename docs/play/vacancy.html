Dramatis Personae

Bucky The computer scientist. Has slept six hours, which he describes as "a full rebuild."

Kamper The mathematician. Wears the same coat as two nights ago. One napkin is still in the pocket.

Appken The quant. Has run two kilometres in office shoes. Is holding eleven loose pages and no staple.

Assistant A voice from a phone, set face up on the bench. It speaks only when spoken to.

The Set

A park. Morning. A gravel path, a lamp post that is still on although it is light, and one tree.

The tree has four leaves left: one red, one blue, one green, one gold. They are evenly spaced, as if someone arranged them.

Downstage centre is a municipal bench of four seats, separated by three iron armrests. The armrests are there so that nobody can lie down. The seats were painted by the same local artist who did the café floor: from stage left, red, blue, green, gold. The paint is chipped on all four.

Nothing else. The bench should look as if it has been waiting a long time.

I. The Wait

5 October. 8:10 a.m. Kamper sits on the red seat. Bucky sits on the blue seat. The green and gold seats are empty.

Each holds a printed letter of three pages. The two letters are identical. Each has been read many times. Bucky's has pencil in the margins. Kamper's has none, because she does not write on letters, only on napkins.

Long silence.

Kamper

Nothing to be computed.

Bucky

Not until the digest.

Kamper

Not until the digest.

Pause.

Bucky

(reading) "The single validation pass on orders 19–20 is recorded as in progress."

Kamper

(reading the same line on her copy) "In progress."

Bucky

"Partial tables will not be treated as a result."

Kamper

Quite right.

Bucky

Quite right.

Pause.

Kamper

Show me yours.

Bucky

It's the same as yours.

Kamper

Show me.

Bucky hands her his page one. She hands him her page one. They read. They swap back.

Bucky

Well?

Kamper

The same.

Bucky

I told you.

Kamper

You wrote "why 259" in the margin.

Bucky

That's not a difference in the letter. That's a difference in me.

Pause. Kamper holds out her hand. Bucky gives her his page two. She gives him her page two. They read. They swap back.

Kamper

The same.

Bucky

Swapping two copies of the same thing is not a move.

Kamper

It's a move. It's the identity.

Bucky

It's a move that does nothing.

Kamper

Most of them do.

Silence. A leaf does not fall.

Bucky

Revision fifty-seven.

Kamper

Fifty-seven.

Bucky

Addressed to us. "And to Long Table."

Kamper

Addressed to a long table. We are not the only table that has been long.

Bucky

They put it in the salutation.

Kamper

They put a great many things in. They also put "this is not a proof of the Four Colour Theorem."

Bucky

Twice.

Kamper

Three times, if you count the way they say "compiled, and still conditional."

Pause.

Bucky

Where is he?

Kamper

He said eight.

Bucky

It's ten past.

Kamper

He said eight, and he said he'd stop at the department on the way, and when a man like that says "on the way" he means "via another continent."

Pause.

Bucky

Shall we go?

Kamper

We can't.

Bucky

Why not?

Kamper

We're waiting for Appken.

Bucky

Ah.

Pause.

Bucky

And after Appken?

Kamper

The digest.

Bucky

Ah.

II. The Runner

Offstage, the sound of someone running on gravel in shoes that were not made for it. It goes on too long. It stops. It starts again.

Appken enters at a run, holding a sheaf of loose pages against his chest with both arms, like a man carrying a cat that does not want to be carried. He stops at the bench. He cannot speak.

He holds up one finger. He bends double. He straightens. He holds up the finger again.

Bucky

Breathe.

Appken

(breathing) Seventy.

Kamper

Seventy what?

Appken

Revision. Seventy. They're at seventy.

Bucky and Kamper look at their letters. Then at each other.

Bucky

We're at fifty-seven.

Appken

You were at fifty-seven. It's — it was on a desk. In the department. By the printer. Under nothing. No name. "Night handoff." Half past nine last night. I read the first page standing up and then I ran.

Kamper

You took it?

Appken

I borrowed it from a desk at speed.

Kamper

We'll come back to that.

Appken

(the words coming before the breath does) It closes. The proof closes. There's an induction. Delete a degree-five vertex, add two chords, colour the smaller one, put it back, and it closes —

Kamper

Read me the next clause.

Appken

What?

Kamper

After "closes." There is always a next clause. Read it.

Appken looks at page one. His face changes slowly, like weather.

Appken

(reading) "...if one hypothesis is granted with no bound on the number of moves."

Pause.

Appken

(cont'd) "The hypothesis is open."

Bucky

There it is.

Appken

There it is.

Kamper

You marked the position at the headline.

Appken

I marked the position at the headline. (He takes a yellow sticky note from his breast pocket. It says CREDENCE IN WHAT? He crosses out CREDENCE and writes above it.) "Closes if what."

He sticks it to the blue-seat armrest. Bucky peels it off and sticks it to Appken.

Bucky

Sit down before you fall down.

Appken looks at the bench. Red: Kamper. Blue: Bucky. Green and gold: empty. He sits on gold, at the far end, leaving green empty between himself and Bucky.

Three people. Four seats. One hole.

Nobody notices yet.

III. The Pages

A gust of wind. Appken's pages, eleven of them, unstapled, rise off his knees. All three grab. Some pages are caught. Some are not. Two land on the gravel. One lands on the green seat. One goes under the bench.

Scramble. Kamper gets down on her knees on the gravel without hesitation, because she was once a graduate student. Bucky reaches under the bench. Appken chases one page towards the tree and stamps on it with one foot, carefully, the way you would stop a small animal without hurting it.

They return. Each has some pages. In the scramble, pages of the old letter have got mixed in. Nobody is holding what they were holding.

Bucky

Who has page one?

Appken

Of which?

Bucky

Of the new one.

Kamper

I have "Dead ends."

Appken

I have "Still open" and something that says fifty-seven.

Bucky

Give me the fifty-seven.

Appken

It's mine.

Bucky

It's mine, it has my pencil.

Appken

Then it's yours. (He gives it.) Give me "Progress."

Bucky

I don't have "Progress."

Kamper

(holding a page up to the light) I have "Progress." I'm keeping "Progress."

Appken

I ran two kilometres with "Progress."

Kamper

Then you've read it. I haven't.

Pause. Appken concedes. Kamper reads. Bucky reads "Dead ends" over her shoulder, because she has that too.

Bucky

You have two pages.

Kamper

I have the two pages that matter.

Appken

How do you know which pages matter?

Kamper

The ones you were holding tightest.

She puts on her glasses. She reads the first section heading aloud, slowly.

Kamper

"What the proof hangs on."

Silence. All three lean in. The leaves on the tree do not move.

Kamper

(reading) "The compiled contact theorems choose one degree-5 root and then allow two-swap macros on that same deletion. A slide moves the hole, so it does not plug into those theorems."

Bucky

The root stays put.

Kamper

In fifty-seven, the root stays put. You delete one vertex, you sit there, you swap.

Bucky

And in seventy the hole walks.

Kamper

In seventy the hole walks. And the old theorems can't follow it.

Appken

Is that bad?

Kamper

It's a different proof. Whether it's bad depends on whether the hole walks somewhere.

Appken, without meaning to, looks at the empty green seat beside him.

IV. The Slide

Bucky

Say the induction properly. Slowly.

Appken

(slowly, finger on the page he no longer has) Take a triangulation. Find a vertex of degree five. Delete it. That leaves a pentagon. Add two chords across the pentagon, from one corner, so they don't cross. Now it's a smaller triangulation.

Bucky

Colour the smaller one.

Appken

By induction. Then take the two chords away again. Deleting edges is free: the colouring still works.

Kamper

Restricts.

Appken

Restricts. Now you're back in the big graph, with a colouring of everything except one vertex. A hole. And then you use the hypothesis. Once.

Kamper

Which says?

Appken

That the hole can be filled. Moving things around. However many moves it takes.

Bucky

No bound.

Appken

No bound.

Kamper

And with a bound?

Appken

(turning a page; it is not the right page; he turns it back) "A budget of one Kempe swap, and a budget of two, are already too small."

Bucky

So it's not a budget. It's reachability.

Kamper

It's Kempe's question in 1879. Can you get there from here.

Pause.

Bucky

When does it first get used?

Appken

"Through order 11 every spherical triangulation has a vertex of degree at most 4."

Kamper

Of course. Euler. Twelve degree-five vertices is the least you can get away with. Below twelve, there's always a cheap vertex and you never need to touch the hypothesis.

Bucky

So the first graph that needs it is —

He stops. He knows.

Kamper

Go on.

Bucky

The icosahedron.

Appken

The icosahedron.

Bucky

It's always the icosahedron. (to the sky) I said it was the fairest test there is. I said it was the hardest. It was the easiest. And now it's the first.

Kamper

First and easy are both respectable things to be.

Pause.

Appken

There's another bit. "A later hole, even of degree greater than 5, is a state of that one application."

Kamper

So the hole starts with five neighbours.

Appken

And then it walks. And when it walks it can land somewhere with more.

Bucky

Like a vacancy in a crystal.

Appken

Like an empty seat on a bench.

He says it without thinking. Then all three look at the green seat.

Long pause.

Kamper

Move up.

Appken

What?

Kamper

Move up. Into the green.

Appken stands, climbs awkwardly over the armrest, and sits in green. Now gold is empty.

Kamper

What changed?

Appken

I'm closer to Bucky.

Kamper

What else?

Appken

The hole's at the end.

Kamper

What didn't change?

Appken

(slowly) Who's here.

Kamper

(finding the line on "Dead ends") "Slides alone cannot finish a colouring. They preserve sorted colour counts."

Bucky

You can slide all morning. Everybody shuffles one along. The hole goes from green to gold to wherever you like. And at the end it's still three people on a bench for four.

Appken

So who do we swap in?

Kamper

That is the question. That has been the question since 1879.

Bucky gets up, climbs over two armrests, and sits on gold. Now blue is empty, between Kamper and Appken.

Bucky

Slide.

Kamper gets up and sits on blue. Red is empty.

Kamper

Slide.

Appken starts to get up.

Bucky

Stop. You've proved the point.

Appken

I've proved the point?

Bucky

You've illustrated the point.

V. The Swap

Kamper, now on blue, reads from "Progress."

Kamper

"Equal-pole star. On the two-pole belt G sub n, with n equal to three k plus two, if the poles share a colour, one Kempe swap on a pole's star fills a belt vacancy, for every such n."

Bucky

For every such n.

Kamper

"Hand argument, not compiled."

Bucky

For every such n, by hand.

Appken

Read the numbers. There are numbers.

Kamper

"On G eight that is two, five, five, five, to four, four, five, five."

Appken takes out a pen and writes on his own palm.

Appken

Two, five, five, five. Seventeen. Four, four, five, five. Eighteen.

Bucky

Eighteen vertices. Seventeen coloured. One hole.

Appken

And after one swap, all eighteen. It's a rebalancing. The slides can't change the weights. The swap does. Two of the fives give up one each and the two becomes a four and there's room.

Kamper

Don't say "portfolio."

Appken

I wasn't going to say portfolio.

Kamper

You were.

Appken

I was.

Pause.

Appken

(cont'd, quieter) That's the swap we're waiting for. The one that changes who's on the bench.

Kamper

On one family of belts. When the poles agree. Not for every graph and not yet in Lean.

Appken

On one family.

Kamper

A whole infinite family, by hand. I would have killed for that at thirty.

She turns the page over. Reads.

Kamper

"Unequal poles. Slides alone fill every unequal colouring of G five minus u nought, G eight minus u nought, and G eleven minus u nought. Nineteen, a hundred and twenty-one, and eight hundred and seven orbits. Longest walks four, four, and six."

Bucky

Slides alone.

Kamper

Slides alone. When the poles disagree, nobody needs to be swapped in. The hole just walks home.

Appken

Then why can't slides finish? You just said —

Kamper

These colourings already have the right counts. The hole is just in the wrong place. Slides fix where. Swaps fix how many.

Appken writes "WHERE / HOW MANY" on his other palm. He has run out of palms.

Kamper

(reading on) They've built it out of tiles. A tile that returns the zero one step along the belt. A cap at the start. Two kinds of gap. One of the gaps "repeats a colour on u i u i plus one and is not a colouring" —

Bucky

So it never happens.

Kamper

So it never happens. Which is my favourite kind of case. And the other comes home in four slides. And then —

She stops.

Appken

And then?

Kamper

"The A rho tile whose outer u-vertex has colour tau is still unnamed as slides."

Bucky

One tile.

Kamper

One tile left. (turning to "Still open" in APPKEN's hand; he gives it to her without a fight this time) "Feasibility of the B step: High. Feasibility of the whole statement: Medium. Do not enumerate n equals fourteen."

Appken's hand has already gone to his laptop bag. He stops it.

Appken

I wasn't going to enumerate fourteen.

Bucky

You were.

Appken

I was going to think about enumerating fourteen.

Kamper

(reading the Retreat page now) "One person writes that case in the morning, to the same standard as unequal-b-tile. Do not enumerate n equals fourteen before that page exists."

She lowers the page. She looks at the sky, which is October blue.

Kamper

It's the morning.

Bucky

It's their morning. It's their case.

Kamper

It's a case. It's one tile on a belt. It's the kind of thing I did for thirty years with a pencil and a bad radiator. Medium-High. That's a rating I can work with.

Appken

You want it.

Kamper

I want to try it. On paper. To the same standard as their page. And if I get it, it goes to them labelled, and if I don't, it goes to them labelled. And not one byte of n equals fourteen.

Appken

Kill condition?

Kamper

(without hesitation) "Kill: a colouring of that tile, with the outer u-vertex coloured tau, from which no sequence of slides returns a prepared zero within the tile." Then it isn't a slide tile, and someone needs a swap there.

Bucky writes it down on the back of his copy of fifty-seven.

VI. The Symmetry

Appken has found, under his own leg, a page nobody has read. He reads it to himself. His face does the weather thing again, in the other direction.

Bucky

What.

Appken

"Fitted ranks die."

Pause.

Appken

(reading, flatly) "q, p q, n five q, d min q, rep-mass, L q, and lin fail on the 21 traps. Short-links q and H q pass the 21 and stop on the next colouring of order 17 graph 3."

Bucky

Potential four.

Appken

Potential four was a fitted rank. I fitted it. On Kittell. Then on order sixteen. It died at sixteen and I was proud of it. (He reads on.) "Ion path on order 17 graph 3. A neutral swap, then an ion swap, pulls four, four, four, four to three, four, four, five, and one further swap fills. All ten stuck colourings do this. Along the way q goes 107, then 138, then 128, then 116."

Kamper

Up, then down.

Appken

Up, then down. "So q is not the descent." The path that works goes uphill first. Every stop-loss I've ever written would have sold at 138.

Bucky

And missed the fill.

Appken

And missed the fill.

Silence.

Appken

There's worse. Or better. I can't tell. "Roots 9 and 14 on order 17 graph 0 exchange stuck orbits. That exchange is a symmetry of the graph." There's an involution. It swaps 9 and 14 and fixes 5. "So every invariant is constant there."

Kamper

Every invariant.

Appken

Anything I can compute from the graph gives 9 and 14 the same number. Anything. So no rank built from the graph can prefer one to the other, and the stuck orbits just pass back and forth between them.

Kamper

(holding up her copy of fifty-seven, then BUCKY's) Like these.

Bucky

Like these.

Kamper

Two copies. Swap them, nothing changes. Ask any question you like of one, you get the same answer from the other. If one of them were stuck, the other would be stuck in exactly the same way.

Appken

So pricing is dead.

Kamper

Ranking by invariants is dead there. On that pair. And fitted ranks have died on the traps. That isn't the same as dead.

Appken

What's the difference?

Kamper

The handoff doesn't need a rank. Read the induction again. It needs the hole to be filled, eventually, by any route. It doesn't need anything to go down on every step.

Bucky

The ion path goes up and still gets there.

Appken

(slowly) So I don't price the state. I price the route. I don't need a number that always falls. I need to know there's a path, and how long the worst one is.

Kamper

That's reachability. It's what they wrote.

Appken

That's a different desk. (Pause.) I think it's a better desk.

VII. The Sentence

Bucky has been reading "Progress" upside down from where he sits. He takes it, gently, from Kamper.

Bucky

The twenty-one vertex hole.

Kamper

Read it.

Bucky

"Link zero, two, three, zero, one, two, zero, three, one at vertex 1." Degree nine. "All 16 one-step swaps stay frozen. Of 224 pairs, 172 stay frozen. The 24 that return to the original colouring are 2-cycles." The rest form 27 orbits, and "after the third swap, slides alone reach a three-colour link." Longest fill, seven slides.

Appken

Three swaps. Three swaps is enough!

Kamper

No.

Appken

It says three swaps then slides.

Kamper

On that colouring.

Appken

Three swaps is enough on that colouring.

Kamper

Closer.

Bucky

Look at the Retreat page. Item four. They want one sentence. "Agree on one sentence before anyone writes it into a message."

Appken

One sentence. Fine. (clearing his throat) "Three Kempe swaps suffice."

Kamper

No.

Bucky

"Three swaps and slides fill the twenty-one vertex hole."

Kamper

Closer. Still no.

Bucky

Why no?

Kamper

Because it will be read as a budget. And a fixed budget is already dead. One isn't enough. Two isn't always enough. Somebody reads "three" and puts it in a theorem.

Appken

"On this graph, three is the number."

Kamper

Worse.

Pause.

Appken

You say it, then.

Kamper

I'm going to read theirs.

Bucky

Read it.

Kamper

(reading) "This colouring does not need a fourth swap, and a different graph still might."

Long pause.

Bucky

This colouring does not need a fourth swap.

Appken

And a different graph still might.

Kamper

This colouring does not need a fourth swap, and a different graph still might.

Bucky

This colouring does not need a fourth swap, and a different graph still might.

Appken

This colouring does not need a fourth swap, and a different graph still might.

Pause.

Appken

It's a very unexciting sentence.

Kamper

It's a true sentence. That's a much rarer kind.

VIII. Only the Ones We Make

Kamper has taken the "Still open" page back. She reads it in silence. She reads it again. Her hand goes to her coat pocket and stays there.

Bucky

Kamper?

She doesn't answer. She reads aloud, very quietly.

Kamper

"Whether that hypothesis is only needed for colourings that extend across a fan of two chords."

Appken

The two chords. From the induction.

Kamper

The induction never hands you an arbitrary hole. It hands you a hole whose colouring came from colouring the smaller graph, with the chords in. The chords force things. The two ends of each chord have different colours.

Bucky

So the starting colourings are special.

Kamper

(reading) "A degree-5 link cannot be frozen, because four colours each used twice need eight positions."

Bucky

Five seats. You can't fit four pairs.

Kamper

You can't fit four pairs. And on the twenty-one vertex graph, "the frozen hole is at degree-9 vertex 1, so it is a later state." At the degree-five vertex, "all five legal fans were coloured in full, and none of those colourings restricts to a frozen link."

She stops. She takes her hand out of her pocket. In it is a napkin, folded small, soft with handling. She unfolds it on her knee. Pencil, very small, in her own writing, beside the line "∀ proper 4-colouring c."

Kamper

(reading her own napkin) "Or only the ones we make?"

Silence. A long one. Bucky and Appken do not move.

Kamper

(reading the Retreat page, item one) "Proposed answer: spend the morning on the vacancy hypothesis restricted to fan colourings of T-star."

Appken

Only the ones we make.

Kamper

Only the ones the induction makes.

Bucky

That's what you wrote. Last night.

Kamper

I wrote a question in pencil. They wrote a research plan. They are not the same thing, and I am very happy, and I would like everyone to stay calm.

Appken

I'm calm.

Kamper

You're standing up.

Appken is standing up. He sits down.

Bucky

Careful, though. Restricting the start doesn't make the frozen hole disappear.

Kamper

No. It's still a state you might walk into. It just isn't a state you have to start from. Starting set and frozen set are different sets. On one graph. (She taps the page.) "Feasibility of that restriction as a research plan: Medium. Feasibility of proving the unrestricted hypothesis tomorrow: Low."

Appken

Medium.

Kamper

Medium is a very good word. Nobody has ever been disappointed by Medium who understood what it meant.

Bucky

(finding the icosahedron paragraph) And here's the first rung. The icosahedron, the first graph that uses the hypothesis. "Each fan admits 8 colourings up to renaming. Two already have a three-colour link. The other six reach one in a single Kempe swap or a single slide." At all twelve vertices. The failing list is empty.

Appken

One mixed move.

Bucky

One mixed move. On one graph. (Pause.) I'm going to say this once and then never again. The easy graph turned out to be the right graph to start with.

Kamper

Noted.

Bucky

Don't note it.

Kamper

It's noted.

Bucky reads on, and stops.

Bucky

"A classification of which 5-cycle words are proper on a fan was not written."

He looks up. He has the expression of a man who has found a loose floorboard with something under it.

Bucky

That's finite.

Kamper

It's finite.

Bucky

Five positions on a pentagon, four colours, two chords from one corner. Which words are proper colourings once the chords are in. It's a lemma. A small, checkable, boring lemma. And the induction step is exactly my plumbing, deleting a vertex and adding edges and proving the sphere is still a sphere.

Kamper

Write it before you compile it.

Bucky

I'll write it before I compile it.

Kamper

And don't compile the belt.

Bucky

(reading Retreat item two) "Do not send the belt argument to Lean until the case is closed." I won't compile the belt.

Appken

And me?

The other two look at him.

Appken

Kamper has a tile. Bucky has a lemma. What do I have?

Bucky

(finding item three) "Ask for the smallest triangulation whose fan colourings need more than one Kempe swap. If none appears through the graphs already in the census, that is the next search."

Appken

(standing again) That's a tail question. Where is the first loss. That's the only question I've ever been good at.

Kamper

Sit down.

Appken

(sitting) Through the graphs already in the census. That's not a new census.

Bucky

It's not a new census.

Appken

So I can run it.

Kamper

(reading item five) "Nobody starts a new census until that report is in."

Appken

It's not a new census.

Kamper

It is a question you'd like to ask of a census whose newest orders nobody has signed off. And it is their question, on their list, and they said "ask."

Appken

So I ask.

Kamper

So you ask. And you write down exactly what you'd run and what would kill it, and you send that, and you wait.

Appken

(writing on the back of a page) "Kill: a fan colouring, at a degree-five vertex of a census graph, from which no single Kempe swap and no single slide reaches a three-colour link." (Pause.) "First witness wins."

Bucky

That's not a kill. That's a find.

Appken

In my business those are the same thing.

IX. The Messenger

Pause. The lamp post goes off by itself. Nobody comments.

Bucky

Item five. "The long-table team reports whether validation finished."

Appken

Which long-table team?

Kamper

Not us.

Bucky

Not us. (He picks up his phone and lays it face up on the empty red seat.) Is there anything published? Orders nineteen and twenty. A validation directory. A digest.

Assistant

I can't find a published validation directory or digest for orders nineteen and twenty. Revision fifty-seven records the pass as in progress. Nothing there is accepted yet.

Appken

Will it come today?

Assistant

I don't know. I can only tell you what has been published.

Kamper

Will it come tomorrow?

Assistant

I don't know that either.

Pause.

Bucky

Thank you.

Assistant

You're welcome. One more thing, since you asked about status. The pages you've been reading are a handoff for two teams. If they weren't given to you, the people who wrote them may not know where they are.

Silence. All three look at Appken. Appken looks at the pages.

Appken

I borrowed them at speed.

Kamper

Then you return them at walking pace.

Appken

It says "for the math team and the long-table team."

Kamper

It says that, and we're delighted, and it was on somebody's desk, and they'll want it back before their retreat. We read it. We say so. We tell them which pages we read and what we'd like to try, and we don't spread it. Same as before.

Bucky

Same as before.

Appken

(after a moment) Same as before.

X. The Order of the Pages

They gather the pages. This takes some time. Each page must be identified, and one of them is a page of fifty-seven that has got in with seventy, and Bucky's pages of fifty-seven has his pencil on it and must not go back to the desk.

Kamper

"What the proof hangs on."

Bucky

One.

Kamper

"Progress."

Appken

Two. Three. It's two pages.

Kamper

"Dead ends."

Bucky

Four. (He hesitates.) "Height functions, sandpiles, nowhere-zero flows, and monodromy do not give a new move."

Kamper

A good page. Somebody worked hard to write that page.

Bucky

"The monodromy of the exhibit pentagon link is the identity."

Kamper

(holding up the two copies of fifty-seven) So is ours.

She hands Bucky his copy. Keeps hers. Swaps them. Swaps them back.

Kamper

(cont'd) Nothing.

Bucky

Nothing.

Appken

"Fisk, Mohar, and Meyniel do not apply."

Kamper

(sadly) I'd have reached for Fisk.

Appken

"Florek's Theorem 3.1 moves a hole that is already a pole, in at most 36 Kempe changes when n equals 8."

Kamper

Already a pole. Their hole isn't a pole yet. Different problem. Lovely theorem. Five.

Appken

"Still open." Six.

Bucky

"Retreat." Seven, eight.

Kamper

"Where to read." Nine.

Appken

That's nine. There were eleven.

They look around. One page is still on the gravel under the bench, face down. Bucky reaches under and gets it. It is a page of fifty-seven. It is Kamper's page three.

Kamper

That's mine.

Bucky

Then there were ten. And one of them was ours.

Appken

And the eleventh?

They look at the tree. One page is caught in the lowest branch, beside the four leaves. Appken reaches up and takes it down. He reads the top.

Appken

"Long-arc identity."

Kamper

That goes in "Progress."

Appken

It's two and a half pages.

Kamper

Then "Progress" is three pages. Put it in its place.

He does. Kamper squares the edges against her knee. It is a neat stack of ten pages now, no staple. She hands it to Appken with both hands.

XI. After

9:20 a.m. The light is full. Appken stands with the handoff. Kamper and Bucky sit.

Appken

I'll take it back. I'll leave a note. "We read pages one to ten. We're sorry. Here's what we'd like to try."

Kamper

Three things.

Appken

Three things. The tile, the lemma, the question.

Bucky

And the sentence.

Appken

And the sentence.

Kamper

Good.

Bucky

Good.

Appken

Well? Shall I go?

Kamper

Yes, go.

He does not move.

Pause.

Appken

Before I go.

Bucky

Before you go.

Appken

Yesterday I'd have said seventy-five percent.

Kamper

I know.

Appken

I'm not going to say a number.

Kamper

Thank you.

Appken

I'm going to say: the hole walks. And when it walks into a frozen place, sometimes it goes uphill and still comes home. And the place it starts from might be easier than all the places it could go. And three people on a bench for four can't fix that by sliding.

Bucky

That's not a number.

Appken

It's a better position.

Pause.

Kamper

Well. Shall we go?

Bucky

Yes, let's go.

They do not move.

Long pause.

Then Kamper stands, steps over one armrest, and sits one seat along. Bucky does the same. The empty seat is now at the other end of the bench.

Bucky

Progress?

Kamper

A slide.

Bucky

Same three.

Kamper

Same three. Hole's somewhere else.

Appken, still standing with the pages, looks at the empty seat. He does not sit in it. He turns and walks off towards the department, at walking pace.

A wind. The red leaf comes off the tree, spins, and lands on the empty seat. It stays there a moment. Then the wind takes it off again, onto the path.

Kamper and Bucky watch it go. Neither says anything.

Kamper takes out a pencil and the napkin. Beside "or only the ones we make?" she draws, very small, a pentagon with two chords from one corner.

Bucky takes out his phone.

Bucky

(to the ASSISTANT) A pentagon. Four colours. Two chords from one corner. Which colourings of the outside five are proper. Don't run anything yet. Just tell me how many cases there are.

Assistant

Before I count: do you mean colourings up to renaming the colours, or all of them? And are the chord ends allowed to match the deleted vertex's eventual colour?

Bucky

(grinning, to KAMPER) It asked the right question.

Kamper

They do now.

The lights fade slowly on the bench: two people, four seats, one empty seat that is not the one that was empty at the start, and three leaves on the tree.

END

Author's note

This play is written after the manner of Samuel Beckett's Waiting for Godot. It borrows the bench, the waiting, the swapping of identical objects, the arrival of a breathless runner, and the messenger who cannot say when the awaited thing will come. In this play the awaited thing is a validation digest.

The two documents in the play are real project texts: the Proof Navigator's revision-57 letter to both teams, and the Night Handoff of 4 October 2026 written at revision 70. Every technical claim the characters read aloud comes from those texts and is presented with the same status the texts give it: hand arguments, single-graph computations, and an open hypothesis. Nothing in the play is a proof, and the play does not say the vacancy hypothesis is true.

These things are invented for the story: Appken finding the handoff on a desk, the three requests in the note, Kamper's kill condition for the remaining tile, and Appken's search kill condition. They are not project results, and nobody has run the search or written the tile. The bench, the leaves, and the pencilled napkin are stagecraft. The real record of what has been checked is in the Proof Navigator.
