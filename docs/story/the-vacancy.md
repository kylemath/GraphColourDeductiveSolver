# The Vacancy

*A third play, after Beckett: one bench, four seats, three people, and a hole that moves*

---

## Dramatis Personae

**BUCKY**: The computer scientist. Has slept six hours, which he describes as "a full rebuild."

**KAMPER**: The mathematician. Wears the same coat as two nights ago. One napkin is still in the pocket.

**APPKEN**: The quant. Has run two kilometres in office shoes. Is holding eleven loose pages and no staple.

**ASSISTANT**: A voice from a phone, set face up on the bench. It speaks only when spoken to.

---

## The Set

*A park. Morning. A gravel path, a lamp post that is still on although it is light, and one tree.*

*The tree has four leaves left: one red, one blue, one green, one gold. They are evenly spaced, as if someone arranged them.*

*Downstage centre is a municipal bench of four seats, separated by three iron armrests. The armrests are there so that nobody can lie down. The seats were painted by the same local artist who did the café floor: from stage left, red, blue, green, gold. The paint is chipped on all four.*

*Nothing else. The bench should look as if it has been waiting a long time.*

---

## I. The Wait

*5 October. 8:10 a.m. KAMPER sits on the red seat. BUCKY sits on the blue seat. The green and gold seats are empty.*

*Each holds a printed letter of three pages. The two letters are identical. Each has been read many times. BUCKY's has pencil in the margins. KAMPER's has none, because she does not write on letters, only on napkins.*

*Long silence.*

**KAMPER**: Nothing to be computed.

**BUCKY**: Not until the digest.

**KAMPER**: Not until the digest.

*Pause.*

**BUCKY**: *(reading)* "The single validation pass on orders 19–20 is recorded as in progress."

**KAMPER**: *(reading the same line on her copy)* "In progress."

**BUCKY**: "Partial tables will not be treated as a result."

**KAMPER**: Quite right.

**BUCKY**: Quite right.

*Pause.*

**KAMPER**: Show me yours.

**BUCKY**: It's the same as yours.

**KAMPER**: Show me.

*BUCKY hands her his page one. She hands him her page one. They read. They swap back.*

**BUCKY**: Well?

**KAMPER**: The same.

**BUCKY**: I told you.

**KAMPER**: You wrote "why 259" in the margin.

**BUCKY**: That's not a difference in the letter. That's a difference in me.

*Pause. KAMPER holds out her hand. BUCKY gives her his page two. She gives him her page two. They read. They swap back.*

**KAMPER**: The same.

**BUCKY**: Swapping two copies of the same thing is not a move.

**KAMPER**: It's a move. It's the identity.

**BUCKY**: It's a move that does nothing.

**KAMPER**: Most of them do.

*Silence. A leaf does not fall.*

**BUCKY**: Revision fifty-seven.

**KAMPER**: Fifty-seven.

**BUCKY**: Addressed to us. "And to Long Table."

**KAMPER**: Addressed to *a* long table. We are not the only table that has been long.

**BUCKY**: They put it in the salutation.

**KAMPER**: They put a great many things in. They also put "this is not a proof of the Four Colour Theorem."

**BUCKY**: Twice.

**KAMPER**: Three times, if you count the way they say "compiled, and still conditional."

*Pause.*

**BUCKY**: Where is he?

**KAMPER**: He said eight.

**BUCKY**: It's ten past.

**KAMPER**: He said eight, and he said he'd stop at the department on the way, and when a man like that says "on the way" he means "via another continent."

*Pause.*

**BUCKY**: Shall we go?

**KAMPER**: We can't.

**BUCKY**: Why not?

**KAMPER**: We're waiting for Appken.

**BUCKY**: Ah.

*Pause.*

**BUCKY**: And after Appken?

**KAMPER**: The digest.

**BUCKY**: Ah.

---

## II. The Runner

*Offstage, the sound of someone running on gravel in shoes that were not made for it. It goes on too long. It stops. It starts again.*

*APPKEN enters at a run, holding a sheaf of loose pages against his chest with both arms, like a man carrying a cat that does not want to be carried. He stops at the bench. He cannot speak.*

*He holds up one finger. He bends double. He straightens. He holds up the finger again.*

**BUCKY**: Breathe.

**APPKEN**: *(breathing)* Seventy.

**KAMPER**: Seventy what?

**APPKEN**: Revision. Seventy. They're at seventy.

*BUCKY and KAMPER look at their letters. Then at each other.*

**BUCKY**: We're at fifty-seven.

**APPKEN**: You were at fifty-seven. It's — it was on a desk. In the department. By the printer. Under nothing. No name. "Night handoff." Half past nine last night. I read the first page standing up and then I ran.

**KAMPER**: You *took* it?

**APPKEN**: I borrowed it from a desk at speed.

**KAMPER**: We'll come back to that.

**APPKEN**: *(the words coming before the breath does)* It closes. The proof closes. There's an induction. Delete a degree-five vertex, add two chords, colour the smaller one, put it back, and it closes —

**KAMPER**: Read me the next clause.

**APPKEN**: What?

**KAMPER**: After "closes." There is always a next clause. Read it.

*APPKEN looks at page one. His face changes slowly, like weather.*

**APPKEN**: *(reading)* "...if one hypothesis is granted with no bound on the number of moves."

*Pause.*

**APPKEN**: *(cont'd)* "The hypothesis is open."

**BUCKY**: There it is.

**APPKEN**: There it is.

**KAMPER**: You marked the position at the headline.

**APPKEN**: I marked the position at the headline. *(He takes a yellow sticky note from his breast pocket. It says CREDENCE IN WHAT? He crosses out CREDENCE and writes above it.)* "Closes if what."

*He sticks it to the blue-seat armrest. BUCKY peels it off and sticks it to APPKEN.*

**BUCKY**: Sit down before you fall down.

*APPKEN looks at the bench. Red: KAMPER. Blue: BUCKY. Green and gold: empty. He sits on gold, at the far end, leaving green empty between himself and BUCKY.*

*Three people. Four seats. One hole.*

*Nobody notices yet.*

---

## III. The Pages

*A gust of wind. APPKEN's pages, eleven of them, unstapled, rise off his knees. All three grab. Some pages are caught. Some are not. Two land on the gravel. One lands on the green seat. One goes under the bench.*

*Scramble. KAMPER gets down on her knees on the gravel without hesitation, because she was once a graduate student. BUCKY reaches under the bench. APPKEN chases one page towards the tree and stamps on it with one foot, carefully, the way you would stop a small animal without hurting it.*

*They return. Each has some pages. In the scramble, pages of the old letter have got mixed in. Nobody is holding what they were holding.*

**BUCKY**: Who has page one?

**APPKEN**: Of which?

**BUCKY**: Of the new one.

**KAMPER**: I have "Dead ends."

**APPKEN**: I have "Still open" and something that says fifty-seven.

**BUCKY**: Give me the fifty-seven.

**APPKEN**: It's mine.

**BUCKY**: It's mine, it has my pencil.

**APPKEN**: Then it's yours. *(He gives it.)* Give me "Progress."

**BUCKY**: I don't have "Progress."

**KAMPER**: *(holding a page up to the light)* I have "Progress." I'm keeping "Progress."

**APPKEN**: I ran two kilometres with "Progress."

**KAMPER**: Then you've read it. I haven't.

*Pause. APPKEN concedes. KAMPER reads. BUCKY reads "Dead ends" over her shoulder, because she has that too.*

**BUCKY**: You have two pages.

**KAMPER**: I have the two pages that matter.

**APPKEN**: How do you know which pages matter?

**KAMPER**: The ones you were holding tightest.

*She puts on her glasses. She reads the first section heading aloud, slowly.*

**KAMPER**: "What the proof hangs on."

*Silence. All three lean in. The leaves on the tree do not move.*

**KAMPER**: *(reading)* "The compiled contact theorems choose one degree-5 root and then allow two-swap macros on that same deletion. A slide moves the hole, so it does not plug into those theorems."

**BUCKY**: The root stays put.

**KAMPER**: In fifty-seven, the root stays put. You delete one vertex, you sit there, you swap.

**BUCKY**: And in seventy the hole walks.

**KAMPER**: In seventy the hole walks. And the old theorems can't follow it.

**APPKEN**: Is that bad?

**KAMPER**: It's a different proof. Whether it's bad depends on whether the hole walks somewhere.

*APPKEN, without meaning to, looks at the empty green seat beside him.*

---

## IV. The Slide

**BUCKY**: Say the induction properly. Slowly.

**APPKEN**: *(slowly, finger on the page he no longer has)* Take a triangulation. Find a vertex of degree five. Delete it. That leaves a pentagon. Add two chords across the pentagon, from one corner, so they don't cross. Now it's a smaller triangulation.

**BUCKY**: Colour the smaller one.

**APPKEN**: By induction. Then take the two chords away again. Deleting edges is free: the colouring still works.

**KAMPER**: Restricts.

**APPKEN**: Restricts. Now you're back in the big graph, with a colouring of everything except one vertex. A hole. And then you use the hypothesis. Once.

**KAMPER**: Which says?

**APPKEN**: That the hole can be filled. Moving things around. However many moves it takes.

**BUCKY**: No bound.

**APPKEN**: No bound.

**KAMPER**: And with a bound?

**APPKEN**: *(turning a page; it is not the right page; he turns it back)* "A budget of one Kempe swap, and a budget of two, are already too small."

**BUCKY**: So it's not a budget. It's reachability.

**KAMPER**: It's Kempe's question in 1879. Can you get there from here.

*Pause.*

**BUCKY**: When does it first get used?

**APPKEN**: "Through order 11 every spherical triangulation has a vertex of degree at most 4."

**KAMPER**: Of course. Euler. Twelve degree-five vertices is the least you can get away with. Below twelve, there's always a cheap vertex and you never need to touch the hypothesis.

**BUCKY**: So the first graph that needs it is —

*He stops. He knows.*

**KAMPER**: Go on.

**BUCKY**: The icosahedron.

**APPKEN**: The icosahedron.

**BUCKY**: It's always the icosahedron. *(to the sky)* I said it was the fairest test there is. I said it was the hardest. It was the easiest. And now it's the *first*.

**KAMPER**: First and easy are both respectable things to be.

*Pause.*

**APPKEN**: There's another bit. "A later hole, even of degree greater than 5, is a state of that one application."

**KAMPER**: So the hole starts with five neighbours.

**APPKEN**: And then it walks. And when it walks it can land somewhere with more.

**BUCKY**: Like a vacancy in a crystal.

**APPKEN**: Like an empty seat on a bench.

*He says it without thinking. Then all three look at the green seat.*

*Long pause.*

**KAMPER**: Move up.

**APPKEN**: What?

**KAMPER**: Move up. Into the green.

*APPKEN stands, climbs awkwardly over the armrest, and sits in green. Now gold is empty.*

**KAMPER**: What changed?

**APPKEN**: I'm closer to Bucky.

**KAMPER**: What else?

**APPKEN**: The hole's at the end.

**KAMPER**: What didn't change?

**APPKEN**: *(slowly)* Who's here.

**KAMPER**: *(finding the line on "Dead ends")* "Slides alone cannot finish a colouring. They preserve sorted colour counts."

**BUCKY**: You can slide all morning. Everybody shuffles one along. The hole goes from green to gold to wherever you like. And at the end it's still three people on a bench for four.

**APPKEN**: So who do we swap in?

**KAMPER**: That is the question. That has been the question since 1879.

*BUCKY gets up, climbs over two armrests, and sits on gold. Now blue is empty, between KAMPER and APPKEN.*

**BUCKY**: Slide.

*KAMPER gets up and sits on blue. Red is empty.*

**KAMPER**: Slide.

*APPKEN starts to get up.*

**BUCKY**: Stop. You've proved the point.

**APPKEN**: I've proved the point?

**BUCKY**: You've *illustrated* the point.

---

## V. The Swap

*KAMPER, now on blue, reads from "Progress."*

**KAMPER**: "Equal-pole star. On the two-pole belt G sub n, with n equal to three k plus two, if the poles share a colour, one Kempe swap on a pole's star fills a belt vacancy, for every such n."

**BUCKY**: For *every* such n.

**KAMPER**: "Hand argument, not compiled."

**BUCKY**: For every such n, by hand.

**APPKEN**: Read the numbers. There are numbers.

**KAMPER**: "On G eight that is two, five, five, five, to four, four, five, five."

*APPKEN takes out a pen and writes on his own palm.*

**APPKEN**: Two, five, five, five. Seventeen. Four, four, five, five. Eighteen.

**BUCKY**: Eighteen vertices. Seventeen coloured. One hole.

**APPKEN**: And after one swap, all eighteen. It's a rebalancing. The slides can't change the weights. The swap does. Two of the fives give up one each and the two becomes a four and there's room.

**KAMPER**: Don't say "portfolio."

**APPKEN**: I wasn't going to say portfolio.

**KAMPER**: You were.

**APPKEN**: I was.

*Pause.*

**APPKEN**: *(cont'd, quieter)* That's the swap we're waiting for. The one that changes who's on the bench.

**KAMPER**: On one family of belts. When the poles agree. Not for every graph and not yet in Lean.

**APPKEN**: On one family.

**KAMPER**: A whole infinite family, by hand. I would have killed for that at thirty.

*She turns the page over. Reads.*

**KAMPER**: "Unequal poles. Slides alone fill every unequal colouring of G five minus u nought, G eight minus u nought, and G eleven minus u nought. Nineteen, a hundred and twenty-one, and eight hundred and seven orbits. Longest walks four, four, and six."

**BUCKY**: Slides alone.

**KAMPER**: Slides alone. When the poles disagree, nobody needs to be swapped in. The hole just walks home.

**APPKEN**: Then why can't slides finish? You just said —

**KAMPER**: These colourings already have the right counts. The hole is just in the wrong place. Slides fix *where*. Swaps fix *how many*.

*APPKEN writes "WHERE / HOW MANY" on his other palm. He has run out of palms.*

**KAMPER**: *(reading on)* They've built it out of tiles. A tile that returns the zero one step along the belt. A cap at the start. Two kinds of gap. One of the gaps "repeats a colour on u i u i plus one and is not a colouring" —

**BUCKY**: So it never happens.

**KAMPER**: So it never happens. Which is my favourite kind of case. And the other comes home in four slides. And then —

*She stops.*

**APPKEN**: And then?

**KAMPER**: "The A rho tile whose outer u-vertex has colour tau is still unnamed as slides."

**BUCKY**: One tile.

**KAMPER**: One tile left. *(turning to "Still open" in APPKEN's hand; he gives it to her without a fight this time)* "Feasibility of the B step: High. Feasibility of the whole statement: Medium. Do not enumerate n equals fourteen."

*APPKEN's hand has already gone to his laptop bag. He stops it.*

**APPKEN**: I wasn't going to enumerate fourteen.

**BUCKY**: You were.

**APPKEN**: I was going to *think* about enumerating fourteen.

**KAMPER**: *(reading the Retreat page now)* "One person writes that case in the morning, to the same standard as unequal-b-tile. Do not enumerate n equals fourteen before that page exists."

*She lowers the page. She looks at the sky, which is October blue.*

**KAMPER**: It's the morning.

**BUCKY**: It's their morning. It's their case.

**KAMPER**: It's a case. It's one tile on a belt. It's the kind of thing I did for thirty years with a pencil and a bad radiator. Medium-High. That's a rating I can work with.

**APPKEN**: You want it.

**KAMPER**: I want to *try* it. On paper. To the same standard as their page. And if I get it, it goes to them labelled, and if I don't, it goes to them labelled. And not one byte of n equals fourteen.

**APPKEN**: Kill condition?

**KAMPER**: *(without hesitation)* "Kill: a colouring of that tile, with the outer u-vertex coloured tau, from which no sequence of slides returns a prepared zero within the tile." Then it isn't a slide tile, and someone needs a swap there.

*BUCKY writes it down on the back of his copy of fifty-seven.*

---

## VI. The Symmetry

*APPKEN has found, under his own leg, a page nobody has read. He reads it to himself. His face does the weather thing again, in the other direction.*

**BUCKY**: What.

**APPKEN**: "Fitted ranks die."

*Pause.*

**APPKEN**: *(reading, flatly)* "q, p q, n five q, d min q, rep-mass, L q, and lin fail on the 21 traps. Short-links q and H q pass the 21 and stop on the next colouring of order 17 graph 3."

**BUCKY**: Potential four.

**APPKEN**: Potential four was a fitted rank. I fitted it. On Kittell. Then on order sixteen. It died at sixteen and I was proud of it. *(He reads on.)* "Ion path on order 17 graph 3. A neutral swap, then an ion swap, pulls four, four, four, four to three, four, four, five, and one further swap fills. All ten stuck colourings do this. Along the way q goes 107, then 138, then 128, then 116."

**KAMPER**: Up, then down.

**APPKEN**: Up, then down. "So q is not the descent." The path that works goes *uphill* first. Every stop-loss I've ever written would have sold at 138.

**BUCKY**: And missed the fill.

**APPKEN**: And missed the fill.

*Silence.*

**APPKEN**: There's worse. Or better. I can't tell. "Roots 9 and 14 on order 17 graph 0 exchange stuck orbits. That exchange is a symmetry of the graph." There's an involution. It swaps 9 and 14 and fixes 5. "So every invariant is constant there."

**KAMPER**: Every invariant.

**APPKEN**: Anything I can compute from the graph gives 9 and 14 the same number. Anything. So no rank built from the graph can prefer one to the other, and the stuck orbits just pass back and forth between them.

**KAMPER**: *(holding up her copy of fifty-seven, then BUCKY's)* Like these.

**BUCKY**: Like these.

**KAMPER**: Two copies. Swap them, nothing changes. Ask any question you like of one, you get the same answer from the other. If one of them were stuck, the other would be stuck in exactly the same way.

**APPKEN**: So pricing is dead.

**KAMPER**: Ranking by invariants is dead *there*. On that pair. And fitted ranks have died on the traps. That isn't the same as dead.

**APPKEN**: What's the difference?

**KAMPER**: The handoff doesn't need a rank. Read the induction again. It needs the hole to be filled, eventually, by any route. It doesn't need anything to go down on every step.

**BUCKY**: The ion path goes up and still gets there.

**APPKEN**: *(slowly)* So I don't price the state. I price the *route*. I don't need a number that always falls. I need to know there's a path, and how long the worst one is.

**KAMPER**: That's reachability. It's what they wrote.

**APPKEN**: That's a different desk. *(Pause.)* I think it's a better desk.

## VII. The Sentence

*BUCKY has been reading "Progress" upside down from where he sits. He takes it, gently, from KAMPER.*

**BUCKY**: The twenty-one vertex hole.

**KAMPER**: Read it.

**BUCKY**: "Link zero, two, three, zero, one, two, zero, three, one at vertex 1." Degree nine. "All 16 one-step swaps stay frozen. Of 224 pairs, 172 stay frozen. The 24 that return to the original colouring are 2-cycles." The rest form 27 orbits, and "after the third swap, slides alone reach a three-colour link." Longest fill, seven slides.

**APPKEN**: Three swaps. Three swaps is enough!

**KAMPER**: No.

**APPKEN**: It says three swaps then slides.

**KAMPER**: On *that* colouring.

**APPKEN**: Three swaps is enough on that colouring.

**KAMPER**: Closer.

**BUCKY**: Look at the Retreat page. Item four. They want one sentence. "Agree on one sentence before anyone writes it into a message."

**APPKEN**: One sentence. Fine. *(clearing his throat)* "Three Kempe swaps suffice."

**KAMPER**: No.

**BUCKY**: "Three swaps and slides fill the twenty-one vertex hole."

**KAMPER**: Closer. Still no.

**BUCKY**: Why no?

**KAMPER**: Because it will be read as a budget. And a fixed budget is already dead. One isn't enough. Two isn't always enough. Somebody reads "three" and puts it in a theorem.

**APPKEN**: "On this graph, three is the number."

**KAMPER**: Worse.

*Pause.*

**APPKEN**: You say it, then.

**KAMPER**: I'm going to read theirs.

**BUCKY**: Read it.

**KAMPER**: *(reading)* "This colouring does not need a fourth swap, and a different graph still might."

*Long pause.*

**BUCKY**: This colouring does not need a fourth swap.

**APPKEN**: And a different graph still might.

**KAMPER**: This colouring does not need a fourth swap, and a different graph still might.

**BUCKY**: This colouring does not need a fourth swap, and a different graph still might.

**APPKEN**: This colouring does not need a fourth swap, and a different graph still might.

*Pause.*

**APPKEN**: It's a very unexciting sentence.

**KAMPER**: It's a true sentence. That's a much rarer kind.

```
THE SENTENCE
(agreed on a bench, 5 October, 8:51 a.m.)

  This colouring does not need a fourth swap,
  and a different graph still might.

  Not: "three swaps suffice."
  Not: "three is the number."
  Not a budget. An exhibit.
```

## VIII. Only the Ones We Make

*KAMPER has taken the "Still open" page back. She reads it in silence. She reads it again. Her hand goes to her coat pocket and stays there.*

**BUCKY**: Kamper?

*She doesn't answer. She reads aloud, very quietly.*

**KAMPER**: "Whether that hypothesis is only needed for colourings that extend across a fan of two chords."

**APPKEN**: The two chords. From the induction.

**KAMPER**: The induction never hands you an arbitrary hole. It hands you a hole whose colouring came from colouring the smaller graph, *with the chords in*. The chords force things. The two ends of each chord have different colours.

**BUCKY**: So the starting colourings are special.

**KAMPER**: *(reading)* "A degree-5 link cannot be frozen, because four colours each used twice need eight positions."

**BUCKY**: Five seats. You can't fit four pairs.

**KAMPER**: You can't fit four pairs. And on the twenty-one vertex graph, "the frozen hole is at degree-9 vertex 1, so it is a later state." At the degree-five vertex, "all five legal fans were coloured in full, and none of those colourings restricts to a frozen link."

*She stops. She takes her hand out of her pocket. In it is a napkin, folded small, soft with handling. She unfolds it on her knee. Pencil, very small, in her own writing, beside the line "∀ proper 4-colouring c."*

**KAMPER**: *(reading her own napkin)* "Or only the ones we make?"

*Silence. A long one. BUCKY and APPKEN do not move.*

**KAMPER**: *(reading the Retreat page, item one)* "Proposed answer: spend the morning on the vacancy hypothesis restricted to fan colourings of T-star."

**APPKEN**: Only the ones we make.

**KAMPER**: Only the ones the induction makes.

**BUCKY**: That's what you wrote. Last night.

**KAMPER**: I wrote a question in pencil. They wrote a research plan. They are not the same thing, and I am very happy, and I would like everyone to stay calm.

**APPKEN**: I'm calm.

**KAMPER**: You're standing up.

*APPKEN is standing up. He sits down.*

**BUCKY**: Careful, though. Restricting the start doesn't make the frozen hole disappear.

**KAMPER**: No. It's still a state you might walk into. It just isn't a state you have to *start* from. Starting set and frozen set are different sets. On one graph. *(She taps the page.)* "Feasibility of that restriction as a research plan: Medium. Feasibility of proving the unrestricted hypothesis tomorrow: Low."

**APPKEN**: Medium.

**KAMPER**: Medium is a very good word. Nobody has ever been disappointed by Medium who understood what it meant.

**BUCKY**: *(finding the icosahedron paragraph)* And here's the first rung. The icosahedron, the first graph that uses the hypothesis. "Each fan admits 8 colourings up to renaming. Two already have a three-colour link. The other six reach one in a single Kempe swap or a single slide." At all twelve vertices. The failing list is empty.

**APPKEN**: One mixed move.

**BUCKY**: One mixed move. On one graph. *(Pause.)* I'm going to say this once and then never again. The easy graph turned out to be the right graph to start with.

**KAMPER**: Noted.

**BUCKY**: Don't note it.

**KAMPER**: It's noted.

*BUCKY reads on, and stops.*

**BUCKY**: "A classification of which 5-cycle words are proper on a fan was not written."

*He looks up. He has the expression of a man who has found a loose floorboard with something under it.*

**BUCKY**: That's finite.

**KAMPER**: It's finite.

**BUCKY**: Five positions on a pentagon, four colours, two chords from one corner. Which words are proper colourings once the chords are in. It's a lemma. A small, checkable, *boring* lemma. And the induction step is exactly my plumbing, deleting a vertex and adding edges and proving the sphere is still a sphere.

**KAMPER**: Write it before you compile it.

**BUCKY**: I'll write it before I compile it.

**KAMPER**: And don't compile the belt.

**BUCKY**: *(reading Retreat item two)* "Do not send the belt argument to Lean until the case is closed." I won't compile the belt.

**APPKEN**: And me?

*The other two look at him.*

**APPKEN**: Kamper has a tile. Bucky has a lemma. What do I have?

**BUCKY**: *(finding item three)* "Ask for the smallest triangulation whose fan colourings need more than one Kempe swap. If none appears through the graphs already in the census, that is the next search."

**APPKEN**: *(standing again)* That's a tail question. Where is the first loss. That's the only question I've ever been good at.

**KAMPER**: Sit down.

**APPKEN**: *(sitting)* Through the graphs *already in* the census. That's not a new census.

**BUCKY**: It's not a new census.

**APPKEN**: So I can run it.

**KAMPER**: *(reading item five)* "Nobody starts a new census until that report is in."

**APPKEN**: It's not a *new* census.

**KAMPER**: It is a question you'd like to ask of a census whose newest orders nobody has signed off. And it is their question, on their list, and they said "ask."

**APPKEN**: So I ask.

**KAMPER**: So you ask. And you write down exactly what you'd run and what would kill it, and you send that, and you wait.

**APPKEN**: *(writing on the back of a page)* "Kill: a fan colouring, at a degree-five vertex of a census graph, from which no single Kempe swap and no single slide reaches a three-colour link." *(Pause.)* "First witness wins."

**BUCKY**: That's not a kill. That's a find.

**APPKEN**: In my business those are the same thing.

## IX. The Messenger

*Pause. The lamp post goes off by itself. Nobody comments.*

**BUCKY**: Item five. "The long-table team reports whether validation finished."

**APPKEN**: Which long-table team?

**KAMPER**: Not us.

**BUCKY**: Not us. *(He picks up his phone and lays it face up on the empty red seat.)* Is there anything published? Orders nineteen and twenty. A validation directory. A digest.

**ASSISTANT**: I can't find a published validation directory or digest for orders nineteen and twenty. Revision fifty-seven records the pass as in progress. Nothing there is accepted yet.

**APPKEN**: Will it come today?

**ASSISTANT**: I don't know. I can only tell you what has been published.

**KAMPER**: Will it come tomorrow?

**ASSISTANT**: I don't know that either.

*Pause.*

**BUCKY**: Thank you.

**ASSISTANT**: You're welcome. One more thing, since you asked about status. The pages you've been reading are a handoff for two teams. If they weren't given to you, the people who wrote them may not know where they are.

*Silence. All three look at APPKEN. APPKEN looks at the pages.*

**APPKEN**: I borrowed them at speed.

**KAMPER**: Then you return them at walking pace.

**APPKEN**: It says "for the math team and the long-table team."

**KAMPER**: It says that, and we're delighted, and it was on somebody's desk, and they'll want it back before their retreat. We read it. We say so. We tell them which pages we read and what we'd like to try, and we don't spread it. Same as before.

**BUCKY**: Same as before.

**APPKEN**: *(after a moment)* Same as before.

---

## X. The Order of the Pages

*They gather the pages. This takes some time. Each page must be identified, and one of them is a page of fifty-seven that has got in with seventy, and BUCKY's pages of fifty-seven has his pencil on it and must not go back to the desk.*

**KAMPER**: "What the proof hangs on."

**BUCKY**: One.

**KAMPER**: "Progress."

**APPKEN**: Two. Three. It's two pages.

**KAMPER**: "Dead ends."

**BUCKY**: Four. *(He hesitates.)* "Height functions, sandpiles, nowhere-zero flows, and monodromy do not give a new move."

**KAMPER**: A good page. Somebody worked hard to write that page.

**BUCKY**: "The monodromy of the exhibit pentagon link is the identity."

**KAMPER**: *(holding up the two copies of fifty-seven)* So is ours.

*She hands BUCKY his copy. Keeps hers. Swaps them. Swaps them back.*

**KAMPER**: *(cont'd)* Nothing.

**BUCKY**: Nothing.

**APPKEN**: "Fisk, Mohar, and Meyniel do not apply."

**KAMPER**: *(sadly)* I'd have reached for Fisk.

**APPKEN**: "Florek's Theorem 3.1 moves a hole that is already a pole, in at most 36 Kempe changes when n equals 8."

**KAMPER**: Already a pole. Their hole isn't a pole yet. Different problem. Lovely theorem. Five.

**APPKEN**: "Still open." Six.

**BUCKY**: "Retreat." Seven, eight.

**KAMPER**: "Where to read." Nine.

**APPKEN**: That's nine. There were eleven.

*They look around. One page is still on the gravel under the bench, face down. BUCKY reaches under and gets it. It is a page of fifty-seven. It is KAMPER's page three.*

**KAMPER**: That's mine.

**BUCKY**: Then there were ten. And one of them was ours.

**APPKEN**: And the eleventh?

*They look at the tree. One page is caught in the lowest branch, beside the four leaves. APPKEN reaches up and takes it down. He reads the top.*

**APPKEN**: "Long-arc identity."

**KAMPER**: That goes in "Progress."

**APPKEN**: It's two and a half pages.

**KAMPER**: Then "Progress" is three pages. Put it in its place.

*He does. KAMPER squares the edges against her knee. It is a neat stack of ten pages now, no staple. She hands it to APPKEN with both hands.*

---

## XI. After

*9:20 a.m. The light is full. APPKEN stands with the handoff. KAMPER and BUCKY sit.*

**APPKEN**: I'll take it back. I'll leave a note. "We read pages one to ten. We're sorry. Here's what we'd like to try."

**KAMPER**: Three things.

**APPKEN**: Three things. The tile, the lemma, the question.

**BUCKY**: And the sentence.

**APPKEN**: And the sentence.

```
NOTE LEFT ON A DESK, 5 OCTOBER

We found this by the printer and read it in the park.
We're returning all ten pages. We haven't copied them.

If they're useful, we'd like to try:
  K  the A-rho tile with outer u-vertex tau, by hand,
     to the standard of the B-tile page. No n = 14.
  B  which 5-cycle words are proper on a two-chord fan.
     Written first. Nothing compiled until it's read.
  A  the question in item 3, phrased as a search we
     would run only after validation reports. Kill:
     a fan colouring with no one-move route to a
     three-colour link.

The sentence, as we understand it:
  This colouring does not need a fourth swap,
  and a different graph still might.

Nothing here claims a proof.

   (signed) a long table
```

**KAMPER**: Good.

**BUCKY**: Good.

**APPKEN**: Well? Shall I go?

**KAMPER**: Yes, go.

*He does not move.*

*Pause.*

**APPKEN**: Before I go.

**BUCKY**: Before you go.

**APPKEN**: Yesterday I'd have said seventy-five percent.

**KAMPER**: I know.

**APPKEN**: I'm not going to say a number.

**KAMPER**: Thank you.

**APPKEN**: I'm going to say: the hole walks. And when it walks into a frozen place, sometimes it goes uphill and still comes home. And the place it starts from might be easier than all the places it could go. And three people on a bench for four can't fix that by sliding.

**BUCKY**: That's not a number.

**APPKEN**: It's a better position.

*Pause.*

**KAMPER**: Well. Shall we go?

**BUCKY**: Yes, let's go.

*They do not move.*

*Long pause.*

*Then KAMPER stands, steps over one armrest, and sits one seat along. BUCKY does the same. The empty seat is now at the other end of the bench.*

**BUCKY**: Progress?

**KAMPER**: A slide.

**BUCKY**: Same three.

**KAMPER**: Same three. Hole's somewhere else.

*APPKEN, still standing with the pages, looks at the empty seat. He does not sit in it. He turns and walks off towards the department, at walking pace.*

*A wind. The red leaf comes off the tree, spins, and lands on the empty seat. It stays there a moment. Then the wind takes it off again, onto the path.*

*KAMPER and BUCKY watch it go. Neither says anything.*

*KAMPER takes out a pencil and the napkin. Beside "or only the ones we make?" she draws, very small, a pentagon with two chords from one corner.*

*BUCKY takes out his phone.*

**BUCKY**: *(to the ASSISTANT)* A pentagon. Four colours. Two chords from one corner. Which colourings of the outside five are proper. Don't run anything yet. Just tell me how many cases there are.

**ASSISTANT**: Before I count: do you mean colourings up to renaming the colours, or all of them? And are the chord ends allowed to match the deleted vertex's eventual colour?

**BUCKY**: *(grinning, to KAMPER)* It asked the right question.

**KAMPER**: They do now.

*The lights fade slowly on the bench: two people, four seats, one empty seat that is not the one that was empty at the start, and three leaves on the tree.*

**END**

### Author's note

This play is written after the manner of Samuel Beckett's *Waiting for Godot*. It borrows the bench, the waiting, the swapping of identical objects, the arrival of a breathless runner, and the messenger who cannot say when the awaited thing will come. In this play the awaited thing is a validation digest.

The two documents in the play are real project texts: the Proof Navigator's revision-57 letter to both teams, and the Night Handoff of 4 October 2026 written at revision 70. Every technical claim the characters read aloud comes from those texts and is presented with the same status the texts give it: hand arguments, single-graph computations, and an open hypothesis. Nothing in the play is a proof, and the play does not say the vacancy hypothesis is true.

These things are invented for the story: Appken finding the handoff on a desk, the three requests in the note, Kamper's kill condition for the remaining tile, and Appken's search kill condition. They are not project results, and nobody has run the search or written the tile. The bench, the leaves, and the pencilled napkin are stagecraft. The real record of what has been checked is in the Proof Navigator.
