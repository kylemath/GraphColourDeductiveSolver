Reducible configurations · link words · the frame class

Diamonds and Holes

A triangulation is a map on the sphere where every face is a triangle. Pick a vertex with five neighbours and delete it: that leaves a hole. Its five neighbours form a ring called its link, and reading their degrees in order gives the hole's link word, for example 55666. The Four Colour Theorem is already proved, with heavy computer help. This project looks for a different route: show that every triangulation in a small, well-behaved family (the frame class) has one hole that Kempe swaps can always fill (Lemma R*, [open]).

This page is about the shape of that family. Two classical reducible configurations, the Birkhoff diamond and RSST 2.122, are cut out of it. What is left still has holes, and the question is which link words those holes can have. The page Cages and Diamonds walks through the diamond's ring colourings one at a time, and The Six-Ring Trap studies one 66666 hole in depth. This page does not repeat them; it maps the territory between.

Story 1

Two shapes a smallest counterexample cannot contain

Suppose some map needed five colours, and take the smallest one. A reducible configuration is a small patch that such a smallest map can never contain: if the patch were there, you could cut it out, colour the smaller map that is left (it is smaller, so it can be coloured), and then always repair the colouring so that the patch fits back in. Sometimes the colours around the cut already fit; otherwise a few Kempe swaps outside the cut make them fit.

Both patches below are made of four vertices: two centres joined by an edge, and two tips, one on each side. In the Birkhoff diamond (1913) all four have degree five, and the patch is surrounded by a ring of six vertices. RSST configuration 2.122 (Robertson, Sanders, Seymour and Thomas, 1997) is the same patch with one centre raised to degree six; its ring has seven vertices.

Both are proved reducible inside this project's Lean library [formal]: each has a compiled certificate covering every colouring of its ring, and a theorem that a triangulation containing the patch is four-colourable whenever all smaller maps are. So the frame class may leave them out.

Precise statement (Lean)
  • An occurrence is the structure Occ T ring int in DiamondPOcc.lean, DiamondMOcc.lean, C2122POcc.lean and C2122MOcc.lean (P and M are the two orientations): injective ring, injective interior, disjoint, the interior degrees ![5,5,5,5] or ![6,5,5,5], and the rotation at every interior vertex given by Nx facts. Ring chords are allowed. The drawings above are rebuilt from exactly those Nx facts ().
  • colorable_of_occ: a triangulation with an occurrence is four-colourable when every spherical map of smaller support is. It rests on the D-reducibility certificates DiamondPCert, DiamondMCert, C2122PCert, C2122MCert.
  • four_color_of_RStarFrame (FrameF3.lean): if every connected triangulation with minimum degree 5, no separating triangle (NoSep), DiamondFree and Conf2122Free has a degree-five vertex with PureClean, then every spherical map is four-colourable. Axioms: propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound.
  • "Free" means no Occ. It does not mean "no induced diamond subgraph"; see the clean-tips caveat in Story 2.
  • Scope: the Lean result is about combinatorial spherical maps (rotation systems). The bridge from planar drawings is a separate, unformalised step.

Story 2

A hole and its link word

Here is one hole (the dashed centre) with its five link vertices. Click a link vertex, or use the buttons, to change its degree. A vertex of degree d has d − 3 neighbours further out, so the outer ring grows as the degrees grow. The link word lists the five degrees in order; degrees of 8 or more are all written 8+, and words that differ only by rotating or reflecting are the same hole type. There are 136 hole types.

Watch for three consecutive link degrees 5 5 5 or 5 6 5. Then the hole and its middle neighbour are the two centres of a diamond (or of 2.122, with the degree-six neighbour as the bigger centre), and the two outer neighbours are its tips. The patch you just learned to cut out is sitting right there.

The clean-tips caveat. A 555 or 565 run always gives the four vertices of a diamond or 2.122 with the right degrees and triangles (an appearance) [formal]. To be an occurrence that the frame class excludes, the two tips must also have no common neighbour other than the two centres (clean tips). A tip-sharing vertex y would make a four-cycle tip–hole–tip–y that cuts the map in two. Ruling that out needs the separating-four-cycle reduction (F2), which is not formalised. So "frame-class holes have no 555 or 565" is a theorem only with clean tips. In the data it never matters: no frame-class graph in any census has an appearance with unclean tips [data].
Precise statement (Lean, FrameScope.lean)
  • Hypotheses: T triangulated, NoSep T, deg h = 5, pentagonal link P : Pent T.graph h with vertices x 0 … x 4. Minimum degree 5 is not needed.
  • tips_not_adj: x j and x (j+2) are not adjacent.
  • appears_diamond_of_run: degrees 5, 5, 5 at x j, x (j+1), x (j+2) give Appears ![5,5,5,5] T ![h, x j, x (j+1), x (j+2)] (centres h and x (j+1)).
  • appears_c2122_of_run: degrees 5, 6, 5 give Appears ![6,5,5,5] T ![x (j+1), x j, h, x (j+2)] (degree-six centre x (j+1)).
  • LinkTipsClean P j: every common neighbour of x j and x (j+2) is h or x (j+1). Under it, occ_diamond_of_run / occ_c2122_of_run give an Occ, so no_555_run (from DiamondFree) and no_565_run (from Conf2122Free) hold.
  • Of the 136 hole types, 19 contain a cyclic 555 or 565, leaving 117 allowed types (recounted by build_data.py).

Story 3

The frame class, and its smallest member

The frame class keeps only triangulations that a smallest counterexample could still be, once the classical reductions are used:

  • no separating triangle (every triangle of edges is a face; otherwise the map splits and each half is coloured separately),
  • minimum degree 5 (a vertex of degree 4 or less is easy to colour last),
  • no Birkhoff diamond and no 2.122, in either mirror image.

Is anything left? Yes. The smallest frame-class triangulation has 22 vertices, and there is exactly one of that size [data, exhaustive]. Lean checks it is in the class [formal], so the class is not empty. It is drawn flat, centred on one hole. Every degree-five vertex is a hole; click one (or a button) to see its link.

Precise statement and checks
  • Lean: FrameWit22Map.lean (generated from TrackC/FrameWit22-faces.json) and FrameWit22.lean prove connected, noSep, min_degree, diamondFree, conf2122Free, frameClass and frameClass_nonempty, with standard axioms only. Freeness is a kernel decide: no edge int 0 ~ int 2 has two distinct common neighbours with the configuration's degrees.
  • This page's builder rebuilt the map from the same face list, checked it is a triangulated sphere (Euler characteristic 2, every link one cycle), and ran a Python port of TrackB's frame.c on it: minimum degree 5, NoSep (triangles = 2n − 4), no Occ of any of the four structures, no appearance at all.
  • It is the same map (up to mirror image) as p22#196, the only order-22 graph in the exhaustive census (plantri -m5 -c4, then frame.c).
  • The witness says nothing about R*. Whether it has a pure-clean hole is a separate certificate.

Story 4

Which holes occur in the frame class

Every triangulation has degree-five vertices: Euler's formula makes the degrees add up to 6n − 12, so on average they are just under six. A natural plan is to find a short list of hole types such that every frame-class triangulation has a hole on the list, and then handle each type by hand. Track B tried this. The census below lists every frame-class triangulation up to 34 vertices [data, exhaustive].

The C60 dual: every hole is 66666

Fullerene duals

Twelve pentagons, nothing else

The football-shaped carbon molecule C60 has 12 pentagons and 20 hexagons. Put a vertex in each face and join neighbouring faces: you get a triangulation with 32 vertices, 12 of degree five and 20 of degree six. In C60 no two pentagons touch (the isolated pentagon rule, IPR), so no two degree-five vertices are adjacent, and every hole is a 66666 hole.

So 66666 must be on any list of hole types, and it is the hardest kind to handle locally (see The Six-Ring Trap). It cannot be excluded by a new configuration either: these maps are genuinely in the class.

How short can the list be?

Upper bound [computer-certified]

A list of 59 types is proved to work

By discharging: give each vertex the charge 6 − degree (the total is always 12), let each degree-five vertex send small amounts to its neighbours of degree 7 or more, and check that a vertex can only keep positive charge if its hole type is on the list. Track B's best rule gives a list of 59 allowed types (78 if the 19 555/565 types are kept, since without F2 they cannot be dropped). No rule in which a hole's gifts depend only on its own word does better.

Lower bound [data]

At least 9 types are needed, probably more

Nine verified frame-class graphs have pairwise disjoint sets of hole types, so any working list has at least 9 entries. On all known graphs the smallest list that catches them grew from 7 to 13 as an adversarial search kept building new graphs that dodge the current list, and it was still growing when the search stopped. The target of about 20 types, each with a local proof, is not in sight.

Precise statements (Track B)
  • Hole type: the cyclic link-degree word of a degree-five vertex, up to rotation and reflection, with every degree ≥ 8 written 8+. The canonical form is the lexicographically least of the 10 rotations and reflections.
  • Discharging: a degree-five vertex v sends σ(p, D, q) to each neighbour u of degree ≥ 7, with D = min(deg u, 8) and p, q the capped degrees beside u in v's word. A degree-7 vertex receives at most 1 (all 47 link sequences enumerated); a vertex of degree ≥ 8 receives at most d/4 ≤ d − 6. So Sσ = {words sending less than 1} is unavoidable in every minimum-degree-5 triangulation. The MILP optimum is |S| = 59 allowed + 19 = 78, rechecked in exact rational arithmetic; the general 5-key rule gives the same optimum. The list is TrackB/out/discharge-frameonly.json.
  • Packing bound 9 and the hitting-set growth 7 → 13 are from TrackB/README.md (325 adversarial rounds, witnesses re-verified with frame.c). The census-only minimum hitting set grows 6 → 9 by order 32 (CoordinatorPlan, 7 Oct).
  • No hole type in the frame class has PureClean proved. Every local result so far (F5, weak F6, pureClean_of_hole4) needs a 555 run, which the frame class does not have (with clean tips). Track B's verdict: amber, leaning kill.
