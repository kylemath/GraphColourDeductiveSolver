Wrapping the sphere around a tetrahedron

Each vertex is a panel of the ball, coloured by its colour and raised by its colour's height (or by its degree). A 4-colouring sends every vertex to one corner of a tetrahedron, so every triangle of the map lands on one tetrahedron face. Slide "wrap" to watch the sphere fold onto the tetrahedron.

The charge map

Each vertex carries its Heawood charge: the sum of +1 (right-handed) and -1 (left-handed) over the triangles around it. Step through the shortest fill of a blocked hole by Kempe swaps and watch where the charge moves. Capped, every degree-5 or degree-7 vertex carries +3 or -3, every degree-6 vertex 0, +6 or -6.

Charge does not stay home

    No simple trapped-charge signature

      Reading the map

      A Kempe chain flips two colours along a whole connected stretch of the map, so the triangles along its boundary change handedness wherever the chain goes, not only beside the hole. The ring table shows, for each distance from the hole, how many vertices changed at this step and the net charge there.

      Legal means no crushed faces

      Neighbours never share a colour, so every triangle uses three different corners and lands on a whole tetrahedron face. An illegal colouring would crush a triangle to an edge or a point.

      The folding degree

      Count the triangles landing on one tetrahedron face: right-handed minus left-handed. Every face gives the same number. On this graph its parity never changes within a Kempe class: the big class is even, the small classes odd.

      The hole

      Delete vertex 15 and the sheet has a five-sided opening. If its rim uses three colours, the rim sits on one tetrahedron face and the hole can be capped. If the rim uses all four, it crosses the tetrahedron and no flat cap fits: a blocked state.
