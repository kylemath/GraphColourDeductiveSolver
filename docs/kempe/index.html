Counting Kempe Chains

Pick two of the four colours and keep only the vertices with those colours: the pieces that fall out are Kempe chains. There are six ways to pick the pair, so every colouring has six pictures. N is the total number of pieces across all six. Swapping the two colours on one piece is a Kempe swap: it always gives another proper colouring.

The map here is a small triangulated sphere with 18 vertices, one of them removed (the degree-five hole, dashed). Its 17 remaining vertices have … four-colourings up to renaming the colours. That is small enough for this page to compute everything live in your browser. checking…

bWhat a swap changes

Swap colours p, q on one chain. The pq-picture keeps exactly the same pieces; only the colours inside one piece trade places. The complementary pair rs is not touched at all. Only the four mixed pictures (pr, ps, qr, qs) can rewire, and the change in N comes entirely from them.

cBuilding the Kempe class

Starting from the current colouring, try every swap, then every swap from each new colouring, and so on (breadth-first search). Every colouring you can reach this way forms its Kempe class. A colouring is filled if the five rim vertices use at most three colours, so the hole can take the fourth. Kempe's hope was that every class contains a filled colouring.

dThe chain-parity law, checked

A colouring with all four colours on the rim reads α, μ, α, A, B around the hole (the repeated colour is α). It is doubly locked when a μ–A chain joins the μ to the A and a μ–B chain joins the μ to the B; then neither of Kempe's two swaps frees a colour. Kempe's exchange π swaps α and A on the chain through the second α. The law says: at a doubly locked colouring, N(πc) − N(c) is odd exactly when πc is doubly locked again. At a doubly locked state N ≥ 8. N = 8 (rigid) means the six pictures have 1, 1, 2, 1, 2, 1 pieces, so a rigid state is never followed by another rigid one.

The mod-4 formula, live for the current colouring

cw counts triangles (not touching the hole) whose edge-colour differences, read in the 2×2 grid of colours, turn clockwise. η depends only on the rim colours. L1, L2 are the locks. One π-step turns cw by 2 (mod 4) and leaves η alone, which is where the law comes from. The formula holds for unfilled colourings; for a filled one the hole could be coloured and the formula takes its closed-sphere form instead.

Status. The chain-count formula, the chain-parity law and rigid isolation are compiled in Lean 4 and audited (they hold on every triangulated sphere, and fail on the torus). Whether every Kempe class at a degree-five hole contains a filled colouring is open. This page shows a single small example and proves nothing new.

Data. The triangulation is graph 7 of plantri -m5 -a 18, with the hole at vertex 4. docs/kempe/build_data.py enumerates its colourings, chains, swaps, classes, locks, π, cw and η. It then asserts agreement with TrackM's tm_lib (state by state), the reviewed TrackJL-review engine rv_eng (zero failures), the formula at every unfilled state and the law at every doubly locked state.
