The Tidy Trap

Delete a degree-five root from a triangulation, four-colour what is left, and then try to free a colour for the root using Kempe swaps. The candidate rule says: always make a score R go down within at most two swaps. The score rewards tidiness: few colours on the root's five neighbours, and little chain mass hanging off them.

At root 4 there is one colouring that is tidier than everything within two swaps of it, yet it is not finished. It is the deepest state at that root, four swaps from any target, and the only way out is to make things messier first. Every cheap score we tried has the same blind spot. Pick a root, click states in the landscape, or play swaps yourself.

What a solver must do

Climb out

The trap can be escaped, but not downhill. The cheapest way out takes three swaps and pushes R up by 20 before it falls. A strict two-swap descent rule can never take that first step.

Option 1 · selection

Stand somewhere else

The trap belongs to a root, not to the map. Eight of the twelve roots have no trap at all. Under the linear-mass variant, only the symmetric pair {4, 6} is left. A structural rule that never selects 4 or 6 avoids the trap here.

Avoidance · restricted input

Never be handed the trap

From every other colouring at root 4, a strictly descending two-swap path reaches a target without ever entering the trap. So if the recursion can promise never to produce that one colouring, descent works here. That promise is the "certificate" the teams are looking for. It still has to be named and proved.

Option 3 · tried, did not work

Reward the mess

The escape begins by joining two boundary vertices with a chain, so we tried crediting such chains: q − k·links for k = 5 to 80. The stuck counts at roots 4, 6, 9 and 14 did not change. Inside the trap's two-swap neighbourhood the trap already has the fewest links, the least mass, and the most locking. A useful non-monotone score needs a real idea, not a reweighting.

A score that always works does exist: "number of two-swap steps to a target". But computing it means already knowing the answer, which is the circular rank the plan rules out.
