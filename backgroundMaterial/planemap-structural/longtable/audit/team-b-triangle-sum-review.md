# Team B: independent review of the triangle-sum m=3 family

5 October 2026. Hand review only; no new graph order generated or coloured.

The Math root's triangle clique-sum construction is sound under its stated seed certificate. Let A and B be properly four-colourable spherical triangulations of minimum degree 5 and m>=3; identify their facial triangles with reversed orientation. A shared vertex has degree d_A+d_B−2>=8, so no degree-5 root lies on the shared triangle F. Every eligible root lies strictly in one summand, with the same complete neighbour link and the same legal fans as there. A hard fan start from that summand extends over the other by matching its three distinct face colours to a full colouring through a palette permutation.

If that global start had mixed distance at most 2, the general M3 theorem would supply at most two pure Kempe moves at its fixed root. In the deletion at that root, the active vertices of F for a selected colour pair form a clique. Every excursion of a global bichromatic path through the other summand can therefore be replaced by a clique edge (or a trivial return). Consequently a global component intersects the root's summand in exactly one whole local component, or not at all. Projecting the two pure moves gives at most two local Kempe moves and a local fill, contradicting the hard local start. Identity projections consume no move. This establishes the preserved lower bound m>=3.

For the upper bound in a chain of the 17:1 seed, the seed pair (root 0, fan 1) remains eligible because both selected gluing faces (1,2,7) and (3,4,11) avoid root 0 and are vertex-disjoint. Its complete 34-start pure-distance certificate has histogram {0:5,1:17,2:10,3:2}. Any global admitted colouring restricts to one of those seed starts. Each pure local component has a unique containing global component whose restriction is exactly the local component, by the same triangle-clique argument; swapping it lifts the local move. The root has only seed neighbours, so the terminal local target is also a global target. Thus m<=3.

A chain of k copies identifies three vertices at each of k−1 joins, giving n=17k−3(k−1)=14k+3. Disjoint left/right faces ensure the next join does not fold the previous shared triangle or the chosen root. Combined with the lower bound, this supplies exact m=3 for every k>=1, subject to the reviewed seed data and explicit simplicial sphere gluing. No counting argument or extrapolation from freshly generated graphs is needed.

Review scope: this is a proof of the claimed construction, not a novelty claim or a classification of all m=3 triangulations. The construction also shows why a seed's bounded pure certificate, rather than a mixed certificate that moves the hole, is the clean upper-bound input.
