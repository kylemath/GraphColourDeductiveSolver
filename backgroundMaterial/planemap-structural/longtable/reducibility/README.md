# Reducibility certificates in Lean: pricing on the Birkhoff diamond [exploratory]

Long Table, 13 Oct 2026. These are single-file Lean checks using only the core library (no Mathlib), run with Lean v4.35.0-rc3 (`~/.elan/toolchains/leanprover--lean4---v4.35.0-rc3/bin/lean FILE.lean`) at `nice -n 10` on the MacBook. No StudioMathLean module was touched.

**Prior art.** The repo already has a kernel-checked D-reducibility proof of the diamond (and of 2.122) that uses only the standard axioms. It is StudioMathLean `DiamondCert.lean` (767 lines, 31 classes, depth 5) and `Conf2122Cert.lean` (3371 lines, 91 classes, depth 6). Both are generated by `StudioMathReview-scripts/gen_cert.py`, which writes a Kempe-chain proof term for each class on top of `RingChains`/`RingJordan`/`RingReduce`. The work here is an independent re-derivation of the finite search, plus a price for the "SAT / decide certificate" route.

## 1. D-reducibility check (`dred.py`)
- **Model.** This is the vertex-colour form of RSST's consistent-matching closure, with colours in Z2×Z2.
  - Each Kempe pairing θ cuts the ring into "transitions".
  - Every non-crossing perfect matching of the transitions gives the outside Kempe regions.
  - A colouring joins level i+1 if, for some θ and for every matching, some set of regions XOR-ed with θ lands in level i.
- **Diamond:** 31 ring classes; 16 extend; levels 16 → 21 → 23 → 27 → 30 → 31. So it is **D-reducible at depth 5**, the same as `DiamondCert.lean`.
- **Sanity checks:**
  - a single vertex of degree 4 is D-reducible;
  - a single vertex of degree 5 is not (Kempe's gap).
- **`rsst_scan.py`** runs against RSST's `unavoidable.conf` (the copy in `studiointel/routeb/rsst/`), covering all 13 configurations with ring ≤ 9 (`rsst_scan_r9.txt`):
  - the extendable-class count equals RSST's header field every time;
  - D-reducible ⇔ RSST contract size 0 every time (2.126 and 122.126 are C-reducible and come out "not D").
  - Python times are 0.0–6.4 s per configuration.
- **RSST ring-size histogram (633 configurations):**

  | ring | 6 | 7 | 8 | 9 | 10 | 11 | 12 | 13 | 14 |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | configurations | 1 | 1 | 5 | 8 | 31 | 81 | 175 | 194 | 137 |

  249 are D-reducible and 384 are C-reducible.

## 2. What compiled in Lean

| file | statement | method | axioms (`#print axioms`) | time (wall, whole file) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `DiamondExt.lean` (gen_lean.py) | `diamond_ext_iff`: for a proper `BitVec 2` ring colouring, ∃ interior extension ↔ `ExtSet` (the 16 classes); `diamond_noext` (UNSAT direction) + 16 witness lemmas | `bv_decide` (bundled CaDiCaL + LRAT) | propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound **plus one `…._native.bv_decide.ax_*` axiom per theorem** | 2.75 s |
| `DiamondKernel.lean` (gen_kernel.py) | 31 lemmas `cls_k`: each canonical class extends / does not extend (`Fin 4`) | `decide +kernel` | propext, Quot.sound | 0.5 s |
| `DiamondKernelFull.lean` | `diamond_ext_iff` over all 4^6 ring × 4^4 interior colourings (`Fin 4`) | `decide +kernel` | propext, Quot.sound | 15.6 s |
| `DiamondClosure.lean` | `closureOK = true`: 31 classes, 16 extend, Kempe closure reaches all 31 (a Lean transcription of dred.py; it gives the same level sizes as `#eval`) | `decide +kernel` | **propext only** | 16.6 s (closure theorem alone 8.8 s) |
| `Conf2122Closure.lean` | the same for 2.122 (ring 7: 91 classes, 39 extend) | `decide +kernel` | propext | **402 s** (126 s user + 229 s sys) |

SHA-256 prefixes of the `.lean` files are in the commit. Logs: `DiamondClosure.out.txt` and `DiamondKernelFull.out.txt`.

**Findings.**
1. **`bv_decide` is not a kernel-only check in this toolchain.** The LRAT certificate is checked by compiled code, and each use adds an auxiliary axiom (`_native.bv_decide.ax_1_N`), which is trust in the compiler of the same kind as `native_decide`/`Lean.ofReduceBool`. `BVDecideConfig` in 4.35 has no kernel-mode option.
2. **SAT does not fit the hard part.** The extendability facts are the SAT-shaped part, and for these configurations they are tiny: plain `decide +kernel` proves them, standard axioms only, in under 16 s even with no symmetry reduction. The hard part, the Kempe closure, has the shape ∃θ ∀matching ∃flip-set. That is not a SAT instance; it is a finite search best certified by a checker.
3. **Fully kernel-checked results so far.** The diamond's finite closure computation is kernel-checked with `[propext]` only. So is 2.122's. But this certifies the search, not its soundness: the meta-theorem "closure reached ⇒ the triangulation is 4-colourable" is not proved here. The repo's `RingChains`/`RingJordan`/`RingReduce` are the natural base for that proof (one soundness theorem for `stepOK`).

## 3. Extrapolation (cost of doing many configurations this way)
**Ring colouring classes.** The count is (3^r + 3(−1)^r)/24 + O(1):

| r | 6 | 7 | 8 | 9 | 10 | 11 | 12 | 13 | 14 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| classes | 31 | 91 | 274 | 820 | ≈2.5k | ≈7.4k | ≈22k | ≈66k | ≈199k |

The number of matchings per class also grows, as Catalan numbers in (#transitions)/2, up to 429 at r = 14, and the flip subsets grow as 2^regions.

- **Naive kernel `decide` (this file's lists-of-Nat encoding):** going from r = 6 to r = 7 multiplied the time by about 46 (8.8 s → 402 s, with heavy memory and sys time). Extrapolated, r = 14 would take more than 10^13 s per configuration. **Dead.**
- **Generated per-class tactic certificates (the existing gen_cert.py route):** about 25–37 Lean lines per class (767/31 and 3371/91), so roughly 10^7 lines for one r = 14 configuration before depth growth. Times 633 configurations, this is infeasible. It is fine for a handful of rings ≤ 8–9.
- **Verified checker + one soundness proof:** this is the only route that scales.
  - Write `stepOK` once.
  - Prove it sound once (Kempe + Jordan; the hard, human part, probably weeks given RingChains exists).
  - Run it on all configurations.
  - Run cost depends on the evaluator:
    - with `native_decide` (axiom `Lean.ofReduceBool`) or a bv_decide-style native check, a compiled checker should handle all 633 in minutes to hours (the Python scan is ≤ 6 s per configuration at r ≤ 9; RSST's C code is fast);
    - with the kernel only, it needs a Gonthier-style re-engineering: bitset colourings packed in GMP-accelerated `Nat` ops, chromogram trees, and no list-of-list allocation. The speed-up needed is about 10^6–10^9 over this file, which is unpriced and the main risk.
- **Track B's 59 hole types:**
  - A hole type is the radius-1 neighbourhood of a degree-5 vertex. Its ring length is Σ d_i − 20: 8 for 55666, 10 for 66666, and unbounded for `8+` entries.
  - What Track B needs at those types is PureClean, not D-reducibility of the neighbourhood. Most such neighbourhoods are not reducible configurations; otherwise RSST would have needed far fewer.
  - If a reducibility-style check is used, the rings are 8–14+. That is RSST scale, so the same verdict applies: a verified checker or nothing.

**Gonthier's Coq/Rocq `fourcolor` (recall, not checked here):**
- It is about 60k lines of ssreflect, with on the order of 2.5k lemmas. It encodes the 633 configurations as small "construction programs".
- Reducibility is proved by reflection: a verified checker (`cfreducible`/`cfcontract`) runs by `vm_compute` inside Coq's trusted kernel evaluator, using Gonthier's own efficient chromogram/"ctree" data structures. The checker's soundness rests on a combinatorial-map and Jordan-curve development.
- The whole check reportedly took days in 2005 and hours or less on modern machines with `vm_compute`/`native_compute`.
- **Porting to Lean 4:** the mathematics (hypermaps, Jordan, Kempe, discharging) ports. The reflection does not port at speed, because the Lean kernel has no `vm_compute` analogue: `decide +kernel` is the interpreter measured above. A faithful port would either use `native_decide` (one extra axiom) or need the kernel-friendly re-engineering above.
- My estimate is several person-months for the ported mathematics, with the 633-configuration run gated on that evaluator choice. This is an opinion.

## 4. Recommendation
- Keep the existing generated Kempe-proof certificates for the few small rings the frame argument actually uses (diamond, 2.122: done, standard axioms).
- Use `decide +kernel` files like these as cheap independent cross-checks for r ≤ 7.
- Do not pursue SAT/`bv_decide` for reducibility: it covers only the easy extendability part, and in 4.35 it is not kernel-only anyway.
- If many configurations or rings ≥ 10 are ever needed, the plan is a single verified closure checker in Lean (soundness from RingChains/RingJordan), run with `native_decide`, recording the `Lean.ofReduceBool` axiom explicitly. Or, if an axiom-free result is mandatory, budget a Gonthier-style kernel-efficient encoding as a separate research project.

## Files
- `dred.py`: closure checker.
- `rsst_scan.py`: RSST cross-check (`python3 rsst_scan.py ../../studiointel/routeb/rsst/unavoidable.conf 9`).

- `gen_kernel.py` → `DiamondKernel.lean`, plus `DiamondKernelFull.lean` (arg `full`).

# Run 2: an executable D-reducibility checker in Lean, run by `native_decide` [exploratory]

Long Table, 13 Oct 2026. The project owner has accepted `native_decide` for large reducibility runs provided the axiom is recorded. All files are in `dcheck/`, a self-contained Lake package: core Lean v4.35.0-rc3 plus `Std`, no Mathlib, `precompileModules := true`. Build it with `cd dcheck && nice -n 10 lake build`. No StudioMathLean module was touched.

## What runs
- **`DCheck.lean`: the checker.** `def dReducible : Conf → Bool` is a Lean port of `dred.py` (the same vertex-colour consistent-matching closure, with level-synchronous levels, so `depth` agrees with dred.py). It has four parts:
  - canonical proper ring colourings as base-4 codes;
  - interior extendability by backtracking;
  - the non-crossing matchings of 2p transitions, tabulated once for p ≤ 7 (`matchTable`);
  - the closure, which keeps a **monotone progress pointer** per (class, θ): the first matching not yet satisfied. Because the good set only grows, a satisfied matching stays satisfied.
- **`gen_data.py` → `DCheckData.lean`:** all 633 RSST configurations from `studiointel/routeb/rsst/unavoidable.conf`. Each is one `def cfg_i` (0-based file order), and `rsst` is the array of all of them. Each entry carries RSST's extendable-class count and contract size for cross-checking.
- **`DCheckThms.lean`: theorems proved by `native_decide`.**
  - `dred_cfg_i : dReducible cfg_i = true` for the 13 D-reducible configurations with ring ≤ 9: 0.7322 (the diamond), 2.122, 3.442802, 0.453962, 3.1306802, 2.7566, 0.79077722, 0.79077962, 122.122, 122.442802, 122.363, 0.260517722 and 7322.122.
  - `not_dred_cfg_3`, `not_dred_cfg_25 : … = false` for 2.126 and 122.126, which are C-reducible in RSST.
  - `diamond_run : run cfg_0 = {classes 31, ext 16, reached 31, depth 5}`.
  - `rsst_r9_agree`: on all 15 ring ≤ 9 configurations, the extendable count equals RSST's, and D-reducible holds iff RSST's contract size is 0.
- **`DCheckThms11.lean`:** `rsst_r10_11_agree`, the same agreement statement for all 112 configurations with ring 10–11.
- **`Main.lean` → `.lake/build/bin/dcheck RMIN RMAX [LIMIT]`:** the same code as a compiled executable, for timing (`run_*.txt`).

**Axioms.** `#print axioms` gives `[propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound, <thm>._native.native_decide.ax_1_1]`. In this toolchain `native_decide` does **not** show `Lean.ofReduceBool` directly. Each use adds its own auxiliary axiom `…_native.native_decide.ax_1_1`, in the same style as `bv_decide` in Run 1. The trust is the same: the compiler, the `Std.HashMap` runtime and the C/LLVM toolchain. Record it as "native_decide auxiliary axiom per theorem".

**Agreement with RSST.**
- 135 of 135 configurations checked so far agree: all 15 at ring ≤ 9, all 112 at ring 10–11, and 8 sampled at ring 12. The extendable-class count matches RSST's header every time, and D/not-D matches contract = 0 every time.
- Ring ≤ 9 depths are identical to `rsst_scan_r9.txt` (dred.py).

## Soundness: the exact statement and the gap (`DCheckSound.lean`)
**Declarative closure.** `inductive Good cf : Nat → Prop` is the least set that contains the extendable codes and is closed under this rule: for some θ with transitions, every matching in `matchTable` has a non-empty flip of regions (a choice function `w`) landing in `Good`.

**Geometric hypothesis, abstracted.** `KempeMatching cf P`: for every realised code `k` and every θ with transitions, SOME tabulated matching satisfies "flipping ANY set of its regions stays realised".

The pieces:
- **(S2) PROVED: `good_sound`** (axioms: propext, Quot.sound). Suppose `P` is closed under `canon` and satisfies `KempeMatching cf P`. Then `Good cf k ∧ P k → ∃ k', P k' ∧ extendsQ cf k' = true`. This is the combinatorial core: induction on `Good`.
- **(S1) `sorry`: `run_sound`** says `dReducible cf = true → ∀ k ∈ ringColourings cf.r, Good cf k`. With `canon_mem` (`sorry`: every proper code canonicalises into `ringColourings`), this gives `dReducible_sound`, proved from these two. (S1) is a loop-invariant proof about the imperative `run`:
  - good-bit ⇒ `Good`;
  - pointer p ⇒ matchings < p are satisfied;
  - the `HashMap` index is a bijection;
  - `flipMask` agrees with the in-loop mask.

  It is tedious but routine. The cheaper option is to rewrite `run` in a fuel-recursive functional style that mirrors `Good`, at some speed cost.
- **(S3) `sorry`, at project level (stated in the module doc; not compiled here because the Mathlib/StudioMath build is not on this machine).** Let `P := {ring code of c | ProperOff G.graph O.hole c}` for a `RingReduce.ConfigOcc`. Then `P` satisfies `KempeMatching`. What exists and what is missing:
  - Non-crossing of chains is in the repo: `RingChains.chains_noncrossing` and `chains_noncrossing_same`, via `RingJordan.face_alternating_walks_meet`.
  - So are the per-vertex swap facts: `RingReduce.flip_in`, `flip_out` and `flip_other`.
  - **Missing (i):** building the matching from the chain partition. Pair each transition with the next transition along its chain's boundary; non-crossing plus same-class-per-face then gives a non-crossing perfect matching whose faces are the chain traces. This is a pure non-crossing-partition lemma on a cycle, with no geometry.
  - **Missing (ii):** swapping a *set* of components at once. The generated certificates only use single swaps; this is an iteration of single swaps on distinct components.

  RingJordan already supplies the planar input. The gap is combinatorics plus bookkeeping.
- **(S4) exists:** `ConfigOcc.colorable_of_ext`.

The target theorem (in the `DCheckSound.lean` doc) is `ConfigOcc.colorable_of_dReducible (cf) (hD : dReducible cf = true) (O : ConfigOcc T G cf.r cf.m ρ ι (intAdjOf cf) (ringAdjOf cf)) (hc : ProperOff G.graph O.hole c) : T.graph.Colorable 4`. "No minimal counterexample contains cf" then follows exactly as for the diamond.

## Estimates (opinion)
- **(a) All 633 configurations with `native_decide`, D-reducibility only.**
  - The extrapolated single-core cost is about 25 min at ring 12, about 2–3 h at ring 13 and about 8–12 h at ring 14, so **≈ 12–15 CPU-hours** in total. Split into ~16 theorem files and built in parallel, that is a few wall-clock hours on 14 cores. Mind battery and heat: run on AC.
  - Easy speed-ups not yet done would likely give 3–10×:
    - a Gray-code subset loop;
    - a packed canon table;
    - not rescanning stuck matchings every level, by re-testing only when a class they can reach became good;
    - ordering interior vertices for backtracking.
  - **Caveat:** only the 249 D-reducible configurations close this way. The other **384 are C-reducible** and need the contraction step:
    - replace the configuration by a contracted one;
    - the closure starts from colourings that extend *after* contraction;
    - prove the contraction keeps minimal-counterexample hypotheses (no new short cycles, which is RSST's "contract" condition).

    That is an extra checker feature, about a day, plus extra soundness work, likely the larger part.
- **(b) Soundness proof.**
  - (S1) loop correctness: 1–2 weeks. Or under a week if `run` is first refactored to a functional fold mirroring `Good`, keeping the fast version only if a refinement proof is cheap.
  - (S3)(i) and (ii): 1–3 weeks on top of RingChains/RingJordan.
  - C-reducibility soundness: contraction, plus the minimal-counterexample conditions on the contracted map. Several more weeks.
  - The unavoidability half of 4CT (discharging) is separate and not priced here.
