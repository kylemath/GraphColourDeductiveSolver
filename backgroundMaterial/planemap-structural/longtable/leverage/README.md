# Distant leverage: how swap power depends on the distance from the hole (exploratory)

Long Table, 2026-10-13. Status: **exploratory measurement**. Labels: [data] = computed here, [hand] = short argument.
Nothing here is a theorem or a census claim. Re-check before citing.

## Question

The project owner's game intuition: when stuck at a locked hole, a few far-away swaps seem to open new paths near
the hole ("distant leverage"). We measure, for every Kempe swap available at every unfilled state:

- d(K) = graph distance in T from the hole v to the nearest vertex of the chain K (rim = 1), plus max distance and |K|;
- the effect: lock bits / DL status before and after, the exact change of dF (swaps to fill), and, for DL → DL, whether
  the swap stays on the same π-orbit or jumps to another;
- the "sideways" effect: for a swap that keeps dF, the change in the number of dF-reducing rim swaps (paths near the hole).

## Two facts that fix the denominators [hand]

1. A swap with d(K) ≥ 2 does not touch the rim, so it never changes the rim colours and never fills. From a dF = 1
   state it cannot reduce dF. The meaningful "far leverage" source is therefore `D2` (dF = 2) or `DL`.
2. If an unfilled state is not DL, Kempe's step fills it in one swap (a pure graph argument, any surface). So
   non-DL ⇒ dF = 1, hence D2 ⊆ DL on every surface, and from a D2 state "reduces dF" ⇔ "unlocks". [data] The two
   columns agree exactly in every D2 table.
3. A Kempe swap is an involution on states (same chain, same pair, web status kept). So |ΔdF| ≤ 1, and over a full
   class every sideways swap that adds a near path is matched by its reverse that removes one (open = close in the
   tables is a tautology; the informative number is the rate of change).

## 2. Answers

**Does unlocking power decay with distance on the sphere?** Yes, smoothly and slowly [data]. Per swap, roughly a
factor 0.67–0.72 per step in the frame census (0.71 → 0.49 → 0.34 → 0.24); faster near the antipode in random
order-24 graphs (0.74 → 0.56 → 0.32 → 0.15). It does not drop to zero: a 2-vertex chain three steps away from the
hole unlocks a DL state about one time in three.

**Is there a characteristic radius?** Not separable from finite size here. Holes have eccentricity 3–5 at these
orders; the e-stratified table (TABLES.md, last section) gives rate(d)/rate(1) on the frame census
0.67–0.72 (d=2), 0.48 (d=3), 0.32–0.35 (d=4) for both e = 4 and e = 5, i.e. exponential decay with length
ξ ≈ 1/ln(1/0.7) ≈ 2.5–3 edges. In random order-24 sphere graphs the decay steepens at the last shell (d = e). So
the honest statement is: "decay length about 2.5–3 hops, but the maps are only 4–5 hops deep". [data]

**Far many-weak or near few-strong?** Near dominates in total and is never missing [data]. On the sphere 72–76% of
all dF-reducing swaps touch the rim (d = 1), 17–19% at d = 2, 5–9% at d ≥ 3. Far swaps are fewer **and** weaker:
swaps available per state fall with d (5.9, 2.1, 1.4, 0.5), even per shell vertex (0.22, 0.14, 0.08 at d = 2, 3, 4),
and chains shrink (|K| 8.0 → 1.9). The owner's "outer shells have more chain ends" is **not** borne out at these
orders: the outer shells have more vertices but far fewer non-web chains. Far leverage is real and common (most DL
states have one), but redundant: a near unlock is available at 99.8% of D2 states and at 100% of deep states.

**Sideways swaps (keep dF) and "opening paths near the hole".** On the sphere a sideways far swap changes the number of
near (rim) unlock moves about half the time (D2, frame 30–33: open-rate 0.145/0.510 at d = 2, 0.179/0.662 at d = 3),
more often than a sideways rim swap (0.093/0.286). So far swaps do reshape the near options, which matches the game
feeling. But they almost never create a near exit where none existed (rate ≤ 0.002), because on the sphere one
nearly always exists already. [data]

**66666 holes vs holes with a degree-5 neighbour** (frame 22–33; 48 vs 2942 holes) [data]: the flat 66666 holes have
*more* distant leverage: D2 rates 0.714 / 0.571 / 0.487 / 0.313 against 0.723 / 0.490 / 0.344 / 0.244. Same near
power, about 1.2–1.4× the far power. Small 66666 sample; indicative only.

**Shallow vs deep starts**: see the deep paragraph above. dF ≥ 3 is rare on the sphere (0.1% of unfilled states).

- Off the sphere every distance is weaker in absolute terms: near (d = 1) 0.46–0.57 against 0.72–0.74; at d = 3
  about half the sphere rate (0.14–0.18 against 0.31–0.35).
- The *shape* (decay relative to d = 1) is similar for random sphere graphs and the torus; it is flatter for the
  sphere frame census and steeper on non-orientable and higher-genus surfaces at d ≥ 3. Larger surface maps (e = 5–6)
  fall to 0.01–0.06 of the near rate by d = 5: a short decay length (≈ 1–1.5 hops beyond d = 2).
- Off the sphere ΔdF = +1 swaps are common from D2 (torus 9%, RP² 3.5% at d = 1), DL → DL swaps occasionally stay on
  the same π-orbit at d ≥ 2 (1–6%), and π-cycles and targetless classes appear.
- **Necessity flips**: states whose *only* dF-reducing swaps are far (d ≥ 2): sphere 0.1–0.4% of D2 states (frame,
  random, game maps); torus 5.7%, Klein 4.9%, genus 2 6.2%, RP²#3 5.6%, RP² 2.3%. Deep states: sphere 0%, other
  surfaces 2–5%.

So on the sphere distant leverage is stronger but never needed; off the sphere it is weaker but sometimes the only way.

## 4. What this suggests about local vs global input [hand, interpretation]

- The topological input on the sphere does not show up as long-range power. It shows up as **local sufficiency**:
  every dF = 2 state (and every deep state) has an unlocking or descending swap whose chain touches the rim, and far
  swaps never move you along your own π-run. Off the sphere this local completeness fails in a few percent of states,
  exactly where the far swaps become necessary. This fits the Track F reading that the planarity input is a
  lock-parity statement (a rim-local fact), not a statement about distant regions.
- A proof route may therefore treat far chains as "orbit transfer" moves (they change which π-orbit you are on) and
  rely on near moves for descent. The data give no reason to look for a long-range potential.
- Relation to `GraphColourRLArena/docs/INTERPRETABILITY-1.md`: there "interior-only exits" (every optimal swap avoids
  the rim) are 60 of 11,466 κ = 2 states (0.5%) and carry a 30% model error rate. Here the same notion on the sphere is
  0.1–0.4% of dF = 2 states and 0 of 4,412 deep states (game maps: 22 of 5,540 D2 states, 0.4%), so the two
  measurements agree. The statement "the helpful chain often does not touch the rim" is true in the weaker sense that a
  far optimal swap is **available** at most DL states (76–82%), and 25–28% of all optimal swaps are far. The game
  feeling of "distant leverage" is real, but on the sphere it is an alternative, not a necessity.

## Caveats

- Census frame graphs are a special class (hard holes); the random plantri24 sample is the fair sphere baseline at
  order 24. Sphere orders 30–33 and RP² are partial samples (time caps).
- d is bounded by the hole eccentricity (3–5 on the sphere); no claim about large radii.
- π-cycle winding (picyc) was not measured. Sphere sets contain no π-cycles, so "jump to another π-cycle" is in effect
  "jump to another π-path".
- d(K) uses distance in T (through the hole), with rim = 1.
