## 1. Definitions

Graph PD notation, from `KempePaper/arXiv-2412.18558v1/anc/ProofCode1-6CapGenerator.nb` (section “Graph PD Notation and Caps”): blow each vertex of a plane trivalent graph up to a 3-cycle and number that cycle’s edges counterclockwise. Each original edge is a factor \(M[a,b,c,d]\). The endpoint pairs are \(\{a,b\}\) and \(\{c,d\}\). This reading recovers \(K_4\) as four degree-3 vertices on \(\{1,2,3\}\), \(\{4,5,6\}\), \(\{7,8,9\}\), and \(\{10,11,12\}\), from the notebook’s product

\[
K_4 = M[7,9,2,1]\,M[9,8,4,6]\,M[6,5,3,2]\,M[12,11,5,4]\,M[11,10,1,3]\,M[8,7,10,12].
\]

**Birkhoff diamond.** Same notebook, the product of 21 matchings:

\begin{align*}
&\mathit{BirkhoffDiamond} = M[34,36,13,15]\,M[28,30,16,18]\,M[32,31,23,22]\,M[46,48,26,25]\\
&\quad M[30,29,33,32]\,M[39,38,29,28]\,M[43,45,31,33]\,M[12,11,35,34]\,M[36,35,37,39]\\
&\quad M[40,42,38,37]\,M[42,41,44,43]\,M[45,44,47,46]\,M[48,47,8,7]\,M[10,9,41,40]\\
&\quad M[15,14,2,1]\,M[14,13,18,17]\,M[17,16,19,21]\,M[21,20,4,3]\\
&\quad M[20,19,22,24]\,M[24,23,25,27]\,M[27,26,6,5].
\end{align*}

Labels that meet in an endpoint pair form 12 triangles, \(\{13,14,15\},\{16,17,18\},\ldots,\{46,47,48\}\). Each of those 12 vertices has degree 3. The six pairs \(\{1,2\},\ldots,\{11,12\}\) appear once each and are the boundary spokes. Handshake: \(12\cdot 3 = 2\cdot 15 + 6\).

**Type C reducer.** In `A-new-way-to-prove-configuration-reducibility-using-gauge-theory.tex`, a reducer is Type \(C\) when it is trivalent and planar, and Type \(D\) when it is a single vertex (the paragraph defining Kempe reducibility; figure `BirkhoffTypeCTypeD.pdf`). The text says the Type \(C\) configuration in that figure is the reducer used in the paper. Theorem Main draws it as `BirkhoffReducer.pdf`. The notebook encodes that reducer as

\[
\mathit{Reducer} = M[2,1,12,11]\,M[4,3,14,13]\,M[6,5,13,15]\,M[8,7,16,18]\,M[10,9,18,17]\,M[15,14,17,16].
\]

Two degree-3 vertices, on \(\{13,14,15\}\) and \(\{16,17,18\}\), joined by \(M[15,14,17,16]\). Four edges run from a boundary pair to one of those vertices. \(M[2,1,12,11]\) runs between boundary pairs \(\{1,2\}\) and \(\{11,12\}\).

**Coloured cap.** \(arc[c,A[a,b]]\) is an arc with colour \(c\in\{1,2,3,4\}\) (red, blue, green, yellow) and \(V[a,b]\) is a diamond interaction. The notebook rules `checkColor` and `checkCapMatch` are in `ProofCode3-ColoringTable.nb`.

## 2. Statement

**Blocked census.** For every planar cap \(P\in\mathcal{P}_6\) and every relative state \(R_\alpha\) of this reducer, every proper 3-face colouring of \(P\sharp R_\alpha\) extends to a 4-face colouring of \(P\sharp BD_\beta\) for some relative state \(BD_\beta\) of the diamond, agreeing on \(P\) except that at most two non-interacting arcs may take colour 4. This is the paper’s Proposition on 3-face to 4-face extension (the computer half of Theorem Main). Quantifiers: all 34,179 claimed planar caps, all \(2^6\) reducer states, and a witness among \(2^{21}\) diamond states for each of the 7,210 claimed coloured caps.

**Checked range.** The four rows printed in the tex appendix table, \((1,1)\), \((1,2)\), \((47,2)\), and \((47,3)\). The primary row is \((47,3)\), also stored as a saved output cell in `ProofCode3-ColoringTable.nb` and, for the cap alone, in `ProofCode2-ColoredCapsOnReducer.nb`.

## 5. Kill criterion

The proposition is killed by one coloured cap, among those that 3-colour some reducer state, for which no diamond state has a 4-face colouring of the allowed kind. That search was not run. The criterion was not met. `BadColoring` is the paper’s negative control, obtained by painting a valid yellow circle red.

## 6. Not proved

State-reducibility of the Birkhoff diamond. The four rows are witnesses for four caps, and this check does not rebuild the reducer state that produced them. The lists 34,179, 3,744, and 7,210 are the paper’s counts. They were not recomputed.

## 7. Feasibility
