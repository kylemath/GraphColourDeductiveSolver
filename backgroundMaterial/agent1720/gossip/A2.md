A $d$-degenerate graph on $n\ge d$ vertices satisfies $P(G,k)\ge k(k-1)\cdots(k-d+1)(k-d)^{n-d}$ for integers $k\ge d$, so $P(G,k)>0$ for integers $k\ge d+1$. File: `groups/A2_report.md`
Every outerplanar graph is $2$-degenerate, and every maximal outerplanar graph is a $2$-tree with $P(G,k)=k(k-1)(k-2)^{n-2}$, so $P(G,4)>0$. File: `groups/A2_report.md`
Series-parallel graphs, as subgraphs of two-terminal series-parallel networks, are $2$-degenerate, so $P(G,4)>0$. File: `groups/A2_report.md`
Duffin, J. Math. Anal. Appl. 10 (1965) 303–318, equates that construction with having no $K_4$ minor; the degeneracy proof does not use the minor form. File: `groups/A2_report.md`
