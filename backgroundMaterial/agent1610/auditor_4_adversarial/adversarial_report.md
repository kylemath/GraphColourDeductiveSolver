## 2. The Claim Under Attack

**MTL Lemma (as stated):** For every planar triangulation $G$, every vertex $v$ with $\deg(v) \leq 5$ and $c(v) = 5$, and every proper 4-colouring $c^*$ of $G - v$ reachable by Kempe swaps from the initial 5-colouring restricted to $G - v$: $|\{c^*(w) : w \in N(v)\}| \leq 3$.

**The failure mode:** A proper 4-colouring $c^*$ of $G - v$ where all 4 colours from $\{1,2,3,4\}$ appear on $v$'s neighbours — leaving no free colour for $v$.

## 3. Key Theoretical Reduction

Before running any code, I proved a simplifying equivalence:

**Claim:** The MTL Lemma is equivalent to: *for every planar triangulation $G$ and every vertex $v$ with $\deg(v) \leq 5$, no proper 4-colouring of $G - v$ assigns all 4 colours to $N(v)$.*

**Proof:** ($\Rightarrow$) If MTL holds, then in particular it holds for the trivial starting colouring where only $v$ has colour 5, so the restriction to $G - v$ is already a 4-colouring — reachable by 0 Kempe swaps.

($\Leftarrow$) If no 4-colouring of $G - v$ puts all 4 colours on $N(v)$, then certainly no Kempe-reachable one does.

The key insight: given ANY proper 4-colouring $c^*$ of $G - v$, we can define $c = c^* \cup \{v \mapsto 5\}$. This is a valid 5-colouring of $G$ (since $v$'s neighbours all have colours $\neq 5$). The restriction $c|_{G-v} = c^*$ is trivially reachable by 0 Kempe swaps. So the Kempe reachability condition imposes **no additional constraint**.

This means: **Kempe BFS is unnecessary.** We just need to find ONE proper 4-colouring of $G - v$ with all 4 colours on $N(v)$.

## 6. Degree-4 Analysis (the "Easy" Attack)

The degree-4 case was identified as the most attackable, and this is confirmed:

**At degree 4:** $v$ has 4 neighbours forming a 4-cycle in the link. In $G - v$, if this 4-cycle admits a proper 4-colouring with all 4 distinct colours, MTL is violated. This happens whenever the 4-cycle is $K_4$-minus-one-edge (has exactly one diagonal), because:

1. The three vertices sharing the diagonal form a triangle → need 3 distinct colours
2. The fourth vertex (not on the diagonal) can take the remaining 4th colour
3. The non-adjacent pair (no edge between them) avoids the only constraint that could block this

**When does degree-4 HOLD?** Only when a "blocker" vertex exists — a vertex $w \in V(G-v) \setminus N(v)$ that is adjacent to ALL of $N(v)$. This forces $w$ to need a 5th colour, making all-4-on-$N(v)$ impossible with 4 colours. The octahedron is the unique example at $n = 6$: vertex 3 is antipodal to vertex 0 and adjacent to all of $N(0)$.

**At $n = 5$:** $G - v$ has only 4 vertices, ALL of which are neighbours of $v$. There are no "other" vertices to serve as blockers. So the failure is automatic whenever $G - v$ is not $K_4$ (which would require 4 colours just for $N(v)$, but 4 vertices with 4 colours all distinct is always fine on a non-complete graph).

## 7. Degree-5 Analysis

**At degree 5:** $v$ has 5 neighbours forming a 5-cycle in the link. We need all 4 colours on 5 vertices (one colour repeats, distribution 2-1-1-1). The repeated pair must be non-adjacent in $G - v$.

The 5-cycle link always has 2 chords (from the triangulation of the interior). With a fan triangulation from one vertex $n_1$ (chords $n_1$-$n_3$ and $n_1$-$n_4$):

- $n_1$ is adjacent to all other 4 cycle vertices → needs a unique colour
- Remaining 4 vertices use 3 colours from $\{1,2,3,4\} \setminus \{c(n_1)\}$
- Non-adjacent, non-chord pairs can share a colour
- Result: all 4 colours present

This fails only when ALL non-adjacent pairs in the link are chords — impossible for a 5-cycle (which has 5 non-adjacent pairs but only 2 chords).

## 8. Structural Analysis: WHY MTL Fails

The MTL Lemma fails because of a fundamental structural mismatch:

1. **The claim requires:** no proper 4-colouring of $G - v$ puts all 4 colours on $N(v)$
2. **The reality:** in most triangulations, $G - v$ has enough "flexibility" near $N(v)$ to accommodate all 4 colours
3. **The only defence** is a "blocker" vertex adjacent to all of $N(v)$, which occurs only in very special graphs (octahedron at $n = 6$, and similar structures)
4. **For $n \geq 7$:** no triangulation has a degree-$\leq 5$ vertex with a blocker, so MTL fails universally

The blocker-based analysis was verified computationally: the structural invariant holds perfectly — counterexamples occur if and only if no blocker exists.

## 9. Why Wasn't This Caught Earlier?

The MTL Lemma might have seemed plausible because:

1. **Kempe swap language obscured the reduction.** The claim's mention of "reachable by Kempe swaps" suggests a non-trivial reachability constraint, but the trivial starting colouring (where only $v$ has colour 5) makes every 4-colouring of $G-v$ reachable from it by 0 swaps.

2. **The octahedron is misleading.** The octahedron (the most symmetric $n = 6$ triangulation) *does* satisfy MTL, which might have suggested the property is general. But the octahedron's special structure (every vertex has an antipodal blocker) is the exception, not the rule.

3. **Small-case bias.** At $n = 4$ (the only $K_4$), there are no degree-4 or degree-5 vertices (all have degree 3), so MTL is vacuously true. This gives a misleading "base case."

## 11. Impact on the Proof Strategy

The MTL Lemma was intended to guarantee that after removing a low-degree vertex $v$ and 4-colouring $G - v$ via Kempe swaps, a free colour exists for $v$. Since this guarantee fails:

- **Any proof strategy relying on MTL must be revised.**
- The free-colour guarantee requires additional structural constraints (e.g., restricting which 4-colourings of $G - v$ are considered, or adding constraints on the Kempe swap sequence).
- A possible rescue: restrict to 4-colourings reachable from a *specific* starting 5-colouring that does NOT have only $v$ using colour 5. But even this seems unlikely to help, as the Kempe BFS from ALL starting 5-colourings confirms the violations.
- Another possible rescue: change the lemma to claim $|\{c^*(w)\}| \leq 3$ only for *some* reachable 4-colouring (existential rather than universal). This weaker statement might be true and sufficient for the overall proof.
