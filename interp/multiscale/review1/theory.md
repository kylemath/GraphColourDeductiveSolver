# Desk check of the outside review (claims A–G)

22 Oct 2026. Pen-and-paper plus literature search. Ratings: **correct** / **correct with caveat** / **wrong**. Notation as in `../levelling/RESULTS.md`: Λ the triangular lattice, colours = Λ/2Λ, Γ = 2Λ ⋊ {±1}.

**A. Orbifold dictionary — correct with caveat.** A proper 4-colouring of a triangulation is exactly a non-degenerate simplicial map G → ∂Δ³. Give ∂Δ³ the flat metric with cone angle π at each vertex: that is the pillowcase S²(2,2,2,2) = (R²/2Λ)/(±1), whose cone points are the 4 points of Λ/2Λ, so Λ/2Λ ≅ Z2×Z2 = the colours, and each unit triangle sees all three nonzero cosets (its edge vectors e_g, e_h, −e_g−e_h). Colour-preserving orientation-preserving isometries of this coloured lattice are translations by 2Λ and point reflections about points of Λ (x ↦ 2p − x preserves cosets iff p ∈ Λ): that is Γ = π₁^orb, and pulling back the flat structure gives the Kondev–Henley height as the developing map on the universal cover of G minus the odd vertices. Around a vertex of degree d the link winds w times around a 3-cycle, holonomy (−1)^w, and w ≡ d (mod 2), so an odd vertex is a point reflection, centred at its own lift (levelling §1.1). Full isometry group Λ ⋊ D6 acts on Λ/2Λ as AGL(2,2) ≅ S4 with kernel Γ, giving 1 → Γ → Λ⋊D6 → S4 → 1; the orientation-preserving part Λ ⋊ C6 has index 2 and so maps onto A4, odd permutations are reflections (e.g. the transposition swapping e_g, e_h is the mirror through e_g+e_h). Caveats: it is a semidirect product Λ ⋊ C6, not Λ × C6; and the developing map lives on the punctured surface, not on G itself. This dictionary is essentially Izmestiev's "platonic monodromy" (rolling the tetrahedron) and Fisk's map-degree viewpoint; it is not new, but it is right.

**B. The "quotient cylinder" gap — wrong (but a smaller real issue exists).** The even-case definition in levelling §1.2 is not taken in R²/⟨t⟩: τ is the one-lap circumradius of the *lifted* walk in R², "min over half-lattice p of max_i |H_i − p|", and the text states τ ≥ |t|/2. So τ ≤ 1 forces |t| ≤ 2 and the translation is not hidden; closing Lemma 1 uses exactly this (t ∈ {0, ±2e}). The sentence "∂C is a closed curve in R²/⟨ρ(∂C)⟩" in §0 and §1.2 is a loose gloss and should be dropped or qualified, because in the cylinder a curve can hug the axis while winding once, which is precisely carrying t. The genuine small issue is the base point: §1.2 admits that τ depends on b_0 by up to |t|. The mismatch bound (i) assumes the seam arc lies in one lap; if the base point sits inside the seam arc the bound degrades to 4K + 2|t| ≤ 4K + 4, and closing Lemma 2 likewise needs both sides lapped from a common base point (cost ≤ |t_B| ≤ 2). Fix: define τ as max over base points, or state (i) and Lemma 2 with the extra additive 2. Constant-factor, not a gap.

**C. Citations — correct with caveats.** (1) Fisk, "Combinatorial structure on triangulations. I. The structure of four colorings", Adv. Math. 11 (1973) 326–338 is real (part II is 339–350, same volume). It treats 4-colourings as maps to ∂Δ³ with a degree, proves Kempe-equivalence of all 4-colourings of a 3-colourable sphere triangulation, and contains the "two adjacent odd vertices, all others even ⇒ not 4-colourable" theorem (the standard reference for it). I found no quaternion/binary-tetrahedral double cover or "w mod 4" in Fisk; the mathematics is sound (Γ has a central Z2 extension Z² ⋊ Z4 in which a point reflection lifts to order 4, so loops see w mod 4), but attribute it to the reviewer, not Fisk. The nearest published refinement is Mohar–Salas 2009 (J. Phys. A 42, arXiv 0901.1010): deg(f) mod 12 is a Kempe invariant on 3-colourable orientable triangulations. (2) Fisk, "The non-existence of colorings", JCTB 24 (1978) 247–248 is real: a torus triangulation with exactly two (adjacent) odd vertices, hence not 4-colourable (cited by Mohar–Seymour 2002 as such). The "degrees 5 and 7" detail is **unverified**, and if all other degrees were 6 it would contradict Jendrol'–Jucovič (1972): the sequence (5,6,…,6,7) is not realisable on the torus. Fisk's example must have other non-6 even degrees. (3) Moore–Newman, J. Stat. Phys. 99 (2000) 629–660: height representation (heights in a triangular lattice, four colours = Λ/2Λ, defects as six classes of dislocations with Burgers vectors), stiffness from Kondev–Henley; they prove WSK (Kempe) *is* ergodic on the sphere and projective plane and only conjecture non-ergodicity on the torus (Salas–Sokal 6×6 example), later proved by Mohar–Salas. Single-spin-flip non-ergodicity is stated. So "Kempe non-ergodicity" must be credited carefully. (4) Thurston, "Conway's tiling groups", Amer. Math. Monthly 97(8) (1990) 757–773: height functions for lozenge and domino tilings, the tiling group, linear-time tileability of simply connected regions. (5) Sheffield, Trans. AMS 354 (2002) 4789–4813: Z^n-valued heights for ribbon tilings of simply connected regions, local-move connectivity (Pak's conjecture), tile-count invariants. Neither (4) nor (5) covers annuli, so neither supports an "annulus interpolation" step.

**D. Theorems 1–2 and the fix — Theorem 1 correct (trivial), Theorem 2 correct (degenerate), fix wrong as a cure.** Theorem 1: an interior vertex of degree ≥ 6 needs 7 vertices, so a disc on 4–6 vertices has none, and a triangulated m-gon has exactly m − 3 diagonals, all internal chords; equality, not just ≥. In min-degree-5 maps the wheel W5 is a chordless 6-vertex disc, so the hypothesis is essential. Theorem 2: if no boundary vertex of A becomes interior, A's arc P of ∂U carries all of ∂A, so ∂A = P plus the edge joining P's endpoints, which is a chord of U. Correct, but the hypothesis says the seam has no interior vertices, which no real merge satisfies; our Lemma 1 (hierarchy/RESULTS.md) handles the general seam and is stronger (reflex-degree conditions at both transitions, Gauss–Bonnet budget). The proposed fix, face-disjoint A, B sharing an induced path S, avoids Theorem 2 but not the convexity obstruction: an endpoint x of S has d_U(x) = d_A(x) + d_B(x) − 1 ≥ 5, so both ends of S must be reflex, and flat hexagonal patches (no reflex vertex) remain dead ends. It is not equivalent to W1: W1 keeps vertex-disjoint merges and weakens levelness to pockets (pockets are the special case |S| = 1 edge); the reviewer's version keeps chordlessness but forces c_A = c_B on S, turning the merge into a precolouring-extension problem, which changes conjecture (a) rather than (c).

**E. Torus algebra — correct with caveat.** [t, r] = 2t for t ∈ 2Λ, so [Γ,Γ] = 4Λ, and Γ/4Λ ≅ (2Λ/4Λ) × Z2 is abelian. The boundary of the fundamental polygon is [a,b] in π₁(T ∖ {u,v}), so its holonomy lies in 4Λ; it also equals the product of the two (transported) point reflections, a translation by 2(p_u − p_v) with p_u ≡ h(u) mod 2Λ. Hence 2(p_u − p_v) ∈ 4Λ ⟺ c(u) = c(v), contradicting adjacency. Caveats: the loop must avoid the odd vertices and the transport uses that Γ preserves cosets of 2Λ (which it does); adjacency is used only at the last step; and this is a re-proof of Fisk's 1973 theorem for orientable surfaces (any product of commutators lands in 4Λ), not a new result.

**F. "Renaming aligns holonomies, reducing (b) to a flat annulus interpolation" — wrong in general, correct only as our Lemmas 1–2.** On S² the total odd count is even, so t_A and t_B are both translations or both reflections, but nothing identifies them: t_A is the alternating sum of A's vortex heights, t_B of B's. Alignment by renaming is proved (closing Lemma 1) only when the width-1 band around the seam is Eulerian, via uniqueness of 3-colourings; the data show mixed classes (0 against |t| = 2) whenever ≥ 3 odd vertices touch the seam. The refined w mod 4 invariant does not change this. "Within ≤ 4" is Lemma 2 (with the base-point caveat of B). The "flat annulus interpolation" is our band-filling form of (b), and round2 §3 shows it fails as stated: matched monodromy plus offset ≤ 4 does not bound the filling (circumference-4 tube, R ≥ K/2); internal 6-connectivity is needed, and bands with vortices are not flat.

**G. Hub arithmetic — correct.** Around a hub of degree d the link is a closed walk on K3 with f + b = d, f − b = 3w. Even d, rim b c b c …: f = b, w = 0; in the boundary gauge the signs alternate at every rim vertex, the walk oscillates H_0, H_0 + e, so τ = 1/2. Odd d, rim (bc)^{(d−3)/2} bce: f − b = ±3, |w| = 1; the walk visits 0, e1, e1 − e2, −2e2 with centre q = −e2 and every point at distance exactly 1, so τ = 1. Caveat: this is the wheel W_d in isolation; in a global colouring a hub may carry |w| up to d/3, and only w mod 2 is visible to Γ.
